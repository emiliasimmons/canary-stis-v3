Source: [lasry-2019](https://doi.org/10.15585/mmwr.mm6821a2)

MMWR report summarising routine programme data from CDC-supported PEPFAR implementing
partners in 20 countries over six fiscal quarters, 1 October 2016 to 31 March 2018. Index
testing here means an HIV-positive index patient voluntarily names sexual and needle-
sharing partners and biological children, who are then contacted by the patient or the
provider and offered testing.

## Relevance to project

Programmatic yield figures for a contact-tracing arm, usable as a comparator against which
an etiological STI panel's case-finding is judged, and as a source of coverage and
positivity bounds if a partner-notification pathway is ever added to the model.

The headline yield: 1,700,998 people tested, 99,201 (5.8%) positive; 9.8% positivity among
those aged 15 and over (87,266/889,599) and 1.5% among those under 15 (11,814/799,976).
Against all PEPFAR testing approaches combined over the same period, positivity was 4.1%
in those 15 and over and 1.0% in those under 15, so index testing roughly doubles yield per
test in adults while accounting for 2.4% of all tests.

Yield rises steeply with age and is close to equal between sexes: 3.2% at 15-19, 6.7% at
20-24, 13.2% at 25-49, 9.7% at 50 and over; 7.5% overall in females against 6.8% in males.
Within 25-49 the sexes are indistinguishable (13.5% women, 13.0% men). Men were 45% of
adults tested, which the authors read as index testing reaching men that other modalities
miss.

Uganda specifically: 26,990 tests in those 15 and over with 1,140 positive (4.2%), 16,277
in those under 15 with 160 positive (1.0%), 43,267 total with 1,300 positive (3.0%). That
sits well below the 20-country adult average, consistent with the authors' explanation
that yield is lower where HIV service coverage is already high.

The report gives no acceptance rate, no number of contacts elicited per index patient, and
no linkage-to-treatment outcome, so it cannot parameterise a notification cascade. It gives
the endpoint yield only.

## Key points

Volume and yield both rose over the six quarters: tests from 166,108 to 356,573 per
quarter, positives from 8,186 to 27,893, overall positivity from 4.9% to 7.8%, adult
positivity from 7.6% to 12.5%, paediatric from 1.2% to 2.0%.

Between-country spread is very wide: adult positivity from 2.8% (Rwanda) to 29.1% (South
Sudan), paediatric from 0.7% (Kenya) to 24.5% (South Sudan). Kenya, Mozambique and Tanzania
together supplied more than half of all tests and positives.

The authors attribute the country spread to differences in existing HIV service coverage.
Where coverage is low, a named contact is more likely to be an undiagnosed positive.

Children tested through index testing yield much lower positivity because their infections
are largely perinatal, a different exposure pathway from the sexual and needle-sharing
contacts that drive the adult numbers.

## Limitations

Aggregate routine programme data with no unique identifiers, so repeat testers cannot be
removed. Both people already aware of their positive status who retested and negative
people who tested several times are counted more than once, and the direction of the net
bias on positivity is not determined.

Index testing was introduced as a PEPFAR reporting category only in October 2016, so part
of the rising trend is implementation and reporting maturing rather than yield improving.
Implementing partners may have misclassified non-exposed household contacts such as
neighbours as index contacts. Eswatini changed its index testing definition during the
period.

Eleven countries were dropped for low volume and two (Nigeria, Ukraine) at country office
request, so the 20-country total is not a complete PEPFAR picture.

The 25-49 age group is a single bin and carries the highest positivity, which hides
whatever gradient exists inside it.

No denominator of index patients: the report counts contacts tested, not index patients
approached, so nothing here supports an estimate of contacts elicited per diagnosis.
