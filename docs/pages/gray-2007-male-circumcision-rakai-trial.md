Source: [gray-2007](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(07)60313-4)

The Rakai arm of the three adult male circumcision trials. 4996 HIV-negative, uncircumcised men aged 15-49 in rural Rakai district, Uganda, randomised to immediate circumcision (n=2474) or circumcision delayed 24 months (n=2522), with HIV testing, examination and behavioural interview at 6, 12 and 24 months. NIAID stopped the trial early for efficacy on 12 December 2006, at roughly 73% of anticipated person-time.

## Relevance to project

Three uses here, all for the male side of the Rakai calibration site around 2004-2006.

A calibration target for background male HIV incidence: 1.33 cases per 100 person-years in uncircumcised control men aged 15-49 over 3391.8 person-years, which the authors say matches incidence in uncircumcised men in the wider Rakai population at the time. Age-stratified control incidence, all per 100 person-years, is 0.63 at 15-19, 1.93 at 20-24, 1.91 at 25-29, and 1.04 at 30-49, which gives the shape of the male incidence-by-age curve the model has to reproduce. Because these men were HIV-negative and uncircumcised at enrolment and only 45% of eligible uncircumcised Rakai men volunteered, treat this as incidence in a screened, uncircumcised, consenting subgroup rather than in all men.

A bound on the STI cofactor effect the project is trying to decompose. In the control arm, HIV incidence among men reporting genital ulcer disease during a follow-up interval was 6.32 per 100 person-years against 1.06 in men reporting none, urethral discharge 8.45 against 1.20, and dysuria 4.57 against 1.25. These are crude person-time contrasts by self-reported symptom during the seroconversion interval, unadjusted, on small numerators (12, 6 and 5 control events respectively), so they carry both the biological cofactor and residual confounding by sexual activity. They set an upper envelope on the per-act cofactor multiplier, not an estimate of it.

Baseline prevalence of self-reported symptoms in the preceding year, useful as a prior on the symptomatic care-seeking door the POC arm attaches to: genital ulcer disease 7%, urethral discharge 3-4%, dysuria 6% of enrolled men. Over follow-up, per-visit symptom prevalence in control men was 5.8% genital ulcer disease, 2.2% genital discharge, 3.3% dysuria across 5564 visits.

Circumcision itself is outside the model's two arms. The trial matters as context for what else was reducing male HIV incidence in Rakai over the calibration window, and as a comparator for the scale of effect a prevention intervention can produce in this population.

## Key points

Modified intention-to-treat efficacy was 51% (95% CI 16-72; IRR 0.49, 0.28-0.84; p=0.006), as-treated 55%, and the Kaplan-Meier as-treated estimate 60% (30-77). Adjusting for enrolment age, marital status and sexual risk behaviour left the IRR at 0.49. Cumulative probability of HIV detection over 24 months was 1.1% in the intervention arm and 2.6% in the control arm, a risk difference of 1.49% and about 67 circumcisions per infection averted over two postoperative years.

Efficacy grew over follow-up: IRR 0.76 at 0-6 months, 0.35 at 6-12, 0.25 at 12-24 (p for trend 0.014 on intervention-arm incidence; p=0.054 on the time-by-arm interaction). Kaplan-Meier curves did not separate until month 12.

Circumcision cut self-reported genital ulcer disease by about half (3.1% vs 5.8% of visits, PRR 0.53, 95% CI 0.43-0.64) but did nothing to urethral discharge (PRR 0.84, p=0.21) or dysuria (PRR 0.97, p=0.78). The authors read this as protection against infections held in the moist subpreputial space and none against urethral infection, consistent with observational findings of no circumcision effect on gonorrhoea or chlamydia prevalence. For a model with four curable STIs, this is a pathogen-specific rather than blanket effect.

No behavioural disinhibition: partner numbers, non-marital partners and condom use were much the same in the two arms at 12 and 24 months, and control men reported slightly *more* alcohol with sex. Surgical adverse events ran about 8% of 2328 surgeries overall, 3% moderate, and 0.2 severe events per 100 surgeries, all resolved.

## Limitations

Stopping early for efficacy tends to overstate effect size, and the authors flag it. Person-time is weighted toward the first 12 months, when efficacy looked lowest, so the pooled 24-month Poisson estimate is conservative relative to the later intervals.

STI status is self-reported symptom, never a laboratory diagnosis. Urine and swabs were collected for later testing but no etiological results appear here, so nothing in this paper separates gonorrhoea, chlamydia, trichomoniasis or syphilis, and the symptom strata cannot be mapped onto pathogens.

Only 44% of men had reached the 24-month visit at closure, so the 12-24 month interval rests on roughly 1000 person-years per arm and 3 versus 12 events. Randomisation used replacement of envelopes within blocks of 20, allowing runs of up to 20 same-arm assignments. Six incident infections occurred during intervals of reported abstinence, which the authors take as under-reporting of sexual activity, a reminder of how much the behavioural covariates here are self-report.
