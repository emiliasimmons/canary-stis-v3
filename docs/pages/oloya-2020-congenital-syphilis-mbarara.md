Cross-sectional study of consecutively enrolled mother-newborn dyads on the maternity ward of Mbarara Regional Referral Hospital, southwestern Uganda, June to September 2015. Mothers were screened by fingerprick TPHA, and TPHA-positives confirmed by venous RPR; newborns of dual-positive mothers were then tested by RPR, with a titre four times the mother's taken as congenital syphilis. Stillbirths and perinatal deaths were excluded. Extracted content in [raw/10.1186/s12884-020-03047-y/](../raw/10.1186/s12884-020-03047-y/).

## Key points

- Of 2500 mothers, 117 (4.5%) were TPHA positive and 103 of those (88%) were also RPR positive, giving active maternal syphilis of 4.1% (95% CI 3.4-5.0) at delivery in a hospital population of southwestern Uganda in 2015.
- Among the 103 newborns of dual-positive mothers, 94 were RPR positive. The paper reports this as 3.8% (95% CI 3.1-4.6) congenital syphilis across all 2502 live newborns.
- Treatment gap in antenatal care. 1,159 mothers (54.6% of those enrolled) reported having been tested for syphilis in the current pregnancy. Of the 29 who were told they were RPR positive, 15 (51.7%) reported receiving treatment, and 12 of 29 male partners (40%) were reported treated. Against a measured 4.1% active prevalence, ANC screening found roughly a quarter of what was there.
- Syndromic care-seeking was near-universal and not protective in itself: 2,268 mothers (90.7%) reported treatment for genital ulcer, vaginal discharge or lower abdominal pain in the current pregnancy, and 1,861 (74.5%) in the past. Absence of any such treatment in the current pregnancy carried the strongest association with congenital syphilis, adjusted OR 3.91 (95% CI 2.44-6.25).
- Other adjusted associations: maternal age 24 or under, aOR 1.85 (1.17-2.92); previous history of genital ulcer, aOR 1.88 (1.19-2.95); previous history of abnormal vaginal discharge, aOR 2.72 (1.74-4.26). Parity, occupation and number of partners in the last year showed nothing, with 88.9% of mothers reporting a single partner.
- Clinical signs in the 103 exposed newborns were non-specific: splenomegaly 58.3%, hepatomegaly 52.4%, syphilitic rash and desquamation 16.5% each, condylomata lata 1.0%, petechiae and rhinitis 1.9% each. RPR-positive newborns had a lower mean Apgar (6.7, SD 1.2) than RPR-negative ones (7.9, SD 0.8), and were near-identical on birthweight (3.3 against 3.5 kg) and gestational age (38.8 against 39.6 weeks).

## Relevance to project

A Ugandan syphilis prevalence anchor from a delivery population, 4.1% active infection by TPHA plus RPR in 2015, roughly 300 km west of Rakai. That is a prior for the syphilis component of the model's STI burden in adult women, with the qualifier that a hospital delivery population is not a general adult population.

Better still, it quantifies the failure mode the POC arm is meant to fix, in a Ugandan setting and with numbers. Nine in ten mothers were treated for a genital syndrome during pregnancy, and 4.1% still had active syphilis at delivery. Syndromic treatment did not clear these infections, and the women who avoided it entirely were at nearly four times the risk. Half of the women whom antenatal screening did identify went untreated, and only 40% of their partners were treated. Those are usable numbers for the treatment-completion and partner-treatment leakage between a positive test and a cured infection, which the model should not assume away.

There is no HIV in this study and no incidence, so it bears on the STI burden and the care cascade, not on the cofactor or targeting pathways.

## Limitations

- The reported 3.8% "congenital syphilis" is 94 RPR-positive newborns over all 2,502 live births, not the four-fold-titre definition stated in the methods. Maternal antibody crosses the placenta, so a newborn RPR positive without a titre comparison is exposure, not infection. Treat 3.8% as the proportion of newborns with detectable reagin, and the true congenital infection rate as unmeasured and lower.
- Stillbirths and perinatal deaths were excluded, which removes the most severe syphilis outcomes and biases maternal prevalence downward.
- No dark-field microscopy or immunofluorescence confirmation.
- The reported maternal median age of 20 (IQR 15-36) is inconsistent with the age table, in which 68.7% of RPR/TPHA-negative mothers are 25-35. One of the two is wrong; the age association rests on the table.
- Screening history, treatment receipt and partner treatment were self-reported, with ANC cards reviewed only when available.
- A single regional referral hospital, so the population is skewed toward complicated deliveries and toward women who reach a facility at all. Uganda ANC attendance is cited in the paper as 47%.
