Source: [marrazzo-2015](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1402269)

The VOICE trial (MTN-003), a five-arm randomised placebo-controlled trial of daily oral tenofovir disoproxil fumarate (TDF), oral TDF-emtricitabine (TDF-FTC), and 1% tenofovir vaginal gel as HIV pre-exposure prophylaxis in 5029 women aged 18-45 at 15 sites in South Africa, Uganda and Zimbabwe, enrolled September 2009 to June 2011 and followed to August 2012.

## Key points

- No arm reduced HIV-1 acquisition in the modified intention-to-treat analysis. Effectiveness was -49.0% for oral TDF (HR 1.49, 95% CI 0.97-2.29), -4.4% for TDF-FTC (HR 1.04, 95% CI 0.73-1.49), and 14.5% for tenofovir gel (HR 0.85, 95% CI 0.61-1.21). The TDF and gel arms were stopped for futility in late 2011.
- Background HIV incidence was 5.7 per 100 person-years over 5509 person-years of follow-up, ranging 0.8-9.9 across sites. Incidence was 8.0 per 100 py in women under 25 and 7.2 per 100 py in unmarried women.
- Measured adherence and reported adherence diverged sharply. Returned-product counts gave a mean of 86%, face-to-face interview 90%, and computer-assisted self-interview 88%. Plasma tenofovir was detected in a mean of 30% (TDF), 29% (TDF-FTC) and 25% (gel) of quarterly samples, and 58%, 50% and 57% of women respectively had no drug detected at any visit.
- The women who did take the drug were also the women at lower risk. Detection was associated with being over 25 (adjusted OR 1.62, 95% CI 1.12-2.34), married (2.24, 1.12-4.49) and multiparous (1.84, 1.26-2.69), and those same characteristics predicted lower HIV acquisition within both placebo arms.
- Within the gel arm, women with detectable plasma tenofovir at the first quarterly visit had HIV incidence of 1.9 vs 6.1 per 100 py (adjusted HR 0.34, 95% CI 0.13-0.87, P = 0.02). The authors decline to read this as efficacy, because HIV exposure was not measured and cannot be adjusted for.
- Baseline infection prevalence among the 5029 enrolled HIV-negative women: chlamydia 12%, gonorrhoea 3%, trichomonas 6%, syphilis 1%, HSV-2 46% (of 5005 tested), bacterial vaginosis 40% (of 5010 tested).
- Acquired resistance was rare: one M184V in an active-arm seroconverter, and no K65R in anyone infected after randomisation. The authors attribute this to consistently low rather than intermittent drug exposure.

## Relevance to project

Bounds the prevention-targeting leg of the pathway triad. If a positive point-of-care STI result is used to direct PrEP, the HIV incidence averted is the product of drug efficacy and delivered adherence, and VOICE is the case where the second term collapses: a regimen with 50%+ efficacy under high adherence returned nothing at a population level. A prevention-targeting arm should not inherit trial-arm efficacy from a well-adhering cohort.

The adherence-risk correlation is the sharper constraint. Uptake concentrated in older, married, multiparous women, who were at lower HIV risk to begin with, so targeting that raises coverage need not raise averted infections. Any model in which POC-directed PrEP reaches a fixed fraction of test-positives at fixed efficacy will overstate impact unless uptake is allowed to correlate with risk.

The baseline STI prevalences are a comparator for the four modelled pathogens in a young, high-risk, multi-country sub-Saharan cohort, not a calibration target: the frame is trial screening across South Africa, Uganda and Zimbabwe, with 62% of participants from a single Durban site, not the Rakai population. Compare against the population-based Rakai figures in [Moffa et al.](moffa-2026-sti-couples-stips.md) rather than substituting for them.

VOICE says nothing about the direct-treatment or network-identification legs.

## Limitations

- Effectiveness cannot be separated from adherence. The trial estimates what the products delivered in this population, not what tenofovir does.
- HIV exposure was unmeasured, so the one protective signal (gel arm, drug detected) is confounded by the same characteristics that drive both adherence and risk.
- Product exposure was measured quarterly in a case-cohort subsample of 647 participants, not in everyone, with a median of 3 samples per participant.
- Enrolment excluded pregnant and breastfeeding women and required effective contraception, and 71% used injectable hormonal contraception, which is not representative of the general population.
