A PLACE (Priorities for Local AIDS Control Efforts) study run in Hwange District, Zimbabwe
in November-December 2006. PLACE is a venue-sampling method: community informants name the
public places where people meet new sexual partners, the venues are verified and mapped, and
then people socialising there are interviewed. Here 429 informants named 334 sites, 312 were
found and characterised, and 529 men and 511 women were interviewed at 132 sampled venues
over 12 days. The paper reports the women only, stratified by age (15-19, 20-24, 25+) and by
venue class: Nightlife/Drinking (bars, taverns, nightclubs, lodging), Open/Transport-Related
(schools, boreholes, shops, churches, transport hubs), and Events/Private or Hidden (weddings,
concerts, sports events, bush and abandoned yards). Extracted text sits in
`raw/10.1007/s10461-009-9572-8`.

## Relevance to project

Context and a weak structural argument, not a calibration target. The setting is Zimbabwe in
2006 and the sampling frame is venue-based, so nothing here is a population rate for
Rakai; the denominators are people present at public venues, not a cohort.

Two things it supports. First, the size of the risk gradient between a small
partner-acquisition core and everyone else, which is what the model's risk-structured network
has to reproduce: among women 15-19, 47.8% at drinking venues reported 2 or more partners in
the past 12 months against 3.1% at open/transport venues and 4.9% at events/hidden venues, a
roughly 15-fold contrast within one age band and one district. New-partner rates split the
same way (30.4% vs 2.7% vs 4.9% reporting a new partner in the past 4 weeks). Transactional
sex among sexually active women ran 30.8% at drinking venues against 10.4% at open/transport
venues. That the contrast is this large inside a single small district is an argument for
heterogeneity in the network layer rather than in geography.

Second, that the high-risk group is a small share of the population it sits in: only 8.0% of
15-19 year old women were sampled at drinking venues, against 77.7% at open/transport venues.
A prevention channel reaching only the risk core reaches a small fraction of the age band, and
a channel reaching the age band mostly misses the core. That is the same tension the
POC-versus-SOC arms face, since the etiological panel only reaches people who come through the
symptomatic care-seeking door.

Age mixing is the one place the paper offers a near-flat number usable as a soft prior on a
partner age-gap distribution: 51% of sexually active women 15-19, 47% of 20-24 and 48% of 25+
reported an oldest partner in the past 12 months 5 or more years older, with no significant
difference by age or by venue type (48%, 48%, 53% across the three venue classes). Treat it as
an order-of-magnitude check on an age-mixing kernel, not a fit target: it is the *oldest*
partner in a year, self-reported, among venue attendees.

The paper carries no STI data, no biomarkers, and no incidence, so it bears on the network
layer only.

## Key points

- Venue composition by age: 8.0% of women 15-19 were at nightlife/drinking sites, versus 23.7%
  of 20-24 and 24.7% of 25+ (N=511, p<0.01).
- Ever had sex, women 15-19: 60.9% at drinking sites, 17.5% at open/transport, 31.7% at
  events/hidden.
- 1+ partner in past 4 weeks, women 20-24: 78.1% at drinking sites versus 40.0% at
  open/transport.
- Condom use at last sex among sexually active women, all ages: 53.9% at drinking sites, 37.0%
  at open/transport, 60.5% at events/hidden.
- 27% of women 15-19 said they had met a new partner at the venue where they were interviewed,
  falling to 12% among 25+.
- Venue supply side: 51% of the 312 venues had any prevention activity and 39% had condom
  distribution, while 93% of venue representatives were willing to host prevention activity and
  85% willing to sell condoms.
- National background the authors cite from the 2005/06 ZDHS: HIV prevalence 6.2% in females
  15-19 versus 3.1% in males, and 16.3% versus 5.8% at 20-24.

## Limitations

Convenience-sampled informants define the venue list, so meeting places nobody named are
missing entirely. All behavioural measures are self-reported at the venue; the authors note
over-reporting is plausible at bars and under-reporting plausible at ordinary sites like
boreholes and churches, and cite a Zimbabwean study finding little correlation between
questionnaire responses and biological evidence of sexual experience. No HIV testing was done,
so every risk claim is behavioural. Cell sizes forced the age-by-venue stratification to be
dropped for condom use and transactional sex among 15-19 year olds, and the events/hidden class
had too few women 25+ to compare. The three venue classes are coarse: transport-related sites
were folded into the open class only because 19 venues was too few to stand alone. Finally, the
authors concede the method's premise, that delivering interventions at partner-meeting venues
reduces transmission, was at the time undocumented by any trial.
