> Note: summary derived from the abstract only, not the full source.

Retrospective analysis of programmatic aggregate data on HIV index testing (a form of assisted partner notification) across 48 health facilities in Nairobi County, Kenya, October 2017 to September 2019, spanning the first two years of roll-out. It reports cascade outcomes (partner testing uptake, HIV positivity, and the contribution of index testing to total diagnoses) and compares them between the two years, in the general population and among key populations (KPs).

## Relevance to project

Another partner-notification cascade dataset from a routine African programme, alongside the canon's index-testing and APS entries from [South Africa](aheron-2024-index-testing-south-africa.md) and [Mozambique](golden-2023-aps-safety-mozambique.md). The model omits a partner-notification pathway, so this bounds the excluded pathway and shows how its yield behaves during scale-up.

The concentration-of-effort figure is the transferable one: index testing positivity was 24.3% against 1.3% for other testing modalities in the general population (14% against 0.9% among KPs), and index testing's share of all HIV-positive individuals identified rose from 7.5% in year one to 28.6% in year two. A small, targeted testing stream finding a disproportionate share of new diagnoses is the same argument that motivates the POC arm's symptomatic care-seeking door, which is why the care-seeking tag applies.

Not a calibration target: Nairobi, not Rakai, and aggregate programme monitoring data rather than a population cohort.

## Key points

- Testing among eligible partners in the general population rose from 42.4% (1,471/3,470) in year one to 74.9% (6,114/8,159) in year two.
- Positivity among tested partners stayed high across both years: 25.2% then 24.1% in the general population.
- Index testing positivity far exceeded other testing modalities: 24.3% vs 1.3% (general population) and 14% vs 0.9% (KPs), both p < 0.001.
- Index testing's contribution to all HIV-positive individuals identified rose from 7.5% to 28.6% (p < 0.001).
- More men were tested, but positivity was higher among women (30.0%) and those aged 50 or older (32.4%).
- Among KPs, eligible-partner testing fell from 52.4% (183/349) to 40.7% (109/268, p = 0.674), while positivity rose from 8.6% to 23.9% (p < 0.001).

## Limitations

Abstract only: elicitation ratios, linkage-to-treatment figures, and the implementation-strategy detail were not read, so no per-index contact yield or ART-linkage rate is available from this page. Aggregate programmatic data with no comparison arm and denominators that prevent individual-level analysis. Nairobi is urban with dense facility coverage, so cascade performance may not generalise to rural settings.
