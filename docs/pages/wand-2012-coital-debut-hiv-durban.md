Source: [wand-2012](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjopen-2011-000285)

A cohort analysis nested in the MIRA diaphragm trial (Umkomaas and Botha's Hill, southern Durban, South Africa, September 2002 to September 2005). 3492 sexually active women aged 18-49 were screened and cross-sectionally analysed for HIV prevalence; the 1485 who were HIV-negative and enrolled were followed roughly 24 months for seroconversion. The paper reports risk factors for prevalent and incident HIV and the population attributable risk (PAR) for each.

## Relevance to project

This is a comparator and a weak prior for the STI cofactor on HIV acquisition in women, and a caution about how small the curable-STI share of HIV burden may look in a high-HSV2 setting.

The usable numbers, all women aged 18-49 in a trial-screening population with 41% HIV prevalence:

- Curable STI at screening (chlamydia, gonorrhoea, syphilis or *Trichomonas vaginalis*, 16% prevalent) carries an adjusted OR of 1.24 (95% CI 1.02 to 1.51) for prevalent HIV. That is the closest thing here to the model's cofactor multiplier, and it is a cross-sectional association, not a per-act transmission probability.
- The PAR for those four curable STIs is 5% (95% CI 4% to 6%) of prevalent HIV cases and 3% (2% to 5%) of incident cases. This is the ceiling on what pure STI clearance could have bought in this population, and it is the number to hold against the direct-treatment channel of the POC arm.
- HSV2 (73% prevalent) dominates: adjusted OR 6.19 (4.98 to 7.70), PAR 82% for prevalence and 21% for incidence. The model carries no HSV2, so a cofactor calibrated to the four curable STIs alone should not be asked to reproduce the HIV incidence this cohort saw.
- Overall HIV incidence was 6.8 per 100 person-years (5.8 to 8.0), 148 seroconversions. Far above Rakai-era rates, so it is a comparator for a high-incidence female population, not a calibration target for the Rakai site.

For the network side: at least four lifetime partners gives adjusted OR 4.09 (3.20 to 5.22) versus one, and non-cohabitation OR 4.32 (3.11 to 6.00), PAR 39% for prevalence and 54% for incidence. Those support a risk-structured network with a heavy-tailed partner distribution and a distinct non-cohabiting stratum, but the study reports lifetime cumulative counts, not concurrent degree, so they cannot parameterise the network directly.

## Key points

Age at first sex, in quintiles, tracks HIV monotonically. Prevalence runs 48% in the youngest quintile (under 15) down to 33% above 20; incidence runs 12.0 per 100 person-years (8.0 to 18.0) under 15 versus 6.4 (4.2 to 9.8) above 20. The adjusted hazard ratio for the youngest quintile loses significance once age, education, partner count, cohabitation and condom use are controlled (HR 1.78, 95% CI 0.96 to 3.29), which the authors read as debut acting through accumulated lifetime partners rather than directly.

Curable STI prevalence itself grades with debut age: 20% in the youngest quintile down to 12% in the oldest, as does HSV2 (79% to 68%). STI and HIV risk cluster in the same women.

Age at screening has an inverted-U relation with prevalent HIV: 25-29 elevated (OR 1.52), 35+ protective (OR 0.50) against the under-25 reference.

## Limitations

Trial-screening population, so eligibility filters (sexually active, at least four acts per month, HIV-negative and CT/NG-negative or willing to be treated at screening) make it unrepresentative of the general adult female population. Male partner behaviour, concurrency, migration, sex work and socioeconomic status were not measured. Age at debut is self-reported and retrospective in a cohort with median age 26.

The PARs are computed with the odds ratios, hazard ratios and exposure prevalences all taken from the same sample, and the authors state plainly that reading a PAR as preventable fraction requires causality that this design does not establish. Treat the 5% and 3% STI PARs as an order of magnitude for the direct-treatment channel, not as an effect size.

The STI category is pooled across four pathogens with no per-pathogen breakdown, so it gives no ordering among gonorrhoea, chlamydia, syphilis and trichomoniasis.
