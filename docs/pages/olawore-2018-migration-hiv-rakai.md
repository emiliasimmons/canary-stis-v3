Prospective cohort study from the RCCS examining HIV acquisition among 15,187 HIV-negative
participants aged 15-49, followed for 89,292 person-years (1999-2015). Cataloged from
abstract; the full text has not been extracted.

## Relevance to project

Supplies sex-specific HIV incidence rates for the Rakai calibration site, stratified by
migration status. The permanent-resident rates (women 0.93/100 pys, men 0.84/100 pys)
serve as calibration targets for baseline HIV incidence. The elevated rates among recent
in-migrants (women 1.92/100 pys, men 1.52/100 pys, adjIRR 1.74 for both) quantify the
contribution of migration to incidence, relevant if the model accounts for population
turnover. The finding that HIV incidence declined over time among permanent residents but
remained unchanged among recent in-migrants bounds the extent to which community-level
prevention reaches mobile populations.

## Key points

- 29% of participants (n=4,451) were ever in-migrants over the study period.
- 841 incident HIV infections; 29% (n=243) occurred among in-migrants.
- Recent in-migrants had HIV incidence roughly double that of permanent residents
  (adjIRR 1.74, 95% CI 1.12-2.71 for women; adjIRR 1.74 for men).
- HIV incidence declined over 1999-2015 among permanent residents and non-recent
  in-migrants but not among recent in-migrants.

## Limitations

Rates are period averages over 1999-2015 and may not reflect the current epidemic state
at the model's calibration window. Migration status classification and the lag between
surveys could misclassify recent arrivals.

## Methodology

Open population-based cohort (RCCS), prospective follow-up with serological testing at
each survey round. In-migrant status defined by entry into surveyed communities.
