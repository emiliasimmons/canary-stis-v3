Retrospective cohort recruited at delivery in three hospitals in Mwanza Region, northwestern Tanzania, June 1998 to April 2000, among women whose antenatal cards showed no syphilis screening during pregnancy. Every RPR-positive woman and the next two RPR-negative women were enrolled, then retested at a reference laboratory with quantitative RPR plus TPHA (FTA-ABS where discordant), giving 73 women with high-titer active syphilis (RPR titer at or above 1:8 with a positive treponemal test), 27 low-titer active, 9 past or treated, 38 biological false positive, and 233 seronegative. Extracted content in [raw/10.1086/342952/](../raw/10.1086/342952/).

## Key points

- Serological stage, not seropositivity, carried the risk. Any adverse outcome (stillbirth, prematurity, or IUGR) occurred in 36/73 (49.3%) of women with high-titer active syphilis against 11.2% of 233 seronegative women, adjusted RR 4.4 (95% CI 2.8-6.9). Low-titer active syphilis gave 3.7% (1/27) and past or treated syphilis 11.1% (1/9), neither differing from seronegative.
- Stillbirth (dead fetus after 22 weeks) was 18/73 (24.7%) with high-titer active syphilis against 3/233 (1.3%) seronegative, adjusted RR 18.1 (95% CI 5.5-59.6). Seventeen of the 18 were in women with RPR titer at or above 1:32.
- Among live births, low birth weight (under 2500 g) was 18/55 (32.7%) against 23/230 (10.0%), adjusted RR 3.3 (2.0-5.4); preterm delivery (under 37 weeks by Dubovitz scoring) 11/55 (20.0%) against 7/230 (3.0%), adjusted RR 6.1 (2.5-15.3); IUGR 12.7% against 7.0%, adjusted RR 2.1 (1.0-4.2), the one outcome that was not clearly raised.
- Population attributable fractions in the unscreened population, at an estimated 5.9% prevalence of high-titer active syphilis: 51.4% (19.4-83.1) of stillbirths, 24.1% (5.5-43.2) of preterm live births, 11.6% (3.3-19.9) of low birth weight, 16.7% (8.3-25.1) of any adverse outcome.
- Within women who had high-titer active syphilis, 94% of their stillbirths and 77% of any adverse outcome were attributable to the infection.
- Background infection prevalences in the cohort: 6% chlamydia and 2% gonorrhoea by urine PCR, 4% HIV, 22% peripheral malaria parasitaemia, 68% placental malaria. Chlamydia was more common with high-titer syphilis (11% against 4%, p = 0.03); gonorrhoea, HIV, malaria and anaemia were not associated. Adjusting for these moved the crude risk ratios by less than 10%.
- Clinical signs of congenital syphilis at delivery were near-absent: one infant of a high-titer mother with hepatosplenomegaly out of 358 live births, so examination at birth misses the burden serology implies.

## Relevance to project

A bound on the pregnancy-outcome benefit of detecting and treating syphilis at a point where the woman would otherwise go untested, with the comparator being an unscreened population rather than a screened-but-untreated one. The attributable fractions are the operative numbers if the POC arm is credited with non-HIV benefit: half of stillbirths and a quarter of preterm births in an unscreened population with roughly 6% high-titer active syphilis.

The titer stratification bears on the diagnostic side directly. Risk concentrated entirely in RPR titer at or above 1:8 with treponemal confirmation, and most of it above 1:32. A treponemal-only rapid test cannot separate these women from the 9 with past or treated infection and would attribute benefit to treating a group whose outcomes matched seronegative women here. Any test-performance assumption in the syphilis arm should be against high-titer active infection as the target condition.

Nothing here bears on HIV. HIV prevalence in the cohort was 4%, and the study was neither designed nor powered to see STI-HIV interaction, so it supplies nothing for the cofactor pathway, prevention targeting, or network identification.

## Limitations

- Recruited at delivery in hospitals, so second-trimester losses and home births are absent. The authors expect the impact to be underestimated on the first count and are agnostic on the direction of the second.
- Small numbers behind the stratified estimates: 73 women with high-titer active syphilis, 27 low-titer, 9 past or treated. The stillbirth risk ratio has a confidence interval spanning 5.5 to 59.6.
- Gestational age for live births came from Dubovitz examination, a proxy that misclassifies preterm in both directions, and from recalled last menstrual period for stillbirths.
- Attributable fractions assume the syphilis-outcome association is causal and confounding is fully controlled. Confounders tested here moved nothing, but drug use in pregnancy was self-reported and 22% of women thought they had taken some antibiotic.
- Mwanza in 1998-2000, with 68% placental malaria and 4% HIV. Transport to a lower-malaria or higher-HIV setting is untested.
