Source: [kagaayi-2019](https://doi.org/10.1016/s2352-3018(19)30190-0)

Prospective open population-based cohort of persons aged 15-49 across five surveys
(2011-2017) in four fishing communities on Lake Victoria, Uganda. These communities sit
within the Rakai/south-central Uganda surveillance area and represent the hyperendemic
tail of the HIV distribution. Cataloged from abstract; the full text has not been
extracted.

## Relevance to project

Quantifies the HIV incidence reduction achievable through combination prevention (ART,
circumcision, testing) without behavioural change, in a population adjacent to the RCCS
calibration site. The coverage trajectories (ART 16% to 82%, circumcision 35% to 65%,
viral suppression 34% to 80%) and the resulting incidence decline (adjIRR 0.52) provide
a comparator for the model's SOC arm: the background prevention gains the model must
reproduce before layering POC STI diagnostics. The circumcision protective effect (adjIRR
0.46) is a cross-check on the model's circumcision parameter. The residual incidence of
1.59/100 PY despite high coverage highlights the gap that additional interventions (like
POC STI treatment) must address.

## Key points

- Baseline HIV prevalence in the fishing communities was 41%.
- HIV testing coverage rose from 68% to 95% over the study period.
- Male circumcision prevalence rose from 35% to 65%.
- ART coverage rose from 16% to 82%; population-level viral load suppression from 34% to
  80%.
- No observed decrease in risky sexual behaviours over the period.
- HIV incidence declined from 3.43/100 PY to 1.59/100 PY (adjusted IRR 0.52, 95% CI
  0.34-0.79).
- Circumcised men had lower HIV acquisition (adjusted IRR 0.46, 95% CI 0.32-0.67).
- Incidence remained above epidemic-control targets despite high intervention coverage.

## Limitations

Fishing communities are hyperendemic (41% prevalence) and not representative of the
broader RCCS population. The ecological design cannot attribute the incidence decline to
specific interventions. Behavioural data are self-reported.

## Methodology

Five rounds of an open population-based cohort survey (2011-2017) in four Lake Victoria
fishing communities. HIV incidence estimated by seroconversion between survey rounds.
Adjusted Poisson regression for incidence rate ratios.
