Source: [nardell-2022](https://doi.org/10.1002/jia2.25889)

A systematic review pairing a meta-analysis of men's attainment of the UNAIDS 95-95-95
goals in sub-Saharan Africa with a meta-synthesis of qualitative work on why men stay away
from HIV care. PubMed and Embase, articles published 2014 through 16 October 2020, data
collected from 2009 onward; 129 studies in the meta-analysis covering 1,564,019
participants in 21 countries, and 40 qualitative studies covering 2683 participants in 10
countries. Uganda contributed 18 of the 129 quantitative studies.

## Relevance to project

Two uses here, both about the door a person walks through rather than about STIs.

A cascade prior and a bound on the male side. Among men with HIV, the pooled proportion
knowing their status is 0.49 (95% CI 0.41-0.58, range across studies 0.09-0.97); on ART
among those aware, 0.58 (0.51-0.65); virally suppressed among those on ART, 0.79
(0.77-0.81). Within the studies that enrolled both sexes, men trail women at every step:
0.53 versus 0.66 aware (p = 0.04), 0.54 versus 0.62 on ART (p = 0.09), 0.79 versus 0.82
suppressed (p = 0.01). These are aggregates over 2009-2020, not a 2020 snapshot, so they
set a floor and a plausible sex ratio for cascade parameters rather than a calibration
target. The Rakai-specific cascade should come from RCCS data; this bounds how far a
male-female asymmetry can reasonably be pushed and confirms the asymmetry sits mostly in
the first two steps, not in suppression.

Context for the symptomatic care-seeking door that both arms share. The SOC and POC arms
differ only in what happens after a symptomatic person presents, so the fraction who
present at all is a shared input, and the meta-synthesis says that fraction is lower for
men for reasons that do not change when the test on the other side of the door changes:
clinics read as feminine spaces, anticipated provider stigma, lost work time, transport and
consultation costs. Nothing here quantifies STI care seeking, so the number itself must come
from elsewhere; what this supplies is the structural argument for a sex-differentiated
care-seeking probability and for treating a POC panel as leaving male presentation
unchanged.

Not usable as an HIV incidence or prevalence source, and not usable for anything about STIs.

## Key points

- Heterogeneity is extreme: I-squared is 99.68%, 99.59% and 98.64% for the three goals.
  Study-level ranges span nearly the full 0-1 interval. Any single pooled figure taken
  from this paper carries that spread with it.
- Ascertainment moves the estimate as much as the population does. Men on ART: 0.43
  (0.34-0.51) in healthcare-facility samples versus 0.67 (0.58-0.75) in community samples,
  p < 0.001. Knowledge of status: 0.46 by self-report before study testing versus 0.51 by
  other methods, p < 0.001.
- Studies restricted to men who have sex with men give lower attainment: 0.36 (0.23-0.49)
  aware versus 0.53 in mixed samples (p = 0.04), and 0.71 (0.66-0.77) suppressed versus
  0.79 (p < 0.001).
- Viral suppression was lower in studies whose median data year was 2017-2020 (0.74,
  0.68-0.79) than in 2009-2016 studies (0.81, 0.79-0.83), p = 0.02, which runs against the
  expected direction of programme improvement and the authors do not resolve it.
- Regional gap in ART use: 0.60 (0.52-0.68) in eastern and southern Africa versus 0.47
  (0.38-0.56) in western and central Africa, p = 0.02, from only 13 western and central
  African studies.
- The qualitative synthesis lands on three domains of perceived risk: social norms
  (masculinity, testing framed as a woman's job, HIV as the end of sexual and social
  standing), health system challenges (provider stigma, no privacy, stockouts), and
  poverty (transport and consultation costs, time competing with paid work).
- Self-testing and home testing recur as the mitigation men name themselves, on both
  privacy and cost grounds.

## Limitations

- Pooled across a decade of research studies rather than programmatic surveillance, so the
  estimates are not comparable to an annual UNAIDS figure and mask within-period change.
  The authors argue this is partly a feature, since no modelled estimates enter the pool.
- Viral suppression thresholds ranged from 20 to 5000 copies/ml (44 of 74 studies used
  1000), and minimum time on ART before measurement ranged 2 to 24 months. The third goal's
  pooled value is therefore measured on an inconsistent scale.
- ART status was self-reported in 23 of 43 studies; only 7 used a blood test.
- 61 of 129 studies met quality criteria only partially, though quality stratification
  produced no significant differences.
- South Africa supplies 39 of 129 studies and eastern and southern Africa 113, so the pool
  is not evenly weighted across the region it claims to describe.
- Cisgender status was rarely specified; one study explicitly included transgender men and
  one explicitly excluded them.
