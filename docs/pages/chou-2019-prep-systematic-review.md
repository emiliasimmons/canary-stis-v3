Systematic review and meta-analysis for the US Preventive Services Task Force, pooling 14
RCTs with 18,837 participants. Cataloged from abstract; the full text has not been
extracted.

## Relevance to project

Primary parameter source for PrEP efficacy in the model. The pooled relative risk of 0.46
(95% CI 0.33-0.66) gives the intention-to-treat efficacy, and the adherence-stratified
estimate (RR 0.27, 95% CI 0.19-0.39 at ≥70% adherence) gives the per-protocol efficacy
that the model can use when simulating PrEP with an explicit adherence mechanism. The gap
between these two estimates (54% vs 73% reduction) quantifies how much real-world
effectiveness depends on adherence, informing whether the model needs an adherence layer
or can use a single effective efficacy.

## Key points

- 14 RCTs, N=18,837. PrEP reduced HIV acquisition vs placebo: RR 0.46
  (95% CI 0.33-0.66), absolute risk difference -2.0 percentage points.
- Adherence ≥70% yielded RR 0.27 (95% CI 0.19-0.39).
- Renal adverse events increased (RR 1.43), gastrointestinal events increased (RR 1.63),
  mostly mild and reversible.
- Risk prediction instruments for identifying PrEP candidates had moderate discrimination
  (AUC 0.49-0.72).

## Limitations

Pooled across oral PrEP formulations and populations (MSM, heterosexual, PWID); the
heterosexual-specific estimate may differ. The review predates long-acting injectable PrEP
trials (cabotegravir, lenacapavir). Adverse event rates reflect trial populations with
close monitoring.

## Methodology

Systematic review with meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials, conducted for the
USPSTF. Risk of bias assessed per Cochrane methods.
