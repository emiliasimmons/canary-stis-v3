Secondary analysis of the Sexually Transmitted Infection Prevalence Study (STIPS), a cross-sectional population-based study in a fishing community and an inland community in southern Uganda (May-October 2019), nested within the RCCS. Tested for chlamydia, gonorrhea, trichomonas, syphilis, high-titre syphilis (RPR >= 1:8), and HSV-2. Extracted content in [raw/10.1136_sextrans-2025-056709/](../raw/10.1136_sextrans-2025-056709/).

## Key points

- Among 423 cohabiting heterosexual couples, at least one partner had a curable STI in 37% of couples. Curable STI prevalence in couples was comparable to the broader STIPS population (25.4% vs 26.7%).
- Same-pathogen concordance was strong: gonorrhea prevalence among male partners of infected females was 38% vs 2% among partners of uninfected females (PR 17.8, 95% CI 8.1-39.4). Smallest effect was HSV-2 (PR 2.3, 95% CI 1.9-2.8).
- Cross-pathogen clustering was pervasive. Female partners of males with gonorrhea had elevated prevalence of HIV (PR 2.2), chlamydia (PR 2.5), trichomonas (PR 2.4), and HSV-2 (PR 1.3), not just gonorrhea (PR 10.4).
- Chlamydia was an exception: partner infection with a different pathogen did not generally predict chlamydia, possibly because chlamydia confers partial protective immunity against reinfection.
- Curable STIs were more often serodiscordant (female positive, male negative) than concordant.
- Among 126 females with a curable STI, 21% had multiple curable STIs, and 45% of their male partners also had at least one.

## Relevance to project

Provides population-level couple-concordance data for the four curable STIs the model tracks, from the same Rakai/southern Uganda population. The cross-pathogen clustering rates (prevalence ratios by partner infection status) can inform how the model handles correlated STI risk within partnerships. The finding that syndromic management fails to reach asymptomatic infections (half of symptomatic adults did not seek care, and a third of those who did still tested positive) is directly relevant to the SOC arm's assumptions about treatment coverage.

The chlamydia exception (partial immunity reducing reinfection and co-occurrence) supports modeling chlamydia with a distinct natural history from the other STIs.

## Limitations

- Cross-sectional design: cannot distinguish primary infection from reinfection.
- Two communities in southern Uganda; generalizability limited.
- Lower participation among men and younger individuals.
- Polygamous relationships treated as multiple dyads.
