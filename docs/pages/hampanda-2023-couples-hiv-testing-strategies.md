Source: [hampanda-2023](https://doi.org/10.1002/jia2.26075)

Systematic review (PROSPERO CRD42018086844) of strategies used to promote couples HIV testing and counselling (CHTC) in sub-Saharan Africa. Five databases, publications 1980 to September 2019, heterosexual couples, at least one quantifiable measure of CHTC uptake. 6188 unique records screened, 365 full texts, 30 articles from 29 unique studies retained. Nine countries: Kenya (9), Tanzania (5), Uganda (4), South Africa (3), DRC (2), Malawi (2), Zambia (1), Zimbabwe (1), plus two studies spanning Rwanda and Zambia. No meta-analysis: strategies, outcome definitions, and denominators were too heterogeneous.

## Relevance to project

This is context, and a bound on what the model does not represent, rather than a parameter source. canary-stis implements no partner-notification pathway: the POC arm swaps an etiological panel in at the same symptomatic care-seeking door as syndromic management, and index or couple-based recruitment is out of scope. The review is the reference for what that omission costs, since it fixes the range that a couple-based recruitment arm would have to draw its uptake from.

If a partner-oriented arm is ever added, the usable quantities are the paired intervention-versus-control uptakes, since they hold the denominator fixed within a study. Home-based CHTC against clinic standard of care or written invitation: 21% vs 10%, 77% vs 24%, 85% vs 36%. Secondary distribution of HIV self-test kits against an invitation card, women in antenatal or postpartum care in Kenya: 75.4% vs 33.2%. A three-arm antenatal trial in Kenya: 79% self-test vs 27% standard information card vs 35% enhanced card. Assisted partner tracing plus written invitation against invitation alone, male partners of pregnant women recently diagnosed with HIV in Malawi: 75% vs 52% at one month. A grocery incentive worth about US$1.50 in Zimbabwe against mobile-unit standard of care: 56% vs 10%. Counsellor-supported disclosure at a Kenyan clinic: 28% vs 7%.

A single background rate for "couples get tested together" is better taken from the pooled estimate the review cites from Hailemariam et al., 31.5% (95% CI 23.6-40.0%) across 14 studies, 2000-2017. Note the population: most studies required married or cohabiting primary partners, and nearly half recruited in antenatal settings, so the estimate is not for the whole sexually active adult population the model simulates.

Nothing here bears on STI diagnostics, on assay performance, or on HIV incidence. The outcome throughout is uptake of a testing event, never an infection outcome.

## Key points

The 29 studies sorted into 10 primary strategies: home-based CHTC (7), integrating CHTC into clinical settings (4), verbal or written invitations (4), distribution of HIV self-test kits (3 to 4 depending on the count used), community agents or recruiters (3), index partner tracing (2), intensive relationship counselling (2), financial incentives (1), group education with CHTC coupons (1), and testing at other community venues (1).

Home-based delivery was both the most common strategy and among the most effective, with uptake from 17% (CHTC offered inside an epidemiological survey) to 87% (home-based CHTC bundled with family planning services, Tanzania). Door-to-door outreach alone landed at 21-52%.

Community influence-network agents in two Zambian cities reached 71% and 75%, far above other community-outreach programmes in Zambia (6%) and Rwanda (18%) that recruited through churches, employers, and clinics.

Written invitation letters, the most common control arm, produced 24-36% in Kenyan studies and 52% in Malawi. Written and verbal invitations were indistinguishable in a Tanzanian head-to-head (31% vs 28%, p = 0.59).

Downstream effects reported by individual studies: linkage to HIV care, increased condom use and fewer outside partners, higher voluntary medical male circumcision and contraceptive uptake, and PMTCT outcomes. No study attributed intimate partner violence to CHTC.

Post-2019 literature the authors scanned adds one result: partner notification plus secondary distribution of self-test kits raised male partner testing of any kind but lowered facility-based testing, against partner notification alone.

## Limitations

Uptake figures are not comparable across studies. The denominator shifts between the number of women handed an invitation and the number who returned with a partner, which alone produced 16% versus 81% for two Tanzanian studies of the same strategy. Definitions of CHTC also vary: simultaneous testing and counselling (18 studies), counselling together after individual testing (3), either (4), and testing together with no counselling (4).

Most studies ran several strategies at once, including within a single arm, so the categorisation names the strategy the authors emphasised and not the active ingredient.

Coverage gaps: no West African study, no study focused on adolescents, and mostly married or cohabiting couples, which excludes exactly the non-cohabiting partnerships that carry much of the transmission risk in a risk-structured network.

Quality appraisal (MMAT) covered only the 18 studies with CHTC as a primary outcome. Among the 8 randomised trials, all had appropriate randomisation, comparable baselines, and complete outcome data, but only 2 blinded outcome assessors and only 3 reported adherence to the assigned intervention. Among the 10 other designs, 6 accounted for confounders.

The search closed September 2019, and 14 of the studies were largely observational cohorts.
