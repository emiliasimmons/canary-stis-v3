Source: [shade-2018](https://doi.org/10.1097/QAD.0000000000001958)

Micro-costing of the streamlined HIV care model in the 16 SEARCH trial intervention communities (NCT01864603), covering 17 health facilities across West Uganda, East Uganda and Kenya. Two-week site visits from July 2015 to June 2016 combined staff interviews, administrative records, and self-administered time-and-motion records from 140 clinic staff. Costs in 2016 US dollars per person per year.

## Key points

- Observed cost US$290.62 per person-year overall: ART US$117.35 (40.4%), viral load testing US$110.29 (37.9%), personnel US$51.08 (17.6%), other recurring US$5.33 (1.8%), fixed US$6.57 (2.3%). By region, US$299 West Uganda, US$319 East Uganda, US$286 Kenya.
- Personnel cost varied nearly threefold across regions, US$25.62 in West Uganda against US$64.50 in Kenya, and drove most of the between-clinic heterogeneity. ART cost US$150.36 per person-year in both Ugandan regions against US$99.00 in Kenya.
- Three optimizations, applied cumulatively. Lowest-negotiated first-line TDF/3TC/EFV at US$100 per person-year cut US$50 in Uganda and nothing in Kenya. Moving from two viral load tests per year to the recommended one, at Ministry of Health pricing of US$24 per test with specimen transport absorbed into MOH logistics, cut US$86 everywhere. Ministry of Health salary scales cut a further US$4 (West Uganda), US$21 (East Uganda) and US$16 (Kenya). Combined: US$163 per person-year, a 44% reduction.
- Staff time per patient averaged 15.3 min for nurses, 12.6 min for data officers, 11.1 min for peer educators, 11.0 min for laboratory technicians, 9.7 min for clinical officers. Total staff time per patient was 36.3 min in West Uganda, 68.4 min in East Uganda, 103.6 min in Kenya, reflecting how many cadres touched each patient rather than how long the visit took.
- 7,707 HIV-positive patients had at least one streamlined care visit in the observation year, at a mean 5.0 visits per patient per year (4.6 West Uganda, 4.0 East Uganda, 5.3 Kenya).
- Cost per clinic showed no correlation with retention in care (R-squared 0.003) or with viral suppression below 500 copies/ml (R-squared 0.031), so higher-spending clinics were not the better-performing ones.
- The model bundles quarterly rather than monthly visits, co-located clinical, phlebotomy and dispensing services, viral load counselling, 24-hour telephone clinician access, and co-located hypertension and diabetes care, all inside the US$291.

## Relevance to project

The costing reference for routine HIV care in the Rakai-adjacent setting, in more recent dollars than earlier Ugandan micro-costings and with a per-test viral load price (US$24 at Ministry of Health pricing, US$110 per person-year at two tests) that is the natural benchmark for what a health system in this region already pays per diagnostic.

More useful for the POC arm than the cost figures is the time-and-motion result: 9.7 to 15.3 min of clinical staff time per patient visit, itemised by cadre, with three regions differing threefold in total staff time for essentially the same service. That range is what an added point-of-care test has to fit inside, and it says the binding constraint on adding a diagnostic at the clinic door is provider time allocation rather than the kit price.

It supplies nothing on STIs, incidence, or transmission. The population is patients already in HIV care, the outcomes are cost, retention and suppression, and there is no counterfactual arm in this analysis.

## Limitations

- Time-and-motion data were self-recorded by staff over two weeks, so under-recording of idle or break time would inflate the estimated share of effort going to streamlined care.
- Streamlined and standard care ran side by side in the same facilities. Laboratory tests other than viral load, and medications other than ART, were assumed to be used at the same rate in both, which likely overstates streamlined care cost since those patients were healthier.
- Rent and utilities were estimated from clinic manager interviews rather than documented.
- Costs are for care delivery only. Community-based testing campaigns, linkage, and the research apparatus of the trial are outside this figure, so this is not the cost of the SEARCH intervention.
- Trial conditions with donor-supported supply chains. The authors note that Ministry of Health salary levels, used in the optimized scenario, have been linked elsewhere to shorter clinic hours and lower clinician performance, so the US$163 figure assumes performance holds at pay levels where it may not.
