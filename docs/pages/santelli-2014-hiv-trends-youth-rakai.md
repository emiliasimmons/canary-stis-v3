Source: [santelli-2014](https://doi.org/10.1097/qad.0000000000000533)

An analysis of HIV incidence trends among youth (15-24 years, n = 22,164) in the Rakai
Community Cohort Study from 1999 to 2011, spanning the period before widespread ART
roll-out through its early expansion. Incidence rates (per 1,000 person-years) are reported
by sex and age group across survey rounds, alongside trends in behavioural risk factors
(sexual debut, number of partners, condom use) and exposure to prevention and treatment
programmes.

## Relevance to project

A calibration target source. The model's first calibration site is the RCCS, and this paper
provides age- and sex-specific HIV incidence rates among 15-24 year olds across 12 years of
observation. These are directly usable as calibration targets or validation benchmarks for
the HIV component of the model in the pre-POC baseline period. The behavioural trends
(debut age, partner counts, condom use) also provide context for the sexual network
parameters the model assumes are stable or changing over the calibration window.

The decomposition of incidence declines into behavioural versus programmatic contributions
is relevant to the counterfactual: the model's SOC arm needs to reproduce the observed
trajectory of HIV incidence in the absence of POC diagnostics, and this paper helps anchor
what drove that trajectory.

## Key points

HIV incidence among youth in Rakai declined substantially between 1999 and 2011. The
decline was concurrent with reductions in several behavioural risk factors: later sexual
debut, fewer lifetime and recent sexual partners, and increased condom use. These
behavioural shifts were driven in part by national prevention campaigns (ABC: Abstinence,
Be faithful, use Condoms) and school-based programmes.

Incidence trends were stratified by sex and age: young women (15-19 and 20-24) had
consistently higher incidence than young men of the same age, and the magnitude of decline
differed by group. The paper estimates incidence per 1,000 person-years by round, providing
a time series the model can calibrate against.

ART coverage expanded over the study period but was low among youth, so the observed
incidence declines in this age group are attributed primarily to behavioural change and
primary prevention rather than treatment-as-prevention effects.

## Limitations

The abstract provides limited numerical detail on the incidence estimates; the full text
would be needed for the calibration target values. The 1999-2011 window predates universal
test-and-treat (UTT, 2016 in Uganda), so the behavioural and incidence patterns may have
shifted in the years closer to the model's intervention period. Self-reported behavioural
data are subject to social desirability bias, particularly for sexual debut and partner
counts. The study covers youth only (15-24), so incidence among older adults in Rakai must
come from other sources.

## Methodology

Incidence estimated from seroconversions in the open population-based RCCS cohort with
approximately annual HIV testing. Trends in risk factors assessed from structured
questionnaires administered at each survey round. The design is the same surveillance
platform the model calibrates to, which removes a layer of cross-study comparability
concerns.
