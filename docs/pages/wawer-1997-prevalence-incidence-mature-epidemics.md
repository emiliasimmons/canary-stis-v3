Two-year follow-up (1990-1992) of an open cohort of all adults aged 15-59 in 31
representative community clusters in rural Rakai District. Cataloged from abstract; the
full text has not been extracted.

## Relevance to project

Provides early-epidemic HIV incidence in Rakai (2.0-2.1/100 PYO, stable across two
consecutive years) alongside the prevalence-incidence divergence that high mortality
causes. The incidence estimates anchor the pre-ART transmission rate the model must
reproduce for the Rakai site. The HIV-related mortality of 13.5/100 PYO among
seropositive adults is consistent with
[Sewankambo et al. 1994](sewankambo-1994-demographic-impact-hiv-rakai.md) and reinforces
the pre-ART mortality parameter. The core finding (prevalence is not a reliable surrogate
for incidence in a mature epidemic) is a methodological constraint on how the model's
outputs should be compared with observed prevalence trends.

## Key points

- HIV prevalence declined significantly from 23.4% to 20.9% over two years (1990-1992).
- HIV incidence did not change significantly: 2.1 +/- 0.4 per 100 PYO in 1990-1991 and
  2.0 +/- 0.3 per 100 PYO in 1991-1992.
- HIV-related mortality was high at 13.5/100 PYO among seropositive adults, removing
  infected persons faster than seroconversion added them.
- In a mature epidemic, prevalence can decline while incidence remains stable, making
  prevalence an inadequate surrogate for incidence.

## Limitations

Pre-ART era (1990-1992); the absolute incidence and mortality rates apply only to that
period. Incidence estimated from seroconversion between annual survey rounds, so the
interval-censored timing introduces some imprecision.

## Methodology

Open population-based cohort of adults aged 15-59 in 31 clusters across rural Rakai
District, with annual serological surveys over two years. Incidence by seroconversion,
mortality by vital-event ascertainment.
