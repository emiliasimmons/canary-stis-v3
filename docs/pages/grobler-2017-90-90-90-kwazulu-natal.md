A household-based cross-sectional survey (HIV Incidence Provincial Surveillance System, HIPSS) measuring the three UNAIDS 90-90-90 cascade steps directly in a general adult population, using laboratory HIV serology and viral load rather than clinic records or model estimates. 9812 participants aged 15-49 (3547 men, 6265 women) in Vulindlela and Greater Edendale, uMgungundlovu district, KwaZulu-Natal, enrolled June 2014 to June 2015 under a three-stage random sample (212 census enumeration areas, 50 households each, one member per household), population-weighted for selection and non-response. Extracted text at `raw/10.1016/s2352-3018(17)30122-4`.

## Relevance to project

A calibration reference for the HIV care cascade the SOC arm has to reproduce, and a comparator against the Rakai numbers the project calibrates to. The usable quantities, all among laboratory-confirmed HIV-positive adults 15-49 in 2014-15:

- Knows status: 52% of men (95% CI 47-56, 504/1014), 65% of women (62-67, 1833/2955).
- On ART among those who know: 69% of men (63-75, 344/522), 70% of women (68-73, 1254/1845).
- Viral load under 1000 copies/mL among self-reported ART users: 85% of men (80-90, 294/341), 90% of women (87-92, 1117/1249).
- Population viral suppression among all HIV positive: 44% of men (39-49), 58% of women (55-61); 53% overall (50-56).
- ART coverage irrespective of knowledge or CD4: 37% of men (32-41), 46% of women (43-48).

The age gradient is the part that bears on network structure: knowledge of status among 15-19s versus 45-49s carries an adjusted odds ratio of 0.16 (0.09-0.29), and 20-24s 0.31 (0.20-0.47), while viral suppression conditional on ART is high in every age band. Young adults are therefore the untreated, transmitting fraction, which is where the model's high-transmission network positions sit.

Also a bound on the diagnostic-access door the POC arm uses. Testing here is passive and infrequent: 31% of men and 18% of women reported never having been tested, and 22% of HIV-positive men and 22% of HIV-positive women reported a negative result at their most recent test (median 19 and 13 months before survey). Any assumed rate of contact with a testing service in the SOC arm should sit near that, not near a screening ideal.

Not a source for STI parameters. STI history is one self-reported line: ever diagnosed with an STI, 6% of women and 8% of men, with no etiological result reported here despite urine and genital swabs having been collected. Not Rakai, and not directly transportable: HIV prevalence here is 28% in men and 44% in women, well above Rakai, and South African ART eligibility moved from CD4 under 350 to under 500 during the survey.

## Key points

Knowledge of status is the binding constraint, not treatment or suppression. Once a person knows they are positive, linkage is near-complete: 96% (95-97) reported at least one care contact, 92% (90-94) had had a CD4 count, 77% (74-80) a viral load test. The shortfall sits entirely at the entry point.

The sex gap runs through pregnancy testing. 38% of HIV-positive women (35-41) reported an HIV test at an antenatal visit; among women who had never been pregnant, 42% (38-46) had never tested, versus 8% (7-10) among the ever-pregnant. Removing antenatal-tested women, the never-tested share among women rises to 20% (18-23), close to men's.

Self-report understates treatment. Among 79 participants reporting HIV-negative status who tested positive and were selected for drug-level assay, 31 had ART detectable in blood. Also, 20% of HIV-positive men (16-24) and 32% of women (29-36) who denied taking ART were virally suppressed.

## Limitations

Knowledge of status and ART use are self-reported, so social desirability bias pushes the first two cascade steps down; the drug-level assay result above shows the direction. Adherence was not measured. Cross-sectional, so no incidence estimate comes from this analysis. Restricted to 15-49, so the cascade in older adults is unobserved. The funder participated in study design, interpretation, and writing.
