Source: [hughes-2022](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.78565)

A prospective mucosal-immunology study nested in the Kenya Girls Study (Thika, Kenya), paired
with a systematic review and individual-participant meta-analysis of the two comparable
studies worldwide. Twenty immune mediators were measured in cervicovaginal lavage from
adolescent girls and young women before and after first penile-vaginal intercourse.

## Relevance to project

Context, not a parameter. The model's risk structure gives young women elevated HIV and STI
acquisition through partnership formation and partner age mixing; this source is the
biological argument that a behavioural network alone may not carry the whole age gradient,
because genital inflammation itself rises sharply in the first year of sexual activity.

It supplies no number the model can use. The outcome is cytokine concentration in pg/mL, and
the paper draws no link from any mediator to a per-act acquisition probability, so it cannot
be turned into an age- or debut-dependent susceptibility multiplier without an external
mapping the authors explicitly decline to make ("whether the increase in immune mediators
brings protection from or vulnerability to STIs is unclear"). Treat it as a structural
caution: if calibration to Rakai leaves residual STI incidence in the youngest women that the
network cannot produce, this is the named candidate mechanism, and the case for adding a
susceptibility term rather than distorting the mixing matrix.

The chlamydia figure is usable as a rough incidence check only with care: 10 of 69 post-first
sex specimens (14%) were CT-positive versus 0 of 111 pre-first sex, in AGYW with a median age
of 19.1 years. That is a selected sub-study sample of 195 specimens chosen to balance pre and
post participants, not a cohort incidence rate.

## Key points

Final analysis set: 180 cervicovaginal lavage specimens from 95 participants, all Black
Kenyan women assigned female at birth, median age 19.1 years (IQR 18.1-19.6). 111 specimens
pre-first sex, 69 post. Pre-sex specimens came a median 344 days before debut, post-sex
specimens a median 66 days after.

19 of 20 mediators were consistently detectable and all were higher post-debut. Thirteen of
19 reached p<0.05; three survived Holm-Bonferroni across 19 tests: IL-1β (ratio 2.51,
95% CI 1.40-4.51), CXCL8 (2.02, 1.29-3.17), and IL-2 (1.86, 1.32-2.62). Ratios are post-sex
over pre-sex concentration in pg/mL.

Concentrations were flat for three years before debut and rose sharply after. The change in
slope at day 0 was significant for 17 of 19 mediators. The median mediator, CCL20, gained
0.69 log2 pg/mL per 100 days, an average doubling time near five months. The increase is
cumulative, not a step at debut.

The effect survives removing every obvious confounder. Restricting to 137 specimens negative
for pregnancy, contraception, chlamydia, HSV-2, and Nugent score 4 or above left all effects
positive. Adjusting for age, education, income, urban/rural residence, and roof material
changed effect sizes little. Normalizing to total protein shrank the effects modestly but
left all but IL-7 higher post-debut.

Meta-analysis over this study plus 93 Belgian and 18 American participants covered the nine
mediators measured in at least two studies. Eight of nine were higher post-debut, six at
p<0.05: IL-1α (1.96, 1.45-2.64), CXCL8 (2.12, 1.51-2.98), CCL5 (1.55, 1.25-1.91), IL-1β
(2.09, 1.44-3.03), IL-6 (1.41, 1.07-1.86), CCL4 (1.34, 1.06-1.70). Heterogeneity was I²=0 for
four of them. TNF-α went the other way with I²=0.89 and is unresolved.

## Limitations

The fully paired subset, 66 specimens from the 33 participants who gave both a pre- and a
post-debut sample, showed smaller effects and only one significant result. The authors
attribute this to timing, since paired post-samples came a median 31.5 days after debut
against 157 days for post-only participants, and the effect accumulates over months.
Unmeasured confounding inflating the unpaired comparison is the competing explanation and the
paper cannot separate the two.

Debut classification rests on self-report, supplemented by pregnancy, Y-chromosome DNA, PSA,
or a positive NG/CT/TV test. Of 123 specimens where the participant reported no sexual
activity, 12 (9.8%) were reclassified as post-debut on those markers, so residual
misclassification runs in the direction of diluting the effect.

Total sample across all three meta-analysed studies is under 500 women. HSV-2 was nearly
absent (3 seropositive specimens), and TV, NG, and active HSV specimens were dropped for
sparsity, so the result does not speak to how those infections modify the pattern. The
mechanism is unsettled among bacterial vaginosis onset, semen exposure, vaginal washing
(reported by about 56% of the cohort, but not recorded per specimen), and microtrauma.
