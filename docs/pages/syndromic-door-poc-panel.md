The model's two arms differ only in what happens after a person with symptoms reaches
a clinic: the SOC arm treats on the presenting syndrome, the POC arm runs an etiological
panel and treats on the identified pathogen. Both arms are gated by the same two upstream
steps, having a symptom and presenting with it, so the size of the contrast between them
is set by where in the chain the curable-STI burden is lost. In the Rakai setting most of
it is lost before the door, to infection that is asymptomatic and to symptomatic people
who never present. The panel sits at the door and cannot recover either loss. It recovers
only the accuracy of the decision made at the door, which is a large gain for women and
a small one for men.

## The chain from infection to correct treatment

Most infection is asymptomatic and never triggers the door. In the population-based STI
Prevalence Study nested in the RCCS, self-reported clinically indicative symptoms had
a sensitivity for etiological chlamydia, gonorrhoea or trichomoniasis of at most 50% in
women and under 40% in men, and most such infections would not have been diagnosed under
syndromic management ([Grabowski 2022, STIPS](grabowski-2022-stips-sti-burden-rakai.md)).
The same asymmetry appears in a Kenyan and South African cohort of adolescent girls and
young women where 29% had chlamydia and 10% gonorrhoea but only 23% of the 770 infected
women reported any symptom ([Celum 2022](celum-2022-power-prep-agyw.md), urban clinic, not
Rakai). Genital inflammation and cervicitis are as common in infected women without
symptoms as with them, so vaginal discharge is a poor marker of infection and the
asymptomatic fraction carries comparable HIV risk ([Mtshali
2021](mtshali-2021-genital-inflammation-hiv-susceptibility.md)).

Only a minority of symptomatic people reach a clinic. Among adults in two RCCS communities
reporting an STI symptom in six months, 57% sought treatment anywhere and 48% (457/956)
reached a government or private clinic, while 43% did nothing. Of the clinic visits, 86%
were to a private (58%) or government (28%) facility that could host a panel, 9% went to
a pharmacy or drug shop and 3% to a traditional healer ([Ogale
2023](ogale-2023-sti-treatment-seeking-rakai.md)). A clinic-confined rollout therefore
reaches about half of symptomatic adults. Two decades earlier the same population reached
the door less often: of people symptomatic between survey rounds in the mass-treatment
trial, only 16-20% obtained treatment at a project or government clinic ([Wawer
1999](wawer-1999-rakai-std-mass-treatment-trial.md)).

The syndromic flowchart flags less than half of infected women who reach the clinic.
Pooled sensitivity of the vaginal-discharge flowchart for chlamydia or gonorrhoea ranges
from 28% (risk assessment only) to 90% (with laboratory microscopy), with specificity
moving inversely from 57% down to 35%. The common field implementation, history plus risk
plus speculum, sits at 45% sensitivity and 74% specificity ([Wi
2019](wi-2019-sti-diagnostics-resource-constrained.md)). The male urethral-discharge
syndrome performs far better, at 84-95% sensitivity, which is why the door works for men
and fails for women.

Treatment clears the presenting infection when it fires but leaves a large residual
through reinfection and non-presentation. Recent genital sores predicted lower odds of
a current curable STI among HIV-positive people in a Nairobi couples cohort, read as
syndromic treatment having already cleared the symptomatic infection ([Guthrie
2009](guthrie-2009-stis-hiv-discordant-couples.md)). In Rakai, symptomatic adults who had
previously sought clinic care were no less likely to test positive at survey than those
who had not, about a third positive either way (males 33%, females 31%, male PRR 1.23, 95%
CI 0.86-1.75) ([Ogale 2023](ogale-2023-sti-treatment-seeking-rakai.md)). That study cannot
separate reinfection from ineffective treatment, and both leave burden in place. The
syphilis case makes the leak concrete: in a Ugandan delivery population 90.7% of mothers
were treated for a genital syndrome in pregnancy yet 4.1% still had active syphilis at
delivery, and of the RPR-positive women antenatal screening identified, 51.7% were treated
and 40% of partners ([Oloya 2020](oloya-2020-congenital-syphilis-mbarara.md), Mbarara).
Treated syphilis itself carries a 12-20% serologic non-response rate ([Trovato
2021](trovato-2021-syphilis-diagnosis-treatment.md)).

## A composite estimate for women

Multiplying the four terms for a woman's curable STI in Rakai gives a rough figure, with
the caveat that the terms come from different studies and populations and compose only
loosely. Symptomatic (at most 0.50) times reaching a clinic (about 0.48) times flagged by
the flowchart (about 0.45) leaves roughly 11% of prevalent female infections correctly
treated at a cross-section, before reinfection erodes durability. Dropping the flowchart
term, on the reading that any discharge presentation triggers presumptive treatment of the
whole syndrome, still leaves only about 24%. The door works better for men, since urethral
discharge is both a more specific symptom and an 84-95% sensitive flowchart, so the male
loss concentrates in the asymptomatic and non-presenting fractions rather than at the
clinic.

## The point-of-care panel at the door

The panel replaces the flowchart term and not the two terms above it. A molecular assay
lifts detection at the door from the flowchart's 45% to roughly 95%: GeneXpert CT/NG runs
95-98% sensitivity and above 99% specificity on vaginal and urine specimens ([Wi
2019](wi-2019-sti-diagnostics-resource-constrained.md), [Karellis
2022](karellis-2022-multiplexed-rapid-sti-tests.md)). At 15% chlamydia-gonorrhoea
prevalence that is the difference between the flowchart's 219 overtreatments and 83 missed
treatments per 1000 women and the molecular panel's 17 and 8. A cheaper rapid test at 70%
sensitivity and 90% specificity falls between, at 85 and 45.

| per 1000 women, 15% CT/NG prevalence | overtreated | missed |
|---|---|---|
| syndromic flowchart (45%/74%) | 219 | 83 |
| rapid test (70%/90%) | 85 | 45 |
| molecular POC (~96%/~99%) | 17 | 8 |

From [Wi 2019](wi-2019-sti-diagnostics-resource-constrained.md).

The panel roughly doubles the correctly-treated fraction among women who reach the door
and nearly removes overtreatment. For men, whose door already works, its incremental
accuracy is small. It does not touch the 50% or more of infection that is asymptomatic or
the roughly 52% of symptomatic people who never reach a clinic, because the arm replaces
the test at the symptomatic door rather than adding a screening channel ([Grabowski
2022](grabowski-2022-stips-sti-burden-rakai.md)).

Two design assumptions decide whether the panel realizes that gain. The single-visit
assumption, treating within the same encounter with no return-visit loss, rests on a time
to result of 60 minutes or less, which the WHO target product profiles set as the minimal
bar and which GeneXpert at about 90 minutes on tabletop district-hospital hardware does
not meet ([Toskin 2017](toskin-2017-sti-poct-target-product-profiles.md)). Against
laboratory NAAT the timing gain is real: point-of-care testing in a Cape Town antenatal
clinic treated 79% of women the same day against 0% for lab testing, where 28% of
lab-diagnosed women never returned ([de Voux
2022](voux-2022-poc-sti-prep-anc-cape-town.md)). Against syndromic management, which
already treats presumptively at the visit, the panel's advantage is accuracy rather than
timing. The assay sensitivity the panel actually achieves is the parameter the whole gain
is most exposed to, because a missed infection behaves like the SOC arm. The target
product profiles set minimal and optimal floors (gonorrhoea 90/98%, chlamydia above
90/100%, trichomonas 85/98%, treponemal syphilis above 80/90%), no test met them all in
2016, and antigen point-of-care tests were under 50% sensitive for chlamydia and
gonorrhoea on vaginal swabs ([Toskin
2017](toskin-2017-sti-poct-target-product-profiles.md)). Running the arm at a floor and
a ceiling rather than a single sensitivity is what those two values are for. The panel is
not penalized on specimen acceptance: 86% of Rakai women gave a self-collected vaginal
swab against 51% who accepted a pelvic examination ([Safaeian
2006](safaeian-2006-self-collected-swabs-rakai.md)), and molecular assays perform
equivalently on self-collected specimens, so a self-swab panel keeps the acceptance
a clinician-collected one would lose.

## Symptom prevalence and STI burden at the door

Symptom prevalence sets the size of the door. In the STIPS communities 53% of adults
reported any STI symptom in six months, 34% of men and 70% of women, though the female
figure includes substantial non-STI symptomatology such as urinary tract infection and is
a ceiling on STI-driven presentation ([Ogale
2023](ogale-2023-sti-treatment-seeking-rakai.md)). Among self-reported symptomatic adults,
etiological prevalence was chlamydia 11%, gonorrhoea 10%, trichomonas 13%, active syphilis
7%. Self-reported symptom prevalence in Rakai men from the circumcision trials gives
a tighter recall anchor: per-visit control-arm prevalence of about 5.8% genital ulcer
disease, 2.2% discharge and 3.3% dysuria ([Gray
2007](gray-2007-male-circumcision-rakai-trial.md), [Tobian
2009](tobian-2009-circumcision-hsv2-hpv-syphilis.md)). The burden the door is meant to
catch, population-based and by etiology, is any curable STI at 21.2% inland and 31.2% in
the fishing community, and symptomatic fishing men reached a clinic less often than inland
men, 43% against 55% ([Grabowski 2022](grabowski-2022-stips-sti-burden-rakai.md), [Ogale
2023](ogale-2023-sti-treatment-seeking-rakai.md)).

## Open questions

The residual-positivity result cannot separate reinfection from treatment failure, and the
two imply different things for the POC arm. Reinfection means the panel's per-episode gain
is eroded by network exposure the arm does not change, while treatment failure means
correct etiological treatment recovers burden the syndrome-based drugs missed. Nothing in
the canon resolves which dominates in Rakai. The composite reach figure is assembled from
terms measured in different studies and should be reproduced inside the model rather than
trusted as arithmetic. The gap between the target product profiles and the sensitivity an
antigen or molecular test achieves in a task-shifted rural clinic is the largest single
uncertainty on the size of the arm contrast.
