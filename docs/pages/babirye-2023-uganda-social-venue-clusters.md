Secondary analysis of the Uganda PLACE 2018 survey (Priorities for Local AIDS Control
Efforts), a venue-mapping exercise across 25 districts, Rakai among them. Community
informants list the public places where people go to meet new sexual partners, a
stratified sample of venues is visited and profiled, and workers plus a sample of patrons
are interviewed and HIV-tested on site. This paper takes the 480 venues that employed
workers aged 15-24 and their 1007 HIV-negative young workers, clusters the venues on 18
binary features, and regresses a five-item vulnerability index on cluster membership.

## Relevance to project

Structural justification and a rough prior for the venue-mediated part of the risk-
structured network, not a calibration target. The cluster shares say how much of the
partner-meeting infrastructure carries a sex-work component: of 480 venues, 24% (127)
serve alcohol with sex work and on-site sex, 17% (80) serve alcohol with sex work but no
on-site sex, 24% (113) serve alcohol with neither, and 33% (160) serve no alcohol. Two
fifths of venues therefore sit in a sex-work-linked type. Within the highest-risk cluster,
sex workers solicit customers at 76% of venues and sex is provided on site at 79%, against
19% and 7% in the alcohol-serving cluster without sex work. That gradient is the kind of
heterogeneity the model's sex-work and high-activity strata are meant to represent, and
these venue proportions bound how large that stratum can plausibly be in the population
the model draws from.

Second use is service coverage. Any HIV prevention service in the past 12 months (posters,
condom distribution, testing outreach, on-site education) was reported at 37.6% of
highest-risk venues and about 20% of venues in each other cluster; 54.3% of the 447 venues
with a response reported none. That gives a coverage figure for the counterfactual of
prevention delivery reaching high-transmission positions in the network, which is the
mechanism the POC arm competes with.

Not usable for anything on STI burden. The only STI content is a self-reported binary for
ulcerative symptoms in the past 12 months, one of five index components, never reported
alone. HIV prevalence by cluster sits in an unextracted supplemental figure; the discussion
cites 10%-26% prevalence among workers at comparable venues in Uganda and Tanzania, which
is a secondhand range and belongs in the entry only as context.

## Key points

Four clusters emerged from Ward's-method hierarchical clustering on 18 venue features
(activities, who meets partners on site, presence of key populations). Names follow the
features with the highest cluster means, so the labels are descriptive, not thresholds.

Workers at highest-risk venues scored 0.15 higher on the vulnerability index than workers
at alcohol-serving venues without sex work (95% CI 0.030 to 0.287, adjusted for age, sex
and marital status). The index runs 0 to 1 as the proportion of five binary high-risk
markers present: any ulcerative STI symptom, inconsistent condom use over 6 months, two or
more partners in 12 months, any alcohol in 4 weeks, and primary education or less. A 0.15
shift is roughly three quarters of one marker.

Venue cluster membership accounted for 26.7% of the total variance in the index (between-
cluster variance 0.267, residual 0.156). Individual venue characteristics regressed
separately showed no significant association, so the combination of features, not any one
feature, is what tracks with the outcome.

Composition of the highest-risk cluster: 273 young workers, 77% female, over 70% aged
20-24, 72% with no or incomplete primary education, over 80% unmarried. Across all
clusters 69% of young workers were female.

## Limitations

Cross-sectional, so nothing causal. The outcome is a self-reported behaviour and education
index among workers who tested HIV negative, which makes it a proxy for exposure risk
rather than an infection measure; 92% of the 1007 workers fell in the moderate or high
band, so the index barely discriminates at the top. The response rate in the primary
survey was never reported and representativeness is unknown, and the venue sample covers
only venues employing 15-24 year olds, so the cluster proportions describe that subset of
venues rather than all social venues. Cluster validation was a split-half of the same
dataset, not an external one. Districts were chosen purposively on 90-90-90 programme
performance, which is a sampling frame tied to programme weakness, so the national label
does not mean nationally representative. Underreporting of stigmatised behaviour is likely.
One table entry is internally inconsistent: the unadjusted coefficient for previously
married reads 0.97 (SE 0.09) against a CI of -0.095 to 0.289, and the adjusted value of
0.06 is the plausible one.
