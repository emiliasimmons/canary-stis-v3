Source: [trovato-2021](https://doi.org/10.33590/emj/20-00221)

Narrative review from the Dermatology and Skin Bank Unit, University of Siena, covering syphilis epidemiology, clinical staging, diagnostic assays and treatment. Built from a PubMed and Google Scholar search, with 55 of 100 screened papers included. The dermatologic and dermoscopic material is the bulk of the article; the parts that bear on this project are the stage timings, the transmission estimate, and the assay accuracy table.

## Relevance to project

Two usable roles, both weak because this is an unreferenced-consensus review rather than a primary study.

A prior on the syphilis natural-history stage durations the model needs: chancre appears 2-3 weeks after contact with an infectious lesion and resolves spontaneously in 3-6 weeks untreated; secondary manifestations follow 3-12 weeks after chancre resolution (sometimes concurrently) and resolve in 4-12 weeks untreated; the CDC 1-year cut-off separates early from late latent; tertiary disease develops in roughly one third of untreated infections after years to decades. Per-sexual-exposure transmission risk approximately 33%, quoted without a stated denominator or partnership definition, so it is a plausibility bound rather than a calibration target.

A comparator for the POC arm's syphilis test performance, from Table 1, all for **primary** syphilis (the hardest stage, and the stage where sensitivity is lowest): treponemal point-of-care-adjacent formats reach ELISA 96.0-100.0% sensitivity with 90.0-98.0% specificity and TPPA 86.2-100.0% / 99.6-100.0%, while the nontreponemal RPR runs 62.5-76.1% (a second cited range, 77.0-99.0%) with 93.0-99.0% specificity and VDRL 62.5-78.4% / 96.0-99.0%. Darkfield microscopy 62.0-92.0% sensitivity, specificity not assessed. *T. pallidum* PCR 72.3-77.8% / 93.1-100.0%. These are pooled ranges across the review's cited studies with no per-study population, specimen type or reference standard given, so they set the plausible interval for a POC syphilis test sensitivity parameter and do not fix its value.

Not usable as an incidence or prevalence target. The epidemiologic figures are US and western-European (CDC-reported cases up 81% in MSM 2014-2018; primary and secondary syphilis rates in females more than doubling 2014-2018), which does not transfer to Rakai. The global figure of more than 5 million new cases per year is uncited here.

Treatment side, for the SOC and POC arms' cure assumptions: benzathine penicillin G 2.4 MU intramuscular single dose for primary, secondary and early latent; the same dose weekly for three weeks for late latent; intravenous aqueous crystalline penicillin G 10-14 days for neuro-, otic- and ocular syphilis; doxycycline 100 mg twice daily for 14 days (early) or 28 days (late) where penicillin-allergic. No penicillin resistance reported to date. Penicillin in pregnancy is 98% effective at preventing congenital syphilis. Serologic nonresponse (no 4-fold titer decline) affects 12-20% of treated primary and secondary syphilis, which is the closest thing here to a treatment-failure rate the model could use, though the review does not separate it from reinfection.

## Key points

The HIV interaction is asserted mechanistically: syphilitic genital ulcers are densely infiltrated with lymphocytes, the primary HIV target cells, giving both an entry breach for acquisition and a focus for onward transmission. No effect size is attached, so this supports the model's syphilis-HIV cofactor structurally without parameterising it.

Serologic testing is sequential in the recommended algorithm: nontreponemal first, then treponemal to confirm. Treponemal tests are more sensitive than nontreponemal in early infection. IgM and IgG become detectable around 2 and 4 weeks after exposure. Nontreponemal titers become nonreactive in roughly 3 months in many successfully treated patients; persistent low titers (1:1 to 1:4) are serofast reactions, more common with HIV. Nontreponemal false negatives occur in HIV infection, pregnancy, and neurosyphilis or lues maligna.

Partner notification is discussed only as clinician advice and optional health-department assistance, with the observation that app-mediated anonymous partnering has reduced notification feasibility. The review gives no notification yield or partner-treatment rate, so it does not bear on the model's absent partner-notification pathway.

## Limitations

A narrative review with no quality appraisal of the included studies and no meta-analytic pooling. Table 1's accuracy ranges carry no per-study citation mapping, no specimen types, no sample sizes and no consistent reference standard, and several figures in the text (5 million cases, 33% transmission risk, 98% pregnancy efficacy, 12-20% nonresponse) appear without an attached citation. The clinical and epidemiologic framing is high-income, MSM-weighted and dermatology-clinic-based, which is the opposite of the population-based heterosexual Rakai setting. Nothing here is field-evaluation data for a point-of-care syphilis test under task-shifted conditions.
