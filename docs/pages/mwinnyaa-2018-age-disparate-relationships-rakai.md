Cross-sectional analysis of 10,061 never-married women aged 15–49 in the Rakai Community Cohort Study, 1997–2013. Cataloged from abstract; the full text has not been extracted.

## Relevance to project

Age-disparate partnership frequency (30% of never-married women reporting a male partner five or more years older) is a direct input to the model's age-mixing matrix. The HIV prevalence ratio between age-disparate and age-concordant partnerships (14% vs 10%, adjusted prevalence ratio 1.36, 95% CI 1.22–1.53) provides a calibration comparator for HIV risk stratified by partnership age gap. The stronger association in women aged 15–17 (adjusted prevalence ratio 1.83, 95% CI 1.10–3.17) can bound the age-specific mixing effect among AGYW. The stability of age-disparate partnership frequency over the 15-year observation window supports treating the mixing pattern as time-invariant in the model.

Does not provide data on STI prevalence by partnership type, concurrency, or partnership duration.

## Key points

- 30% (2,999/10,061) of never-married women had a male partner five or more years older; this proportion was stable from 1997 to 2013.
- HIV prevalence was 14% in women with age-disparate partners vs 10% in women with partners 0–4 years older (adjusted prevalence ratio 1.36, 95% CI 1.22–1.53).
- After age adjustment the overall association attenuated, consistent with the age-disparate effect operating partly through exposure to older (higher-prevalence) male partners.
- Among women aged 15–17 the association remained after adjustment (adjusted prevalence ratio 1.83, 95% CI 1.10–3.17); in older age groups it did not.

## Limitations

- Cross-sectional design: the prevalence ratio reflects cumulative exposure, not incident risk.
- Partner age reported by the woman, not independently verified.
- Never-married women only; the mixing pattern may differ for married or previously married women in the same population.
- No data on partnership concurrency, duration, or number of partners.

## Methodology

Population-based cohort (RCCS), women aged 15–49, self-reported age of most recent male sexual partner. Prevalence ratios estimated with modified Poisson regression adjusted for woman's age, education, and community type.
