Cataloged from abstract; the full text has not been extracted.

Systematic review and meta-analysis of 67 studies reporting prevalence of curable STIs
among women living with HIV in sub-Saharan Africa.

## Relevance to project

The model tracks co-circulating HIV and four curable bacterial STIs, with STI-HIV
coinfection as a central mechanism. This paper provides pooled STI prevalence estimates
among women with HIV in SSA that can serve as calibration comparators or plausibility
bounds for modelled coinfection prevalence. The regional stratification (Eastern Africa
chlamydia prevalence 2.8% vs. Southern 12.5%) is directly relevant because the model's
first site is in Eastern Africa (Rakai, Uganda). The elevated relative risk of each STI
among women with HIV (RR 1.54-1.89) provides an empirical check on whether the model's
shared risk structure reproduces the observed coinfection clustering.

The prevalence estimates are among women with HIV specifically, not the general population,
so they are not direct calibration targets for population-level STI prevalence. The
denominator matters when comparing to model output.

## Key points

Pooled prevalence among women living with HIV in SSA: gonorrhoea 3.5%, chlamydia 4.0%,
trichomoniasis 15.6%, *M. genitalium* 10.2%.

Chlamydia prevalence was lower in Eastern Africa (2.8%) than in Southern (12.5%) and
West/Central Africa (19.1%).

Prevalence was higher among pregnant than nonpregnant women for chlamydia (8.1% vs. 1.7%)
and trichomoniasis (17.6% vs. 12.3%).

All four STIs were more prevalent among women with HIV than among HIV-negative women
(risk ratio range 1.54-1.89 across pathogens).

## Limitations

Prevalence estimates are pooled across studies spanning different years, diagnostic methods,
and populations (clinic-based, antenatal, community), introducing heterogeneity. The
abstract does not report I-squared or confidence intervals for the pooled estimates.
*M. genitalium* is not one of the four STIs the model tracks. The study includes only
women, so male coinfection prevalence is not addressed.

## Methodology

Systematic review and meta-analysis of 67 studies. Pooled prevalence by random-effects
meta-analysis. Subgroup analyses by region, pregnancy status, and HIV status.
