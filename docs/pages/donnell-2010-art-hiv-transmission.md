Source: [donnell-2010](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(10)60705-2)

Post-hoc cohort analysis of the Partners in Prevention HSV/HIV Transmission Study, a
placebo-controlled trial of aciclovir suppression in 3381 heterosexual HIV-serodiscordant
couples across 14 sites in seven African countries (Botswana, Kenya, Rwanda, South Africa,
Tanzania, Uganda, Zambia), enrolled 2004-2007 and followed up to 24 months. Index partners
were HIV-1 and HSV-2 seropositive with CD4 at least 250 cells per microlitre at enrolment;
ART was started during follow-up per then-current national guidelines (threshold roughly
200-250 cells per microlitre).

The outcome is phylogenetically linked transmission within the study partnership, so
infections acquired outside the couple (39 events, 0.81 per 100 person-years) are excluded
from the numerator rather than misattributed.

## Relevance to project

A source of transmission-rate levels for the HIV side of the model, on a
within-partnership denominator. Three uses.

Baseline infectiousness in a serodiscordant partnership without treatment: 102 linked
transmissions over 4558 person-years, 2.24 per 100 person-years (95% CI 1.84-2.72), in
couples reporting a median 4 sex acts in the past month and 29% reporting any unprotected
act. This is a per-partner-year, not a per-act, quantity, and it sits on a counselled trial
population with condoms and syndromic STI treatment supplied throughout, so it is a lower
bound on an uncounselled population.

A bound on the ART effect on onward transmission: one linked transmission in 273
person-years after ART start, 0.37 per 100 person-years (0.09-2.04), adjusted incidence
rate ratio 0.08 (0.00-0.57). The single event occurred 18 to 108 days after initiation,
before suppression, which supports treating the ART effect as ramping over the first few
months rather than switching on at initiation. Suppression itself was 70% (241/344) at a
median 7.3 months.

Viral-load and CD4 gradients for staging infectiousness. Untreated rates per 100
person-years by lowest previous CD4: 8.79 (4.40-17.58) below 200, 2.79 (2.06-3.80) at
200-349, 1.70 (1.14-2.54) at 350-499, 1.82 (1.27-2.62) at 500 or above. Within untreated
index partners above 200 cells per microlitre, rates run 4.66 and 4.23 per 100 person-years
at plasma HIV-1 above 50 000 copies per mL (CD4 200-349 and 350 or above respectively),
against 0.27 and 0.76 below 10 000 copies per mL. 66 of 94 transmissions (70%) came from
the above-50 000 group, which held only 33% of person-years. That concentration is the
project's comparator for a targeting argument: it is the HIV-side analogue of the claim
that identifying high-transmission positions buys more than treating an average case.

What it cannot do here: it carries no STI cofactor estimate. All index partners were HSV-2
seropositive by design and STIs were treated syndromically per WHO guidelines in both study
arms, so the cohort holds no contrast between STI-infected and STI-free index partners and
cannot supply a cofactor multiplier for the POC arm. It also says nothing about partner
notification as a delivery mechanism, only about transmission measured inside known
partnerships.

## Key points

349 of 3381 index partners (10%) started ART, at a median CD4 of 198 (IQR 161-265) cells
per microlitre; 52% were below 200 at the visit before initiation. Median time from
enrolment to initiation was 13 months (8-17), and susceptible partners were followed a
median 8.2 months (3.9-12.3) afterwards.

Median plasma HIV-1 fell from 4.88 log10 copies per mL (3.97-5.41) before ART to below the
2.38 log10 limit of quantification at the final visit.

No behavioural disinhibition. Visits reporting unprotected sex fell from 6.2% before
initiation to 3.7% after (adjusted odds ratio 0.63, 0.41-0.96), and monthly sex acts did
not change. Adjusting for unprotected acts left the ART effect essentially unchanged
(incidence rate ratio 0.09, 0.00-0.61).

Above 200 cells per microlitre the ART effect was not statistically resolved: adjusted
incidence rate ratio 0.55 (0.01-3.24) for the combined strata, on 141 person-years of
treated follow-up.

## Limitations

Observational and post-hoc: ART initiation was guideline-driven and confounded with time on
study and with CD4, adjusted for but not randomised. ART use was self-reported at 3-monthly
visits, any reported use marked the whole 3-month period as exposed, and use was carried
forward once reported (45 of 349 later reported no use). Adherence was not measured.

Treated person-time above CD4 250 is thin (30 and 21 person-years in the 350-499 and
500-plus strata), so the paper cannot estimate the prevention benefit of early initiation,
which is what HPTN 052 was then running to answer.

Follow-up is at most 24 months against a lifetime of treatment, and the estimate is a
short-horizon one.
