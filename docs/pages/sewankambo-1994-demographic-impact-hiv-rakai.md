Source: [sewankambo-1994](https://doi.org/10.1097/00002030-199412000-00011)

One-year follow-up (1990-1991) of a population-based rural cohort in Rakai District,
measuring HIV-specific mortality, infant mortality by maternal serostatus, and population
growth. Cataloged from abstract; the full text has not been extracted.

## Relevance to project

Pre-ART HIV-attributable mortality in Rakai (118.4/1,000 person-years among seropositive
adults vs 12.4/1,000 among seronegative, RR 9.5) provides a baseline mortality differential
the model needs for the pre-treatment era. Adult HIV prevalence of 13% and the 52%
HIV-attributable fraction of adult deaths anchor the demographic weight of the epidemic
before any intervention. Not directly a calibration target for the current model (which
starts later), but constrains the plausibility of early-epidemic trajectories.

## Key points

- Mortality among HIV-seropositive adults aged 15+ was 118.4/1,000 PY, compared with
  12.4/1,000 PY in seronegative adults (rate ratio 9.5).
- Infant mortality among offspring of HIV-infected mothers was nearly double that of
  uninfected mothers (210 vs 111 per 1,000 live births; RR 1.9).
- Adult HIV prevalence in the district was estimated at 13%.
- HIV was the leading cause of adult death, accounting for 52% of adult mortality.
- Despite high mortality, the crude birth rate (45.7/1,000) exceeded the crude death rate
  (28.1/1,000), sustaining rapid population growth.

## Limitations

One-year follow-up in 1990-1991, before ART availability. The mortality rates apply only to
the pre-treatment era and cannot be carried forward to the ART-era model without
adjustment.

## Methodology

Population-based open cohort of all adults aged 15+ in rural Rakai District with one-year
follow-up. HIV serostatus by ELISA with Western blot confirmation. Vital events recorded
through household surveillance.
