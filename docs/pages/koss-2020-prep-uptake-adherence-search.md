Source: [koss-2020](https://doi.org/10.1016/s2352-3018(19)30433-3)

Cataloged from abstract; the full text has not been extracted.

72-week interim analysis from the SEARCH study. Following community sensitisation and PrEP
education, HIV testing and PrEP were offered at health fairs and facilities in 16 rural
communities in western Kenya and Uganda. Of 69,121 HIV-negative residents tested, 12,935
had elevated HIV risk and 3,489 (27%) initiated PrEP. 82% initiated on the same day as
testing; 50% were men.

## Relevance to project

PrEP is a competing HIV-prevention exposure that determines the counterfactual against
which the POC arm is measured. This paper gives population-level PrEP uptake and retention
in a rural East African setting comparable to Rakai. The 27% uptake among elevated-risk
adults and the steep adherence decline (40% self-reported at week 4, 27% at week 72)
provide an upper bound on effective PrEP coverage in the general population, since this
was an actively promoted programme. The age gradient (aOR 0.55 for ages 15-24) informs
age-specific PrEP uptake if the model differentiates by age. Hair biomarker data (66% with
4+ doses/week at week 24, 44% with daily dosing) give an objective check on self-reported
adherence.

Not directly from Rakai: the 16 communities are in western Kenya and Uganda, so the rates
are transferable only as an approximate bound, not as a site-specific calibration target.

## Key points

Among those with elevated HIV risk, 27% (3,489/12,935) initiated PrEP. Uptake was lower
among ages 15-24 (aOR 0.55) and mobile individuals (aOR 0.61).

Engagement declined over time: at week 4, 64% engaged, 49% refilled, 40% self-reported
adherence. At week 72, 56% engaged, 33% refilled, 27% self-reported adherence.

Among those reporting ongoing HIV risk at weeks 4-72, refill rates were 89-93% and
self-reported adherence 70-76%, suggesting effective prevention-effective adherence among
those who perceive continued risk.

Tenofovir concentrations in hair at week 24: 66% had levels consistent with 4+ doses/week,
44% with daily dosing.

## Limitations

The study population was defined by a risk-screening tool, so the denominator is not all
HIV-negative adults. Self-reported adherence overestimates true adherence relative to the
hair biomarker. The western Kenya/Uganda setting may differ from south-central Uganda in
risk structure and health infrastructure. The 72-week timepoint is interim; longer-term
retention is not reported.

## Methodology

Observational cohort nested in the SEARCH cluster-randomised trial. PrEP eligibility by an
empirically validated risk score. Uptake and retention from clinic records; adherence from
self-report and tenofovir-diphosphate in hair at week 24.
