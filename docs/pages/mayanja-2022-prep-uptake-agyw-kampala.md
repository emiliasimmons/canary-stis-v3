Source: [mayanja-2022](https://doi.org/10.1002/jia2.25909)

Prospective cohort at the Good Health for Women Project clinic in Kampala, following 265
adolescent girls and young women (AGYW) aged 14-24 for 12 months from enrolment in January-
October 2019. All were HIV-negative, PrEP-naive, sexually active in the prior three months,
and drawn from 22 urban-slum communities where sex work and entertainment-venue work are
common. Volunteers were shown five biomedical prevention products (oral PrEP, long-acting
injectable PrEP, ARV vaginal ring, PrEP implant, HIV vaccine) across two education sessions,
ranked them 1-5, and were then offered daily TDF/3TC.

## Relevance to project

A cascade-attrition prior for any PrEP-targeting pathway in the model, and a comparator for
the claim that identifying a high-transmission position translates into protection. The chain
here is willingness 90.2% (239/265) to uptake 30.6% (81/265) to protective drug level 7.1%
(3/42 samples in months 1-3, and 0 of 58 samples thereafter). If the POC arm's value runs
partly through steering diagnosed people toward PrEP, this cohort bounds how much of that
steering survives to pharmacological protection in a comparable Ugandan high-risk female
population: effectively none past three months, on plasma tenofovir.

It also supplies a targeting-efficiency number: uptake rose with reported partner count,
adjusted RR 2.36 (95% CI 1.20-4.63) for 2-9 partners and 4.70 (2.41-9.17) for 10 or more,
both relative to one partner. Self-selection into PrEP tracks the same risk axis the model's
network structure encodes, so PrEP coverage should not be applied uniformly across risk
groups.

Baseline STI prevalence gives a burden anchor for a comparable population, though not a
calibration target for the model's Rakai site: 26.9% (71/264 screened) had chlamydia,
gonorrhoea, or active syphilis, with CT/NG by GeneXpert on endocervical swab and syphilis by
RPR/TPPA. This is a self-selected clinic-recruited urban sample in Kampala, not
population-based, so it sits above what a general-population Rakai target should be.

What it cannot do: it carries no HIV incidence, no partnership data beyond partner counts in
the past three months, and no syndromic-versus-etiological comparison. Having an STI at
enrolment was not associated with PrEP uptake (unadjusted RR 1.09, 95% CI 0.73-1.63), so it
gives no support for an STI-diagnosis-triggers-PrEP mechanism in this population.

## Key points

Of 265 offered PrEP, 47.6% ranked oral PrEP first or second among the five products. Reasons
given by the 184 decliners: low HIV risk perception 33.7%, dislike of daily pills 27.2%, not
ready 21.7%, preference for another method 12.5%, side-effect concern 2.7%, stigma 2.2%.

Among 81 starters, 51.9% started on the day PrEP was offered, 20.9% within 30 days, 27.2%
later. Median time on PrEP was 2.52 months (IQR not reported here); 55 discontinued (17 lost
to follow-up, 12 side effects, 9 no longer interested or lower perceived risk, 17 other), 4
restarted, and 30 (37.0% of starters) finished the study still on PrEP.

Self-report and assay diverge sharply. Optimal self-reported adherence (>=90% of days) was
78.6% at months 1-3 and 75.0% at months 10-12, while samples below the limit of quantification
(<10 ug/L plasma tenofovir) rose from 73.8% to 93.8% over the same span. Protective level was
defined as >=40 ug/L, consistent with daily dosing.

Baseline awareness of the products was low: oral PrEP 24.5%, injectable PrEP 4.2%, vaginal
ring 2.3%, HIV vaccine 1.5%, implant 0%.

## Limitations

Non-random peer-led recruitment from clinic catchment communities, so prevalence and uptake
levels do not generalize to a population denominator. The COVID-19 lockdown (25 March-31 May
2020) fell nine months into follow-up and drove missed and late visits to 79.8% of month-9 and
100% of month-12 visits, which confounds the late-follow-up adherence and retention figures
against a counterfactual of ordinary care. Plasma tenofovir reflects only recent dosing, so
the assay measures dosing in the days before a quarterly visit rather than cumulative
exposure. Monthly rather than quarterly refills, driven by supply, may have added visit
fatigue relative to standard programmatic delivery. Preference was binarized at rank 1-2,
which the authors note likely inflated the "preferred" group relative to those who would act
on it.
