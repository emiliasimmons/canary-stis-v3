Qualitative study around Uganda's 2017 PrEP pilot for priority populations (sex workers,
fishermen, truck drivers, HIV-discordant couples), comparing anticipated barriers before
rollout with reported experience after, from 75 in-depth interviews and 12 focus groups
with community members and providers in south-central Uganda.

## Relevance to project

Names the mechanism behind PrEP discontinuation in the model's calibration region.
Mobility is the dominant driver: occupational travel among fishermen and sex workers, and
unanticipated migration, break the refill cycle. This argues that PrEP attrition in the
model should be tied to the mobility and migration attributes already carried by agents
rather than modelled as an untargeted constant hazard, and it predicts that attrition
concentrates in exactly the high-activity positions in the sexual network that matter most
for onward HIV transmission.

Anticipated supply-side barriers (untrained volunteers distributing PrEP, price gouging)
did not materialise, so a model need not carry a supply constraint on PrEP for this setting.

Provides no rates. Nothing here supports a coverage, uptake, or discontinuation parameter
value; the contribution is which covariates a discontinuation model should condition on.

## Key points

Adherence support came from partners, family, peers, and experienced PrEP users, which
places PrEP continuation partly inside the same partnership structure the model uses for
transmission.

Awareness of high community HIV risk and trust in PrEP efficacy were the reported
facilitators of uptake after rollout, so perceived risk mediates uptake independent of
measured risk.

PrEP stigma persisted after rollout despite pre-rollout concerns about distribution and
pricing being resolved.

Recommended responses were flexible delivery and refill models and communication led by
successful PrEP users.

## Limitations

Written from the abstract alone; no PDF was available on this machine, so the coding
framework, the split of findings by population, and the pre-post comparison detail are not
recorded here.

Purposively sampled priority populations, so the barriers described are those of sex
workers, fishermen, truck drivers, and discordant couples, not of the general RCCS
population. Reported experience is self-described and collected in the pilot's first years,
before national scale-up changed delivery.
