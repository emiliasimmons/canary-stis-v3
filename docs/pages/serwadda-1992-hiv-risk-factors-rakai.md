Cross-sectional serological and behavioural survey of 1,292 consenting adults in 21
randomly selected community clusters across three geographic strata of rural Rakai
District: main road trading centres, small trading villages, and agricultural villages.
Cataloged from abstract; the full text has not been extracted.

## Relevance to project

Early baseline HIV prevalence in Rakai by sex (15% men, 24% women) and by community type,
predating the RCCS cohort rounds the model calibrates against. The interaction between
community-level prevalence and individual sexual behaviour (higher HIV risk per partner at
higher background prevalence) is directly relevant to the model's risk-structured network:
it supports the premise that individual risk cannot be separated from the network context.
The circumcision and STD-history associations in men inform the cofactor and circumcision
parameters.

## Key points

- HIV-1 prevalence was 15% in men and 24% in women across the three strata.
- On multivariate analysis, age, residence, and number of sex partners were significantly
  associated with HIV infection in both sexes.
- History of STD and not having been circumcised were significant risk factors in men.
- A significant interaction existed between place of residence and reported number of sex
  partners: for any given level of sexual activity, HIV risk was markedly increased where
  background community prevalence was high.

## Limitations

Cross-sectional design cannot distinguish cause from consequence for behavioural
correlates. The 1989-1990 data predate ART and circumcision scale-up, so the absolute
prevalence figures reflect a different prevention landscape.

## Methodology

Population-based serological survey with sociodemographic and behavioural questionnaire in
a stratified random sample of 21 clusters from Rakai District. Multivariate logistic
regression for risk-factor analysis.
