A mixed-methods analysis of the Rakai Community Cohort Study restricted to adolescents aged
15-19, covering 21,735 person-rounds across 28 continuously surveyed communities from 1994 to
2013 (rounds 1-15, round 5 excluded). Its subject is school enrollment: what predicts it, and
what behaviours and infection outcomes go with it. Extracted text and the eight tables are in
`../raw/10.1007/s40609-015-0029-x`.

## Relevance to project

Behavioural context and a soft prior for the youngest end of the model's 15-49 age range, not a
calibration target. The paper reports no STI outcomes at all: HIV serostatus is the only
biological measure, so nothing here constrains gonorrhoea, chlamydia, syphilis or trichomoniasis
prevalence, and nothing bears on either arm's diagnostic pathway.

What it does supply is the size of the behavioural split inside a single age band, pooled over
rounds 1-15 and age-adjusted. Among sexually experienced adolescent men, 2+ partners in the past
year runs 28.4 % for enrollees against 41.6 % for non-enrollees, and concurrency at interview
6.0 % against 10.6 %. Among sexually experienced adolescent women the same two contrasts are
essentially flat (10.5 vs 10.7 % and 1.9 vs 1.8 %), while consistent condom use with all partners
splits 69.5 vs 17.1 %. HIV prevalence among all adolescent women is 1.8 % for enrollees and 6.5 %
for non-enrollees; among men, 0.4 vs 1.1 %. If the model's risk-group assignment at age 15-19 is
tuned by hand, this is roughly the spread a two-group split of that band would have to reproduce,
and it says the male split is carried by partner count while the female split is carried by
condom use and marriage rather than by partner count.

It also documents secular drift in the cohort over exactly the period a Rakai calibration would
cover, which matters if 1994 and 2013 rounds are treated as draws from one stationary process.
Enrollment rose from 25 to 57 % in women and 34 to 67 % in men; ever-married among adolescent
women fell from 47 % (round 4) to 25 % (round 15) and among men from 4.9 to 1.2 %; households in
the lowest SES category fell from 49 % (1994) to a majority (60 %) in the highest by 2013. Ever
had sex rose to round 8 then fell to round 15 in both sexes.

## Key points

Marriage and schooling are near-mutually exclusive: 0.2 % of ever-married adolescent women were
enrolled, against 65 % of never-married. In the age-adjusted multivariate logistic model
(n = 8,782 women, 7,335 men), ever married gives OR 0.004 for women and 0.24 for men, and age OR
0.63 per year for women, 0.60 for men. Losing both parents gives OR 0.52 in both sexes; for women,
mother died OR 0.50 and father died OR 0.66, while for men father's death is null (OR 1.02).
Household size raises enrollment (OR 1.13 per member, women) but a household more than 80 %
under-20s lowers it (OR 0.77).

Orphanhood among adolescents was around 50 % in early rounds and declined after roughly 2004,
which the authors attribute to ART rollout and falling parental mortality. Uganda's Universal
Primary Education began in 1997, but enrollment among 15-16 year olds was already rising from
1994.

Rakai programme history relevant to any Rakai calibration: STI treatment for HIV prevention from
1994, PMTCT from 2000, ART and the male circumcision RCT from 2004, district-wide circumcision
services from 2007.

## Limitations

Cross-sectional associations only. Enrollment is self-reported occupation ("student"), with no
measure of school quality or attendance, and behaviours are self-reported in face-to-face
interview, so school enrollees may under-report risk. The design cannot separate enrollment
causing lower risk from lower-risk adolescents staying enrolled, and marriage sits on both sides
of the causal arrow (the authors class it as an antecedent for convenience). Marriage, condom use
and alcohol items are missing from some rounds, shown as NA in the round-by-round tables. Attrition
is heavy: over rounds 1-14, 42 % of adolescents surveyed in one round were lost to follow-up, and
mobility for school and work is itself correlated with the exposure. One rural district, so the
enrollment gradients need not transfer.
