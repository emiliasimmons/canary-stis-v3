In-depth interviews with 27 women in fishing and trading communities in Rakai, Uganda,
covering perceptions of medical male circumcision and the influence women exert on whether
their partners get circumcised. Participants included women married to circumcised and to
uncircumcised men, single women, and sex workers. Analysis followed a team-based framework
approach. Cataloged from the abstract; no full text on this machine.

## Relevance to project

Two things bear on the model, neither quantitative. First, uptake of a prevention service
in this population is partly a partner-level decision rather than an individual one, which
is a caution against modelling circumcision or any other uptake as an independent
per-agent draw. Second, women expected circumcised men to increase sexual risk behaviour
after healing, the risk-compensation mechanism that would offset part of the direct
protective effect in a network model. The source gives no coverage figure, no effect size,
and no behaviour-change magnitude, so it can motivate a sensitivity arm but cannot
parameterise one.

## Key points

- All 27 participants preferred circumcised partners, citing lower perceived HIV and STI
  risk, penile hygiene, and sexual pleasure.
- Named drawbacks: abstinence during wound healing, expected increase in male sexual risk
  behaviour, fear of being blamed for HIV acquisition, and lost income during recovery.
- Women described concrete levers over partners: persuasion, accompanying the man to the
  clinic, refusing sex with uncircumcised men, and community mobilisation.
- Fishing and trading communities in Rakai, the higher-prevalence settings in that cohort
  area.

## Limitations

Written from the abstract alone. Twenty-seven purposively sampled women in two community
types cannot support prevalence or frequency claims, and stated preference for circumcised
partners is not observed behaviour. The perceived-risk statements are participants'
beliefs about STI protection, which for the non-ulcerative STIs the model tracks do not
match the trial evidence.
