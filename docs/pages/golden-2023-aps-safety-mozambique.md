> Note: summary derived from the abstract only, not the full source.

Prospective programme evaluation of assisted partner notification services (APS) for newly diagnosed HIV-positive individuals at three public health centres in Maputo, Mozambique, 2016 to 2019. Counsellors offered APS to new diagnoses and prospectively assessed adverse events (AEs): being pushed, abandoned, or yelled at; being hit; and loss of financial support or expulsion from the house. It is one of the few reports of APS safety data from a routine African public health programme, alongside cascade effectiveness.

## Relevance to project

The model does not implement a partner-notification pathway, so this entry bounds what the model excludes. The cascade quantifies the reach of an APS arm if one were added: of 8,933 partners named as having no previous HIV diagnosis, 6,137 tested and 3,367 (55%) were diagnosed HIV-positive, a case-finding index of 0.36 new diagnoses per index case. That 55% positivity among tested partners is the targeting efficiency of a network-informed case-finding strategy, far above general-population testing yield, and it is the same concentration-of-effort the POC arm exploits through symptomatic care-seeking rather than through partner tracing.

The safety data is the distinctive contribution. APS is sometimes withheld over concern about intimate partner violence and social harm. An adverse-event rate of 1.2% among index cases who notified partners, with events associated with fear of losing financial support and with having a partner who was notified but not tested, is evidence that APS can be delivered at scale in a sub-Saharan African programme without common serious harm.

Not a calibration target: Mozambique, not Rakai, and facility-level programme data rather than a population cohort.

## Key points

- Of 18,965 persons testing HIV-positive across the three clinics, 13,475 (71%) were evaluated for APS eligibility, 9,314 were eligible and offered APS, and 9,219 received it.
- Index cases named 8,933 partners without a prior HIV diagnosis; 6,137 tested and 3,367 (55%) were diagnosed HIV-positive (case-finding index 0.36).
- Follow-up data were collected from 6,680 (95%) of 7,034 index cases with untested partners who were subsequently notified; 78 (1.2%) experienced an adverse event.
- Among 270 index cases who reported a fear of adverse events at intake, 211 (78%) still notified at least one partner, of whom 5 (2.4%) experienced an event.
- Experiencing an adverse event was associated with fear of loss of support (OR 4.28, 95% CI 1.50 to 12.19) and with having a partner who was notified but not tested (OR 3.47, 95% CI 1.93 to 6.26).

## Limitations

Abstract only: methods detail and the cascade denominators beyond those quoted were not read. Three facilities in one city, so generalisability to rural or lower-resourced settings is limited. Adverse-event ascertainment depends on index-case self-report at follow-up, which likely undercounts events occurring after contact with services ends. No passive-referral comparison arm, so effectiveness is a cascade yield rather than a relative gain.
