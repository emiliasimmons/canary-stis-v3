Decision analytic model, one tree per country for 43 sub-Saharan African countries, comparing universal antenatal screening with an immunochromatographic strip (ICS) treponemal rapid test plus three benzathine penicillin injections against the current national screening rate. National health system perspective, 2011 US dollars, benefits restricted to the infant. Extracted content in [raw/10.1371/journal.pmed.1001545/](../raw/10.1371/journal.pmed.1001545/).

## Key points

- Test performance taken as 86% sensitivity (95% CI 74.5-94.1) and 99% specificity (97.8-99.7) for the ICS test under field conditions in antenatal clinics, from a systematic review of resource-poor-setting evaluations.
- Unit costs: US$0.74 per ICS test kit including consumables, US$1.92 per benzathine penicillin dose, 17.5 min of nurse time for the test and 20 min per each of three injection visits, hourly nurse wages from US$0.31 (DR Congo) to US$8.25 (South Africa). Full screening cost per woman ran US$0.83-3.15 (median US$1.22); a full treatment course US$6.07-14.01 (median US$7.40).
- Outcome probabilities in untreated maternal syphilis, taken from the Gomez meta-analysis: stillbirth 25.6%, early neonatal death 12.3%, congenital syphilis 15.5%, against 4.6% stillbirth in women without syphilis. Penicillin reduced these by 58%, 54% and 66.5% respectively (from Hawkes), giving post-treatment risks of 10.8%, 5.7% and 5.2%.
- Population-weighted mean ICER US$11 per DALY averted across the 43 countries, range US$2 (Liberia) to US$48 (Botswana, 95% CI US$15-925). Under US$20 per DALY in 37 of 43 countries.
- Uganda specifically: antenatal syphilis prevalence taken as 3.0% (a general-adult-population figure, no antenatal estimate was available), 1,514,000 live births, 94% antenatal care coverage, hourly nurse wage US$1.22. ICER US$7 per DALY averted (95% CI US$4-23), with up to 3,759 stillbirths, 1,446 neonatal deaths and 1,893 congenital syphilis cases averted per year at an incremental cost of US$1.13 million.
- Continental totals for moving from current to universal screening: 23.5 million women reached, up to 64,000 stillbirths, 25,000 neonatal deaths and 32,000 congenital syphilis cases averted, 2.6 million DALYs, at US$20.8 million per year.
- Screening stays highly cost-effective down to a mean prevalence of 0.038%, 82-fold below the 3.1% population-weighted current rate. Cost-effectiveness improves with prevalence, because screening cost is essentially fixed per woman while benefit scales with the number infected.
- Baseline antenatal syphilis screening coverage was reported for 23 of 43 countries, weighted average 40.7%, ranging from 0% (Comoros, Eritrea, Sierra Leone) to 93.8% (Namibia). The other 20 countries were assigned 40.7%.

## Relevance to project

The unit-cost source for a rapid syphilis test and its treatment in this setting: US$0.74 per test, US$1.92 per penicillin dose, and 17.5 min of nurse time per test, all in 2011 dollars. Those are the numbers to inflate if the POC arm is ever costed, and the nurse-time figure is the one that dominates in higher-wage countries.

It also gives the treponemal rapid test's field accuracy (86% / 99%) as a prior for the syphilis component of a point-of-care panel, with the caveat that a treponemal test detects past infection as well as current, which this analysis names as a source of overtreatment in repeat pregnancies.

The design is a decision tree over a single pregnancy, not a transmission model, so it supplies nothing on onward syphilis transmission, no HIV, and no dynamics. The screening door is antenatal care, which is not the symptomatic care-seeking door the POC arm uses, so the coverage and uptake assumptions here do not transfer. Use it as a costing and test-performance reference and a comparator for the maternal-outcome side of the benefit, not as a model structure.

## Limitations

- Benefits accrue to the infant only. Maternal morbidity, onward transmission to partners, miscarriage and low birth weight are all excluded, which the authors state biases against the intervention.
- Assumes no loss to follow-up and full completion of all three penicillin doses, which biases in favour.
- Antenatal syphilis prevalence was unavailable for five countries, including Uganda, where a general-population figure was substituted. Several country estimates come from WHO surveillance updates published in 2002 or 2007, carried into a 2013 analysis.
- Lifetime treatment costs of congenital syphilis were not modelled, nor were training, outreach and shipping costs for scale-up.
- A treponemal test stays positive after cure, so in high-fertility settings it will retreat previously treated women. The authors offset this against likely reinfection rather than quantifying it.
- Effectiveness of penicillin comes from a meta-analysis of observational and interventional studies, with wide intervals (stillbirth RR 42%, 95% CI 16-91%). The one-way sensitivity analysis pushing stillbirth effectiveness down to 7% raises the upper-end ICER to US$111 per DALY, still under most thresholds.
