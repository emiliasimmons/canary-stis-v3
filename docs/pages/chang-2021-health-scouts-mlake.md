Source: [chang-2021](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pmed.1003475)

A pragmatic cluster-randomized trial (mLAKE, NCT02556957) of "Health Scouts", community
health workers delivering household counseling supported by a smartphone app, in one
fishing community of roughly 4,000-5,000 adolescents and adults on Lake Victoria in Rakai
District, Uganda. Forty contiguous clusters, 20 intervention and 20 control, with the
intervention running September 2015 to December 2018. Outcomes were measured through three
cross-sectional Rakai Community Cohort Study (RCCS) survey rounds nested in the trial:
baseline (n = 2,148, mid-May 2015), mid-study (n = 2,533, mid-January 2017), and end-study
(n = 1,903, early October 2018), all among residents aged 15-49.

## Relevance to project

Context and a calibration reference for the high-prevalence fishing-community stratum of
Rakai, the tail of the risk structure the model has to reproduce if it is to represent
the population where an STI-driven HIV cofactor effect would be largest. Nothing here is
about STIs, so it supplies no STI target and no diagnostic parameter.

What it does supply, all among 15-49-year-old residents of one fishing community:

- HIV prevalence roughly 38% at baseline (400/1054 intervention, 386/1094 control), and
  36.0% and 35.7% among mid- and end-study survey participants.
- Cascade levels and their movement over three years. Among HIV-positive participants,
  ART coverage 67.8%/67.1% at baseline rising to 92.3% (intervention) and 87.5% (control)
  at end-study; viral suppression (<400 copies/mL) 67.5%/64.2% at baseline rising to 88.8%
  and 84.6%. HIV care coverage 77.3%/75.6% at baseline, 93.4% and 88.2% at end-study.
- HIV testing coverage near saturation: 94.4-95.8% at mid-study, 97.8-97.9% at end-study,
  among all survey participants.
- HIV incidence over the end-study interval: 17 cases in 1,188 person-years (1.43 per 100
  py) intervention versus 12 in 1,353 py (0.89 per 100 py) control, IRR 1.19 (95% CI 0.46
  to 3.09). The confidence interval spans a factor of six, so treat this as an order-of-
  magnitude bound on incidence in a hyperendemic fishing community rather than a target.
- Consistent condom use, among sexually active participants: 20.5% intervention and 27.3%
  control at mid-study, 19.7% and 20.2% at end-study. This bears on the SOC-arm behavioural
  backdrop for STI transmission, which the model does not vary by arm.
- Male circumcision coverage among men: 56.3%/60.5% at baseline, 68.9%/65.8% at end-study.
- Occupational composition, which is what makes this stratum distinct: 28.8%/27.7% fishing,
  10.6%/10.0% bar or restaurant work, 21.6%/20.4% trade or shopkeeping.

The care-seeking side is the second use. The trial is a direct measurement of how much a
well-resourced outreach effort moves engagement when coverage is already high: about a 5
percentage point absolute gain in ART coverage (PRR 1.05, 95% CI 1.01 to 1.10), with viral
suppression not significantly changed (PRR 1.04, 95% CI 0.98 to 1.12). That sets a ceiling
on what a demand-side intervention alone buys at this point on the cascade, and it is a
useful comparator for the POC arm, whose gain has to come from the etiological test at the
symptomatic care-seeking door rather than from pulling more people through that door.

## Key points

Intervention reach was moderate and contamination substantial. By end-study, 38% of
intervention-arm residents reported ever having been counseled by a Health Scout, against
23% in the control arm. Since Health Scouts lived in the community, only about 30% overall
community reach was achieved against a 50% theoretical maximum. Self-reported exposure was
higher among people living with HIV (42%) than not (24%), and higher among women (35%)
than men (27%), so uptake was already selected toward those with a reason to engage.

No outcome differed between arms at the mid-study survey in the unadjusted intention-to-
treat analysis; the care and ART coverage effects appeared only at end-study, about three
years in. The age- and sex-adjusted analysis did find significant care and ART coverage
gains at mid-study.

Population turnover was heavy: large numbers moved in and out of the community between
surveys, with smaller flows between intervention and control clusters. Survey response
among eligible residents present was 99.4%, 99.0%, and 99.5% across the three rounds, so
the losses are migration rather than refusal.

## Limitations

Contamination and mobility both bias toward the null, which the authors read as the true
Health Scouts effect being larger than measured. For this project the direction matters
less than the magnitude: even an inflated estimate is small.

Every outcome except viral suppression is self-reported, including exposure to the
intervention. Self-reported ART use and male circumcision have been validated in this
setting by the same group, but linkage to care and condom use have not.

Cluster assignment used handheld GPS on household coordinates, and some cluster boundaries
physically moved during the study through shoreline erosion and building work, so some
misclassification is certain.

The trial was underpowered for its own outcomes: minimum detectable prevalence risk ratios
at 80% power were 15% for HIV care coverage, 20% for ART coverage, 41% for viral
suppression, and 23% for male circumcision, against observed differences of about 5%. The
HIV incidence comparison is far weaker still, resting on 29 seroconversions in total.

Single community, and the most extreme one in Rakai. Nothing here transfers to the agrarian
and trading communities that make up most of the RCCS.
