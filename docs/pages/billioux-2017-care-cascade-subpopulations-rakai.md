Source: [billioux-2017](https://doi.org/10.7448/IAS.20.1.21590)

Cross-sectional analysis linking Rakai Community Cohort Study survey and surveillance records to the Rakai Health Sciences Program clinical database (OpenMRS, covering 19 government clinics) for the 3,666 HIV-positive participants identified between September 2013 and December 2015. The population spans 41 agrarian, trading and fishing communities in Rakai District. People who died or out-migrated before December 2015 were removed from the denominator.

## Relevance to project

This is the calibration target for the HIV treatment cascade at the Rakai site around 2015, and the only one of the Rakai sources here that gives cascade levels with sub-population breakdowns and denominators. The model's HIV care module should land near 98% diagnosed, 74% enrolled in care and 63% on ART out of all HIV-positive adults 15-49, with roughly 58% of the whole HIV-positive population virally suppressed. On the UNAIDS conditional framing the same data read 98-65-92.

It is a structural input for who reaches care. The gradients are large and consistent across every cascade stage: men, people aged 15-24, the never-married, and recent in-migrants all fall behind, and the gaps persist even in fishing communities where test-and-treat removed the CD4 eligibility barrier. That is direct evidence that access to care in this population is governed by who a person is rather than by whether they are clinically eligible, and it argues for making the model's care-seeking probability depend on sex, age and residence stability rather than treating it as uniform.

The care measured is HIV enrolment and ART initiation. It does not measure symptomatic STI presentation, so it cannot set the rate at which agents with urethral or vaginal discharge arrive at the clinic door where the point-of-care panel would replace syndromic management. It does establish that a working, free, geographically close clinic network existed in Rakai in this period, with every RCCS community within an hour's walk and most within half an hour, which is the service backdrop an STI intervention would be layered onto. Note that pre-ART care in these clinics already included treatment of sexually transmitted infections, so some STI treatment is reaching HIV-positive people through this route.

No STI prevalence, incidence or diagnostic performance figure comes from this source.

## Key points

Cascade for all 3,666 HIV-positive participants, denominator the full HIV-positive population: 97.6% (3577/3666) aware of status, of which 92% received counselling and testing through the RCCS and 5% self-reported having learned their result elsewhere; 74.3% (2729/3666) enrolled in HIV care, defined as at least one clinic visit or self-reported cotrimoxazole or ART; 62.9% (2312/3666) initiated ART; 57.7% (2124/3666) estimated virally suppressed.

Viral suppression was measured directly in only 366 people, those with a viral load 12 months after ART initiation, of whom 92% (336/366) were below 1000 copies per millilitre. The 57.7% population figure is that proportion applied to everyone on ART. Inverse probability weighting for who had a viral load available gave 57%, essentially unchanged.

By sex, on the full-population denominator: women 97.7% aware, 77.3% in care, 67.7% on ART, 63.2% suppressed (n=2308); men 97.4%, 69.6%, 55.2%, 49.0% (n=1358). Awareness does not differ by sex (p=0.658), every later stage does (p<0.001). The conditional targets are 98-69-93 for women and 97-57-89 for men.

By age, in-care and on-ART proportions: 15-24 years 54.8% and 45.8% (n=542); 25-29 64.9% and 53.0% (n=776); 30-39 80.7% and 69.5% (n=1611); 40+ 85.2% and 72.5% (n=737).

By residence: long-term residents 79.4% in care and 66.9% on ART (n=2888); in-migrants 55.9% and 47.7% (n=778).

Adjusted prevalence risk ratios for enrolment in care, reference female, aged 30-39, married, long-term resident: men 0.84 (0.77-0.91); aged 15-24 0.72 (0.63-0.82); never married 0.84 (0.71-0.99); in-migrant 0.75 (0.67-0.83). For ART initiation: men 0.75 (0.69-0.82); aged 15-24 0.69 (0.60-0.80); never married 0.80 (0.66-0.95); in-migrant 0.76 (0.67-0.85).

Community type made no difference to enrolment or ART initiation once adjusted. Fishing communities, which had test-and-treat, gave adjusted PRR 1.08 (0.98-1.18) for enrolment and 1.06 (0.96-1.17) for ART against agrarian. Among those already in care, 85% (2312/2729) had started ART, 83% in fishing communities against 86% agrarian and 87% trading.

Eligibility was not the main brake beyond the enrolment step. Among the 1,288 with a CD4 measurement, 96% (1143/1186) of those eligible under the criteria of the time were on ART. The ART criterion was CD4 at or below 500 cells/mm³ from January 2014, raised from 350, with test-and-treat for most-at-risk populations in fishing communities.

Sample composition, for reading the cascade back onto a modelled population: 63% female, median age 33 (IQR 27-38), 59% married, 21% in-migrant, and by community type 47.5% fishing, 28.6% agrarian, 23.9% trading. HIV prevalence in the RCCS communities is cited at roughly 42% in fishing, 14% in trading and 12% in agrarian.

## Limitations

The heavy fishing-community weight is the main caution. Nearly half the HIV-positive sample lives in fishing communities, which are a small share of the RCCS population overall but carry very high prevalence. A model calibrated on agrarian and trading communities alone is not looking at the same denominator, and the cascade should be compared community-type by community-type where possible.

Viral suppression rests on 366 measurements out of 1,850 people on ART, 20%, missing because of delays returning results from the central national laboratory. The extrapolation to 58% assumes those 366 are representative; the weighted analysis suggests they roughly are, but the estimate is imputed and should not be treated as measured.

Enrolment and ART status combine clinic records with self-report, and care sought at facilities outside the RHSP-supported network is not captured. Self-reported ART in this population has 99% specificity and about 76% sensitivity.

Whether the RCCS cascade generalises to all HIV-positive people in Rakai District is untested, though RCCS behaviour distributions track the Uganda DHS and national serosurvey.

## Methodology

The cascade has four stages: awareness (RCCS counselling result received, or self-reported prior result), enrolment (at least one clinic visit, or self-reported cotrimoxazole or ART), ART initiation (clinically confirmed date, or self-report), and suppression (at or below 1000 copies per millilitre 12 months post-initiation). Linkage between the survey and clinical systems uses the laboratory identification number from RHSP-supported clinics, so a person treated elsewhere appears untreated unless they self-report.

Two denominators are in play and the paper reports both. The 98-74-63-58 sequence is on the full HIV-positive population at every stage; the 98-65-92 UNAIDS reading is conditional at each step. Comparing a model against the wrong one shifts the ART number by two points and the suppression number by 34.

Risk ratios come from modified Poisson regression adjusted for age, sex, marital status and migration status. The period is defined by a programme change: September 2013 is when RHSP handed direct care delivery to Ministry of Health-led services, so the cascade is measured across a service transition, and January 2014 moved the CD4 threshold from 350 to 500 mid-window.
