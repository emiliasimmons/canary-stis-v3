Development and validation of a 9-item perceived-risk instrument, the HIV Salience and
Perception (HPS) scale, administered at enrollment and at 1, 3 and 6 months to 499
adolescent girls and young women aged 16-25 in a PrEP cohort study in Kampala, Uganda.
PrEP dispensing was recorded quarterly and adherence measured by Wisepill electronic
bottle openings. Cataloged from the abstract; no full text on this machine.

## Relevance to project

Supports the behavioural side of PrEP uptake rather than any epidemiological quantity.
The usable content is the split it demonstrates: a one-point increase in perceived risk
raised PrEP dispensing by 7% (adjusted RR 1.07, 95% CI 1.01-1.13) with no detectable
effect on adherence, so initiation and persistence respond to different drivers in this
population. If the model treats PrEP coverage among AGYW as a single background parameter,
this is a reason to separate initiation from continuation rather than a source for either
level. It supplies no PrEP coverage fraction, no adherence proportion, and nothing for the
STI side.

## Key points

- 499 AGYW, Kampala; 54.1% aged 20 or older, full range 16-25.
- Median HPS score 18 on a 9-36 scale (observed range 8-33), Cronbach's alpha 0.77.
- Higher score associated with PrEP dispensing overall (aRR 1.07 per point, 95% CI
  1.01-1.13) and more strongly among those aged 20 and over (aRR 1.10, 95% CI 1.03-1.19).
- No association between score and high adherence, defined as at least 80% of expected
  daily pill bottle openings.
- The authors' motivation was that single-item risk questions are stigmatising and poor
  predictors of PrEP use.

## Limitations

Written from the abstract alone, so the item wording, the dispensing denominators, and the
observed adherence distribution are all unavailable. Urban Kampala AGYW in an existing
cohort are not the Rakai population the model calibrates to, and cohort participants
receive more PrEP contact than a routine programme would provide. Six months of follow-up
cannot speak to longer-term continuation. The associations are observational and the
scale was developed and evaluated in the same sample.
