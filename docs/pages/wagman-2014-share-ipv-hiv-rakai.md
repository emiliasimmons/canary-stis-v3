Source: [wagman-2014](https://doi.org/10.1016/s2214-109x(14)70344-4)

Comparative trial of the Safe Homes and Respect for Everyone (SHARE) intimate partner
violence intervention nested in the Rakai Community Cohort Study, using cluster
assignments inherited from an earlier family-planning trial. 11,448 residents aged 15-49
were enrolled between February 2005 and June 2006, 5,337 in four intervention clusters and
6,111 in seven control clusters, with follow-up surveys at roughly 16 and 35 months.

## Relevance to project

Two uses, and the intervention effect is the smaller one.

The trial gives a dated HIV incidence level for the RCCS population in a defined calendar
window, sex-disaggregated, with person-years attached: 1.15 per 100 person-years overall
in control clusters over 10,390 person-years (women 1.15 over 6,154 py; men 1.13 over
4,237 py), against 0.87 per 100 py in intervention clusters over 9,510 py. Those control
figures are usable as a Rakai HIV incidence target for 2005-2009 among adults aged 15-49
who attended both follow-ups, which is a retained subset rather than the whole enrolled
cohort. The paper also states the contemporaneous district figures the authors calibrated
their power calculation to: HIV prevalence 12%, HIV incidence 1.2 per 100 person-years,
past-year IPV against women 29%.

Separately, it documents what routine care in Rakai consisted of during this period, and
that description is the SOC arm. Rakai Health Sciences Program services in both study
groups comprised free condoms, syndromic STI treatment, general medical care, PMTCT, HIV
education, and ART initiation at WHO stage IV or CD4 below 250 cells per microlitre. A
model comparator arm running syndromic management alongside partial ART coverage matches
what this population actually received.

It supplies nothing on STI incidence, prevalence, or treatment outcomes, and the IPV
mechanism is outside the model's structure. The HIV incidence reduction (adjusted IRR
0.67, 95% CI 0.46-0.97) should not be carried into the model as an effect size, since it
comes from four clusters against seven with baseline imbalance.

## Key points

- Adjusted HIV incidence rate ratio 0.67 (95% CI 0.46-0.97, p=0.0362) overall, 0.59
  (0.35-0.95) in men, 0.72 (0.49-1.07) in women, at about 35 months.
- Past-year physical IPV against women at follow-up 2: 346/2,127 (16%) control vs 217/1,812
  (12%) intervention, adjusted PRR 0.79 (0.67-0.92). Sexual IPV 261/2,038 (13%) vs
  167/1,737 (10%), aPRR 0.80 (0.67-0.97). Emotional IPV did not differ.
- Male-reported perpetration showed no effect at follow-up 2, and the authors attribute the
  gap between women's victimisation reports and men's perpetration reports to
  under-reporting by men rather than to measurement of different events.
- Baseline partner counts differ sharply by sex: 47% of control-group men and 6% of
  control-group women reported more than one sexual partner in the past year; 15% of men
  reported two or more non-marital partners against 2% of women.
- Baseline condom use was 9% always among women and 16% among men, with 91% and 85%
  respectively reporting none or inconsistent use.
- Baseline HIV prevalence 14% in control women and 11% in control men; baseline ART uptake
  among HIV-positive participants was 11% in control women and 7% in control men, rising to
  34% and 24% by final follow-up.
- The RCCS itself: censuses about 28,000 residents in roughly 9,000 households across about
  50 communities, roughly 90% consent to interview, about 98% give blood, about 80% request
  their results. Retention was 75% at either follow-up visit and 57% at follow-up 2.
- The incidence difference between groups disappeared in the post-intervention interval
  (2010-11).

## Limitations

Clusters were inherited from a family-planning trial rather than randomised for this
question, and funding allowed only four intervention clusters against seven controls.
Baseline groups differed in age, education, HIV prevalence, IPV, and disclosure, so all
effects are adjusted rather than randomised contrasts. Retention was 75% and differed by
group, with higher-risk participants more likely to drop out and slightly more so in the
intervention arm, which could produce part of the incidence difference on its own. The
trial period overlaps both the national ART rollout from 2004 and the tail of the Rakai
male circumcision trial, so the incidence levels are moving targets rather than a steady
state. No adjustment for multiple comparisons. IPV frequency and severity were not
recorded.

## Methodology

Intention-to-treat analysis by assigned cluster. HIV status by two enzyme immunoassays
with Western blot confirmation of discordant results and all seroconversions; person-years
counted to the last negative test, or to the midpoint between last negative and first
positive for seroconverters. Modified Poisson regression with robust variance for
prevalence risk ratios and incidence rate ratios, adjusted for baseline HIV prevalence by
group, age, education, marital status, and circumcision status. Generalised linear models
were used in preference to random-intercept models because within-cluster correlation was
near zero and only 11 clusters existed.
