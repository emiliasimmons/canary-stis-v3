Source: [edwards-2022](https://doi.org/10.1177/09564624221121208)

Mixed-methods implementation evaluation of a community health worker programme delivering
PrEP counselling in a hyperendemic Ugandan fishing community, with the quantitative arm
surveying all community members aged 15-49 through the Rakai Community Cohort Study and 74
qualitative interviews across clients, health workers, programme staff, community leaders,
and clinic staff.

## Relevance to project

Bounds how far a demand-side intervention moves background PrEP coverage in the RCCS
footprint. Exposure to the counselling intervention was associated with a prevalence risk
ratio of 1.86 (95% CI 1.22-2.82) for current PrEP use among 1,176 respondents, which sets
the order of magnitude for a plausible PrEP coverage uplift in a fishing community and
therefore for the strength of the competing prevention exposure the POC arm is measured
against.

The counselling channel is also a structural analogue for the POC arm's delivery
assumption: both reach people at an existing community or clinic contact point rather than
through active outreach screening, and the intervention's reported dependence on
counsellor skill is a caution against assuming full uptake at the diagnostic door.

Not a source for a PrEP coverage level: the paper reports a ratio, not an absolute
proportion on PrEP, and the population is a single fishing community, which sits well above
the inland agrarian communities in HIV risk.

## Key points

Counselling was structured on the situated Information, Motivation, and Behavioral Skills
model and delivered by community health workers using motivational interviewing.

Both PrEP knowledge and current PrEP use were higher among clients reached by the
intervention. Qualitative accounts attributed the gain to the counselling itself and to
counsellor interviewing skill rather than to supply-side changes.

Process data from a mobile application showed the PrEP counselling module was delivered
consistently over the implementation period, so the effect is not an artefact of partial
rollout.

## Limitations

Written from the abstract alone; no PDF was available on this machine, and the abstract's
results paragraph is truncated in the source record, so the knowledge-outcome effect size,
the absolute PrEP use proportions, and the adjustment set are not recorded here.

Comparison is between clients reached and not reached by community health workers within
the same community rather than randomised, so selection on motivation to use PrEP is
uncontrolled and the prevalence risk ratio is an upper estimate of the intervention effect.

One fishing community, so the result does not transfer to agrarian RCCS communities without
adjustment.
