Observational study in Lancet HIV using Rakai Community Cohort Study data collected August
2011 to May 2013. All 40 surveyed communities were classified as agrarian (27), trading (9)
or lakeside fishing (4), HIV prevalence was mapped with Bayesian methods, and risk factors,
ART coverage and male circumcision coverage were compared across the three community types.
No PDF is held on this machine; this page is written from the abstract.

## Relevance to project

Calibration targets stratified by community type at the project's first site, and a direct
argument that a single pooled Rakai prevalence target would misrepresent the population the
model is fitting.

The numbers: 17,119 participants aged 15-49, 9,215 (54%) female, 9,931 in agrarian, 3,318
in trading and 3,870 in fishing communities. HIV prevalence ranged from 9% to 43% across
the 40 communities. Median prevalence by type was 41% in fishing (range 37-43%), 17% in
trading (11-22%) and 14% in agrarian (9-26%). ART coverage and male circumcision coverage
were significantly lower in fishing communities, and self-reported risk behaviour was
higher in fishing communities and higher in men than women.

Two uses. As targets, the three community-type prevalences with their ranges are directly
fittable if the model represents community structure, and the 9-43% spread bounds how much
between-community variance the network layer has to generate. As a design constraint, the
fishing communities pair the highest prevalence with the lowest treatment and prevention
coverage, so a model that assumes coverage is uniform across the risk gradient will
understate the burden concentrated at the top of it.

The overlap between the highest-prevalence communities and the lowest service coverage also
bears on the prevention-targeting pathway the project wants to separate, since it is where
a diagnostic that identifies high-transmission network positions would have the most room
to act.

The paper supplies no STI prevalence and no data on symptomatic care-seeking or syndromic
management.

## Key points

The 4 fishing communities hold 3,870 of 17,119 participants, so they are a large minority
of the cohort and not a marginal stratum.

Variation within community type is substantial: agrarian communities range 9-26%, which
overlaps the trading range of 11-22%. Community type is a coarse summary of a continuous
gradient, and the authors report variability both across and within classifications.

The authors' conclusion is that local surveillance is needed and that fishing communities
should be prioritised for combination prevention, on the grounds of high burden, high
reported risk behaviour and low coverage together.

## Limitations

Written from the abstract alone. The age- and sex-stratified prevalence figures, the
confidence intervals, the definitions of the risk behaviour measures and the exact ART and
circumcision coverage values are not available here, and a target set for calibration would
need them from the paper or from RCCS directly.

Cross-sectional over one survey round (August 2011 to May 2013), so it fixes a level and not
a trend, and it predates the large ART scale-up that followed.

Risk behaviour is self-reported, and the male-female difference the authors report is
exactly the direction reporting bias runs.

Community classification is a three-way categorisation applied by the investigators, with
only 4 fishing communities carrying the headline result.
