Cohort study of HIV incidence among 6741 sexually experienced youth aged 15-24 enrolled in
the Rakai Community Cohort Study from 1999 to 2008. Examines behavioral (number of
partners, condom use, alcohol), biological (circumcision, genital ulcer disease), and
demographic (age, gender, marital status, education) risk and protective factors for
incident HIV infection, with attention to the role of gender and social transitions
(marriage, schooling) in shaping risk.

## Relevance to project

Provides age- and sex-specific HIV incidence estimates in Rakai youth over a decade
spanning the model's historical calibration window. Usable as:

- **Calibration target**: age-stratified HIV incidence rates among 15-24 year-olds in the
  RCCS, the model's primary calibration site.
- **Risk-factor structure validation**: the relative importance of behavioral, biological,
  and demographic factors can be compared against the model's predicted risk gradients to
  check whether the network and cofactor structure reproduces observed patterns.
- **Gender-transition effects**: quantifies how marriage and school-leaving shift HIV risk,
  relevant to the model's age-structured entry into sexual activity and partnership
  formation.

The study does not distinguish STI-specific cofactor effects from the generic "genital
ulcer disease" category, so its biological risk factors are not directly usable for
pathogen-specific cofactor calibration.

## Key points

- Large sample (n = 6741) with longitudinal follow-up across multiple RCCS rounds,
  providing stable incidence estimates by age and sex.
- Separates incident from prevalent infections, avoiding the survival bias in
  cross-sectional prevalence data.
- Identifies genital ulcer disease, multiple partners, and lack of circumcision as
  significant risk factors, consistent with the cofactor and network mechanisms the model
  represents.
- Documents protective effects of education and later sexual debut.

## Limitations

- "Genital ulcer disease" aggregates syphilis, HSV-2, and chancroid without
  pathogen-specific attribution.
- Covers 1999-2008, ending before the scale-up of VMMC and universal ART that changed
  Rakai's HIV trajectory.
- Self-reported behavioral measures are subject to social-desirability bias, particularly
  for condom use and partner counts.

## Methodology

Open population-based cohort (RCCS). HIV status determined by paired sera at consecutive
survey visits (roughly annual). Poisson regression for incidence rate ratios adjusting for
demographic, behavioral, and biological covariates. Gender-stratified models.
