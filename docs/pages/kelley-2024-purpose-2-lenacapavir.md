Phase 3, double-blind, randomized, active-controlled trial of subcutaneous lenacapavir (927 mg every 26 weeks) against daily oral emtricitabine-tenofovir disoproxil fumarate (F/TDF), in 3271 cisgender gay, bisexual and other men, transgender women, transgender men and gender-nonbinary people aged 16 and over having condomless receptive anal sex with partners assigned male at birth. 92 sites across the United States, Brazil, Peru, Thailand, South Africa, Argentina and Mexico; screening from June 2021, randomization completed December 2023, stopped early at the interim analysis in September 2024. Funded by Gilead Sciences. Extracted text in `raw/10.1056/nejmoa2411858`.

There was no placebo arm. The comparator for the primary analysis was a counterfactual background incidence estimated cross-sectionally from the 4634 screened participants with an HIV test result, using a recency assay (LAg-EIA avidity) and a recent infection testing algorithm.

## Relevance to project

An efficacy bound and a comparator arm, not a calibration target. The population is receptive-anal-sex MSM and gender-diverse people across seven countries, so nothing here transfers directly to the Rakai heterosexual cohort. Two uses:

Lenacapavir efficacy as an upper bound on what a biomedical prevention product can do: 2 infections in 2179 participants, 0.10 per 100 person-years (95% CI 0.01 to 0.37), against a background of 2.37 per 100 person-years (95% CI 1.65 to 3.42) in the screened population, an incidence rate ratio of 0.04 (95% CI 0.01 to 0.18). Against the F/TDF arm at 0.93 per 100 person-years (95% CI 0.43 to 1.77) the rate ratio was 0.11 (95% CI 0.02 to 0.51). If the project ever compares POC diagnostics against a long-acting PrEP counterfactual, this is the efficacy number, with the caveat that it is a per-protocol-adherence figure in a trial with 91-93% on-time injection.

STI burden under near-complete HIV protection. Baseline laboratory-diagnosed STI in the randomized population: chlamydia 11.6% in both arms, gonorrhoea 8.8% (lenacapavir) and 10.6% (F/TDF), syphilis 3.8% and 4.0%. Over follow-up, 36.8% (771/2096) and 34.2% (354/1036) had a laboratory-diagnosed STI, with rectal chlamydia in 13.2% and oropharyngeal gonorrhoea in 13.0% of the lenacapavir arm. This is a cohort in which HIV acquisition was nearly eliminated while bacterial STI transmission continued at a high rate. It is a counterexample to any modeling assumption that ties STI incidence to HIV incidence through shared exposure alone, and it bears on the project's decomposition: the direct-STI-treatment channel and the HIV-prevention channel come apart under a strong biomedical intervention. Anatomic-site-specific testing (pharyngeal, rectal, urine) also means these prevalences are not comparable to the genital-only sampling behind most Rakai figures.

## Key points

- Adherence to F/TDF fell over time: tenofovir diphosphate concentrations consistent with four or more tablets per week in 82% at week 8, 67% at week 26, 62% at week 52. All nine F/TDF-arm infections had low, unquantifiable, or discontinued drug exposure. The measured superiority of lenacapavir over F/TDF is therefore mostly an adherence effect, not a pharmacological one.
- Both lenacapavir breakthrough infections carried the N74D capsid resistance mutation at diagnosis, with drug concentrations inside the pharmacokinetic cohort's range. Neither showed delayed seroconversion or delayed diagnosis on standard fourth-generation testing, unlike what has been reported for cabotegravir.
- Injection-site reactions in 83.2% of the lenacapavir arm versus 69.5% of placebo injections, mostly grade 1-2; subcutaneous nodules 63.4% versus 39.2%, median nodule duration 183 days (IQR 89-274). 1.2% (26/2183) discontinued for injection-site reactions against 0.3% (3/1088). This is the adherence-relevant tolerability cost of a six-monthly injectable.
- 8.2% (378/4634) of those screened were HIV-positive at screening, of whom 11.9% (45/378) were classified as recent infections. High prevalence and high incidence in the same sampling frame.

## Limitations

- No placebo arm. The counterfactual background incidence is a cross-sectional recency-assay estimate with no longitudinal follow-up, and the authors note it may underestimate prospectively observed incidence, which would make the efficacy estimate conservative.
- The eligibility criteria for the incidence cohort (no HIV test and no PrEP in the preceding three months) select a group plausibly different from the randomized cohort, though the authors report similar behavioral characteristics.
- Two events in the lenacapavir arm. The efficacy confidence interval is wide at the lower end and the point estimate should not be read as a precise number.
- Median follow-up is short: retention denominators fall from 3001 at week 26 to 69 at week 104, so nothing here speaks to multi-year durability, resistance accumulation, or STI incidence beyond about a year on product.
- Sponsor-run: Gilead collected the data and performed the statistical analyses.
