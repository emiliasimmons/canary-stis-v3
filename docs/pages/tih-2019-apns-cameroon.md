A programme evaluation, not a trial, of assisted partner notification services (aPNS) run by Cameroon Baptist Convention Health Services across 16 CBCHS and 22 non-CBCHS facilities in five of Cameroon's ten regions, 2007 to 2015. Health advisors interviewed HIV-positive index persons, elicited sexual contacts, and notified and tested those contacts by provider referral, negotiated referral, or patient referral. Extracted text in `raw/10.1002/jia2.25310`.

## Relevance to project

The model implements no partner-notification pathway, so this is a comparator that bounds what the omitted arm would deliver, and a source of the cascade fractions if one is ever added. The operative numbers are per-step conditional probabilities on a real programme rather than a trial: contacts named per index person 1.1 (annual range 1.1 to 1.3 over nine years), 61% of named contacts notified, 71% of notified contacts tested, 52% of tested contacts HIV positive. That composes to 4764 HIV-positive contacts from 18,730 index persons, a number-needed-to-interview of 3.9 (annual range 2.7 in 2010 to 5.5 in 2015). Only 20% of the 784 contacts tested in the 2014 to 2015 sub-analysis were newly diagnosed; 21.6% already knew they were positive. Case-finding yield and new-diagnosis yield differ by roughly a factor of two here, which is the figure to use if PN is ever compared against the POC test-and-treat arm on incident diagnoses.

Two further uses. The programme reaches men: 68% of index persons were women, 67% of contacts were men, against a national adult prevalence of 3.7%, so PN concentrates testing on a group that does not otherwise present. That is direct evidence for the asymmetric care-seeking the model encodes at the symptomatic door, though it is HIV testing rather than STI symptom presentation. And the harm rates give an upper bound on the cost side of a PN arm: 6.3% of 976 followed-up index persons reported any adverse outcome, all partnership dissolution; 1.1% reported physical intimate partner violence after diagnosis, of which 3 of 11 attributed it to the health advisor's notification.

Not usable as a calibration target. It carries no incidence, no denominators for the general population, and no STI outcomes at all.

## Key points

The 51.8% HIV prevalence among tested contacts is 14 times national adult prevalence (3.7%, CAMPHIA 2017, ages 15 to 64), the paper's central argument for PN as a targeting mechanism. It also means the contact pool is heavily serodiscordant-partnership-derived rather than a broad risk sweep.

Notification method split roughly evenly between patient referral (43.7%) and provider referral (44.3%) in the 2014 to 2015 subset, with negotiated referral at 5.1%. The authors report provider referral yielded better results and the programme promoted it, but give no comparative yield by method.

Referral of HIV-positive contacts to care was 66% overall and reached effectively 100% from 2012 onward, an artefact of ART access expanding nationally rather than of programme design. ART initiation among the 326 HIV-positive contacts in the sub-analysis was 24.5%, with 62.9% missing, so the treatment end of the cascade is not measured here.

Contacts named per index (1.1) is below the Kenya cluster-randomised trial (1.67) and near the Mozambique pilot (1.4). The authors attribute the gap to real-world versus trial conditions and to faith-based facilities possibly discouraging disclosure of contacts. A previous CBCHS report gave a median of four lifetime partners and one partner in the last three years.

## Limitations

Aggregate monthly facility reports drive the nine-year numbers, so nothing can be disaggregated by age, sex, or site beyond what Table 1 holds. The 2014 to 2015 sub-analysis covers 1261 of 5223 index persons who received aPNS in that window, and the follow-up subset is 976, so the harm rates rest on whoever's forms reached the central office. Missingness in the care-outcome fields runs 25% for index persons and 49 to 63% for contacts.

Not a controlled comparison. There is no passive-referral counterfactual and no measure of what testing would have happened absent the programme, so the 3.9 NNTI is a programme yield, not an incremental one. Funding constraints depressed notification and follow-up over the period, which the authors name as the reason notified share fell to 47% by 2015.
