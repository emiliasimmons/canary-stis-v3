A PRISMA systematic review (PROSPERO CRD42018089505) of how the UNAIDS 90-90-90 cascade
varies by sociodemographic group among adults aged 15 and over living with HIV in
sub-Saharan Africa. Embase and MEDLINE plus prespecified grey literature (PHIA, DHS, AIS,
ALPHA, WHO/UNAIDS/MSF reports, conference abstracts) published 2014 to August 2018, with at
least half the study period falling in the universal-test-and-treat era. 3533 titles
screened, 92 studies extracted (50 peer-reviewed, 42 grey). No pooled measure of
association was estimated: the headline numbers are unweighted medians of study-level
prevalences. Extracted text and tables sit under `raw/10.1002/jia2.25470`.

The four outcomes are defined by their denominators. Awareness of HIV-positive status
(first 90) over all people living with HIV; ART use (second 90) over those aware; viral
suppression (third 90) over those on ART; and population-wide viral suppression over all
people living with HIV. Nine viral load cutoffs appear across studies, most commonly
under 1000 copies/mL (63%).

## Relevance to project

This supplies the age and sex structure of the HIV care cascade for the model's treatment
pathway, as a prior on how far ART coverage and suppression differ between groups rather
than as a calibration target. Rakai-specific cascade numbers should come from RCCS
sources; this review sets the plausible spread around them. Uganda contributes 21 of the
92 studies, second only to South Africa.

The usable numbers, all unweighted medians of study estimates (awareness, ART use,
suppression, population-wide suppression, each in percent):

- ages 15-24: 60-48-81-62, from 17, 42, 17 and 16 studies respectively
- ages 25 and over: 70-63-91-76
- men: 66-72-85-56, from 34, 54, 30 and 28 studies
- women: 78-76-89-65

Within the twelve population-based national surveys (eleven PHIA plus the South African
national survey), population-wide viral suppression is higher for women than men in every
age band and rises with age within each sex. Men aged 15-24 sit at a median of 37%, women
15-24 at 49%, against the 73% level that corresponds to 90-90-90. Women aged 35 and over
and men aged 45-54 exceed it.

The negative result bears directly on model structure. Ten studies reported sexual risk
behaviour (condom use, transactional sex, concurrent partnerships) alongside cascade
outcomes, and none reported a measure of association between the two. There is therefore
no empirical basis here for making cascade progression a function of a risk group or
network position, which supports keeping ART coverage independent of risk group in the
model and varying it by age and sex only. The paper's own framing is that models have not
parameterized the cascade by risk, and that this may explain why trial estimates of the
population-level incidence effect of universal test and treat fell short of model
predictions.

Mobility is the one non-demographic gradient with consistent support: 3 or more months
away from the community versus none gave a prevalence ratio of 0.78 (95% CI 0.64 to 0.95)
for population-wide viral suppression in Botswana (n = 3596), and in South Africa
(n = 9812) under 1 month away versus 1 or more months gave an odds ratio of 1.81
(95% CI 1.13 to 2.91) for suppression among those on ART. If the model ever adds
mobility, that is the size of the effect on suppression.

## Key points

Study-level associations from the six general-population studies with measures of
association are directionally consistent for age and mixed for sex. Botswana (Gaolathe
2016, n = 3596, prevalence ratios): ages 16-19 versus 60+ gave 0.74 for awareness, 0.80
for ART use, 0.70 for suppression and 0.41 for population-wide suppression; men versus
women gave 0.90 for awareness but 1.04 for ART use. South Africa (Grobler 2017, n = 9812,
odds ratios): women versus men 2.12 for awareness and 2.11 for population-wide
suppression; ages 15-19 versus 45-49 gave 0.16 for awareness and 0.22 for population-wide
suppression, with a monotonic gradient across the intervening 5-year bands (0.23, 0.32,
0.51, 0.76, 0.94). Kenya and Uganda (Petersen 2017, n = 77,774, risk differences on
population-wide suppression): ages 15-24 versus 45+ at -9.2 percentage points (95% CI
-3.6 to -14.9), men versus women -2.6 (-0.7 to -4.4), never married versus married -5.9
(-2.7 to -9.1).

Sex direction for ART use is not settled: men were more likely to be on ART in Botswana
(PR 1.04, 1.01 to 1.07) and less likely in Zambia (HR 0.54, 0.31 to 0.93), with no
significant association in two South African studies.

Married or partnered people do better on most cascade steps in most settings, though
Botswana and South Africa disagree on the direction for awareness.

## Limitations

The medians are unweighted by sample size or sampling design, so they describe the
distribution of published study estimates and not the underlying populations. Reading
them as population values overstates the weight of small clinic-based studies: 45 of 92
studies used clinic or facility sampling against 21 population-based. The age and sex
medians in the abstract differ slightly from the body (awareness 49 versus 48 for ages
15-24, 79 versus 78 for women); the body's figures are used above.

Only 3 of 32 awareness studies, 6 of 53 ART use studies, 6 of 32 suppression studies and
3 of 23 population-wide suppression studies reported any measure of association, so the
effect sizes rest on a handful of settings each. Measures of association are unadjusted.
Nine different viral load cutoffs are pooled under one label. 58% of studies had some
confounding concern and a third had high risk of bias from selection. The search closed
in August 2018, so nothing after that is captured, and post-2018 cascade gains in this
region have been substantial enough that the level of these medians is dated even where
the age and sex gradient is not.
