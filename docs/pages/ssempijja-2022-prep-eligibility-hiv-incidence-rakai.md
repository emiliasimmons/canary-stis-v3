Cross-sectional and cohort analysis within the Rakai Community Cohort Study applying
Uganda's national PrEP eligibility tool to a general adult population rather than to
designated key populations. Eligibility required at least one of four self-reported risks
in the past 12 months: more than one partner of unknown HIV status, a nonmarital condomless
sex act, transactional sex, or genital ulcers.

## Relevance to project

Supplies a risk-stratified HIV incidence contrast for the model's first calibration site.
The eligible-versus-ineligible incidence rates (0.91 vs 0.41 per 100 person-years overall)
give a target for how much HIV incidence the model's risk structure should concentrate in
the higher-risk stratum, and a rough bound on the achievable gain from behaviourally
targeted prevention. The eligibility criteria themselves are a usable operational
definition of a targeting rule: three of the four items (partner number, condomless
nonmarital sex, transactional sex) are network and behaviour attributes the model carries,
and the fourth (genital ulcers) is an STI symptom, which makes this the closest available
analogue in RCCS to prevention targeting driven by STI presentation.

The 29% eligible fraction sets a plausible size for a targeted-prevention denominator in
the RCCS population aged 15-49.

Not a measure of PrEP effect: nobody in this analysis was on PrEP, so the incidence rates
are untreated risk, and the paper says nothing about uptake or adherence.

## Key points

Of 12,764 participants assessed for eligibility, 29% met at least one criterion.

Incidence estimated over 11,363 participants, 17,381 follow-up visits, and 30,721
person-years. Eligible 0.91 per 100 person-years versus ineligible 0.41 (p < 0.001).

Among women, eligible 1.18 versus ineligible 0.50 per 100 person-years (p < 0.001).

Among uncircumcised men, eligible 1.07 versus ineligible 0.27 per 100 person-years
(p = 0.001). Among circumcised men the difference was not significant, so male circumcision
attenuates the incidence gradient that the eligibility tool otherwise picks up.

## Limitations

Written from the abstract alone; no PDF was available on this machine, so the age and
community breakdowns, the calendar period covered, and the adjusted models behind the
prevalence ratios are not recorded here.

Eligibility rests on self-reported sexual behaviour and self-reported genital ulcers, both
subject to social-desirability and recall bias, which flattens the measured incidence
contrast if under-reporting is concentrated among the highest-risk respondents.

A roughly two-fold incidence ratio means the eligibility tool separates risk weakly: most
incident infections still arise outside a criterion-defined high-risk group when that group
is only 29% of the population.
