Source: [bbosa-2019](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-018-37458-x)

Cross-sectional molecular epidemiology study in Scientific Reports. HIV-1 partial `pol`
sequences (mean 1,257 bp) from HIV-positive adults enrolled September 2014 to September
2016 in 8 fishing communities on the Lake Victoria shore (Mpigi, Wakiso, Masaka, Kalungu
districts) and 2 adjacent general-population cohorts. Transmission pairs were identified by
Cluster Picker and HIV-TRACE at genetic-distance cutoffs of 4.5% and 1.5%, timing came from
BEAST v1.8.4 with a relaxed clock and skygrid prior, and direction of spread came from
discrete-trait phylogeography over 7 locations with Bayesian stochastic search variable
selection. The supplementary tables holding the pair-level results and the migration matrix
are not included.

## Relevance to project

Evidence on the direction of transmission between a high-prevalence occupational subgroup
and the surrounding general population, which is the kind of structure a risk-stratified
network has to encode. If a model treats fishing communities as a core group seeding the
wider epidemic, this analysis says the arrow runs the other way in this setting: very
strong support (Bayes factor above 50) for migration from the neighbouring general
population into three fishing communities, with no significant flow back out.

The practical implication for a model with a high-activity compartment is that the
compartment can carry very high prevalence and incidence while contributing little onward
transmission outside itself, sustained instead by turnover and in-migration. The authors
note that relaxing the genetic-distance threshold from 1.5% to 4.5% added few extra pairs
in the fishing communities, which they read as low residency and an unstable population.

Nothing here bears on STIs, diagnostics, or care-seeking. It gives no rates that can be
used directly as calibration targets, since the sequenced sample is neither
population-representative nor a cohort denominator.

## Key points

Subtype distribution did not differ significantly between fishing communities and general
population: A1 47.4% against 50.4%, D 33.2% against 34.8%, C 0.9% against 2.6%. The
exception was inter-subtype recombinants, 18.5% (39/211) in fishing communities against
12.2% (43/351) in the general population, p = 0.02.

At the 1.5% genetic-distance cutoff, 13 pairs were found, 10 in fishing communities and 3
in the general population. Between 1.5% and 4.5% the pattern inverts: 3 pairs in fishing
communities against 15 in the general population (Fisher's exact p = 0.001). Links in the
fishing communities are recent; the general population carries the older networks.

Time depth, the interval back to the last common ancestor of the pair's viruses, averaged
2 years over 11 pairs (range 0.3-8.4). Six fishing-community pairs averaged 0.53 years
(0.3-1.0); five general-population pairs averaged 4.1 years (1-8), unpaired t-test
p = 0.0076.

Time to most recent common ancestor by occupation, in a sample averaging 35 years old:
fishing-related 3.1 years (95% CI 0.3-5.9), farming 7.3 (4.1-10.5), bar work 6.1
(-8.9-21), sex work 10.6 (2.8-18.5). The differences are not significant (ANOVA
p = 0.1087).

Strong support (Bayes factor above 10) for viral migration between Rakai and Kampala along
the Kampala-Masaka highway, which the authors attribute to the transport corridor and its
truck-stop hotspots.

Background figures the paper cites rather than measures: fishing community HIV prevalence
around 29%, reaching 40% in some communities, against a national 7.3%; incidence up to
6 per 100 person-years at risk among high-risk individuals in fishing communities against
a national 1 per 100 person-years.

## Limitations

Only HIV-positive adults over 18 were enrolled, and sequencing was restricted to the `pol`
fragment used for drug-resistance screening, so the resolution for linkage is limited and
the sampling frame supports no incidence or prevalence estimate.

Sequence counts per site were uneven (70, 70, 71 from three fishing communities plus 44
recent seroconverters from five more; 200 and 151 from the two general-population sites),
and the phylogeographic analysis needed subsampling plus exclusion of sites with under 10
sequences. Discrete-trait phylogeography is known to be sensitive to exactly this.

General-population sequences came from people in care at health facilities adjacent to the
fishing communities and from voluntary counselling and testing clients, a clinic-based
frame rather than a population one.

The direction result rests on Bayes factors from one analysis with background sequences
excluded; the primary analysis including Los Alamos background sequences produced a
different set of supported transitions.

Fishing communities are in Mpigi, Wakiso, Masaka and Kalungu districts. Rakai enters only
as a background sequence location.
