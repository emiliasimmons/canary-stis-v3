Source: [ngure-2021](https://doi.org/10.3389/frph.2021.637869)

Serial qualitative interviews with 50 young Kenyan women (18-24) enrolled in the MPYA PrEP
trial, conducted at enrollment, 3 months, and 12 months. The study sites were Thika (HIV
prevalence 1.1%) and Kisumu (17.5%) in Kenya. All participants scored >5 on the VOICE risk
tool; 54% reported multiple current sexual partners, 50% reported condomless sex in the
prior month, and median age was 21 years.

## Relevance to project

The paper provides no quantitative targets or priors for the model. Its value is as
behavioral context for how AGYW in East Africa respond when a new prevention tool enters
their decision-making. If POC STI diagnostics change a woman's perceived infection risk
the way PrEP changed perceived HIV risk here, care-seeking and condom use could shift in
response. The finding that risk perception is dynamic and deliberately managed, not a fixed
trait, matters for any model arm that assumes stable health-seeking rates after an
intervention switch-on.

The MPYA trial's observed HIV incidence was 0.7 per 100 person-years with only 27% overall
PrEP adherence, suggesting that the counseling, testing, and risk-reduction behavior
bundled with the prevention tool contributed to protection beyond the drug itself. This
bundled-service effect is a partial analogue for what a POC diagnostic visit could deliver.

## Key points

- At enrollment, most women recognized high HIV risk from their own behaviors (multiple
  partners, transactional sex, inconsistent condom use) and from their partners' behaviors
  (refusing condoms, unknown HIV status).
- High risk perception was the primary motivator for PrEP initiation. Women described
  PrEP as lowering their risk relative to peers not on PrEP.
- After starting PrEP, more than half reduced condom use. Some in transactional
  relationships used PrEP confidence to forgo condoms and negotiate higher payments.
  Others reduced condom use after learning their partner's HIV-negative status.
- Over time, regular counseling and HIV testing led many women to reduce risky behavior
  (fewer partners, stable relationships). This reduced risk perception then led to PrEP
  discontinuation, sometimes permanent, sometimes episodic.
- Risk perception and prevention tool use followed a rational, deliberate pattern
  consistent with prevention-effective adherence: women aligned their use of PrEP and
  condoms to their current perceived risk, not to a blanket prescription.

## Limitations

- Qualitative, no generalizable effect sizes. The 50 women were purposively sampled from
  a trial population already willing to take PrEP, so their risk awareness and
  health-seeking may not represent the broader AGYW population.
- Social desirability bias from face-to-face interviews.
- Kenyan urban/peri-urban sites; transferability to Rakai or rural Uganda is uncertain.

## Methodology

Serial semi-structured interviews at three time points over 12 months, analyzed with
inductive content analysis guided by the Expanded Health Belief Model. Interviews averaged
43 minutes, conducted in Dholuo, Kiswahili, or English. Coding by four analysts with
cross-checking for agreement, managed in Dedoose.
