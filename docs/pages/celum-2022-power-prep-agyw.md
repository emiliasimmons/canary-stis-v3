Source: [celum-2022](https://doi.org/10.1002/jia2.25962)

POWER was a prospective implementation-science evaluation of oral tenofovir-based PrEP offered as a routine service to sexually active HIV-negative adolescent girls and young women (AGYW) aged 16-25 at family planning, youth and primary-care clinics in Kisumu (Kenya), Cape Town and Johannesburg (South Africa), June 2017 to September 2020, n = 2550. It measured how far women got along the PrEP cascade with no travel reimbursement and no active retention outreach, and tested for chlamydia and gonorrhoea at enrolment and 6 months.

## Relevance to project

Two uses, both quantitative.

The first is a bound on what syndromic management can reach. At enrolment 29% (667 women) tested positive for *Chlamydia trachomatis* and 10% (221) for *Neisseria gonorrhoeae*, on a tested denominator smaller than the 2550 enrolled, while only 7% of the whole cohort reported STI symptoms, and among the 770 women with either infection only 23% (179/770) reported symptoms. In a model where the POC arm swaps an etiological panel in at the same symptomatic care-seeking door, that 23% is the ceiling on what the SOC arm can even be offered treatment for in this population, before any test-performance or treatment-completion loss. It is a female, 16-25, urban clinic-attending population in Kenya and South Africa, so it does not transfer directly to the Rakai general-population cohort, but it is a comparator for the asymptomatic fraction the model assumes.

The second is a prior on PrEP coverage as actually delivered. Of 2550 enrolled, 94% (2397) initiated PrEP, 31% (749) returned for the 1-month refill, and among the 646 who could reach 6 months of follow-up, 20% (128) persisted without a gap of 15 days or more and a further 14% (92) restarted after a gap. Blood tenofovir diphosphate in a 193-sample subset put 47% at an average of 4 or more doses per week (19.0% at 7 doses/week, 28.2% at 4-6). Coverage calculations gave 20% of women with enough dispensed supply for 4 or more tablets per week over 6 months. Any PrEP arm modelled at population level should be parameterised nearer these numbers than trial-setting adherence.

STI incidence figures usable as a comparator: combined CT/NG incidence 42.9 per 100 person-years (95% CI 37.2, 49.2), CT alone 35.4 (30.3, 41.2), NG alone 13.0 (10.0, 16.7). Of incident cases, 42% of the women had also had CT and/or NG at enrolment, so reinfection dominates rather than a clean new-partner signal.

HIV incidence was 2.2 per 100 person-years (95% CI 1.2, 3.5), 16 seroconversions over 741.8 person-years. This is a PrEP-offered cohort with no control arm, so it is not a counterfactual incidence target.

## Key points

Median age 21 (IQR 19-23). All had a current partner; 66% did not know their partner's HIV status, 4% had a partner living with HIV, and 22% knew or thought their partner had other partners. Condom use was never in 27% and always in 13%.

Of the 16 seroconverters, 13 (81%) either had no PrEP dispensed in the interval before seroconversion or had drug levels indicating under 4 doses/week in the prior 6 weeks. Four seroconverted at month 1 and may have been infected at enrolment. Two had M184M/V (emtricitabine resistance), both with high tenofovir diphosphate at month 1.

Oral contraceptive use at baseline was the only baseline predictor of persistence past both 1 and 6 months (adjusted RR 1.84, 95% CI 1.11-3.05 at 6 months). The modified VOICE HIV risk score predicted neither. Persistence beyond 6 months varied 8% to 38% across sites.

Over 99% were eligible for same-day initiation on creatinine clearance, and under 1% were hepatitis B surface antigen positive, so laboratory screening was not a gate on delivery.

## Limitations

No control arm, for ethical reasons, so incidence carries no comparison group. Enrolment samples were not archived, so four month-1 seroconversions could not be reclassified as prevalent infection, biasing incidence upward. The study collected only what a PrEP delivery programme collects, so there is no information on why 69% did not return at 1 month, and no partner-age data. Adherence was a single time point per woman in a 16.7% sample of eligible visits, and that sample was not uniform across sites (11% at two sites, 33% at the third), so the pooled adherence distribution is weighted toward Johannesburg. CT and NG were tested at enrolment and 6 months only, so the incidence rate rests on two screens and cannot separate reinfection from persistent untreated infection.
