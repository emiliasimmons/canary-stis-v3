Source: [schuyler-2015](https://doi.org/10.1080/17441692.2015.1074715)

Analysis of annual household census data (1999-2011, N=112,117 observations) and travel
data (2003-2008, N=18,318) from the Rakai Community Cohort Study, examining migration and
travel patterns among youth aged 15-24. Cataloged from abstract; the full text has not
been extracted.

## Relevance to project

Quantifies youth mobility in the RCCS population, a driver of sexual-network mixing that
the model's risk structure needs to account for. One in five youth reported
out-migration, and mobile youth were more likely to report multiple partners and
inconsistent condom use. The sex differential (young women more mobile than young men)
interacts with the age-disparate partnership patterns already represented in the model.
Complements [Grabowski et al. 2020](grabowski-2020-migration-hotspots-rakai.md) on
migration and HIV risk in Rakai, with a youth-specific lens.

## Key points

- Migration and travel were more common among young women than young men in Rakai.
- Approximately one in five youth reported out-migration, with the proportion increasing
  over the 1999-2011 study period.
- Mobile youth were significantly more likely to report HIV risk behaviours: alcohol use,
  sexual experience, multiple sex partners, and inconsistent condom use.

## Limitations

Behavioural data are self-reported and subject to social-desirability bias. The abstract
does not report HIV prevalence or incidence among mobile vs non-mobile youth, so the
link between mobility and infection is indirect through behavioural correlates. Travel data
cover only 2003-2008, a subset of the census period.

## Methodology

Secondary analysis of RCCS annual household census (1999-2011) for migration trends, and
population-based cohort travel data (2003-2008) for behavioural associations. Youth
defined as aged 15-24.
