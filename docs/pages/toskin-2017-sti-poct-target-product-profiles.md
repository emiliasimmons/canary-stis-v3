Source: [toskin-2017](https://doi.org/10.1136/sextrans-2016-053071)

WHO Department of Reproductive Health and Research report combining four landscape analyses of point-of-care tests (2014, 2015, updated December 2016) with target product profiles agreed by a 32-member international expert group across two technical consultations, using an adapted Delphi method. Covers syphilis, *Neisseria gonorrhoeae*, *Chlamydia trachomatis*, *Trichomonas vaginalis* and HPV, specifying 28 analytical and operational characteristics each at a minimal and an optimal level.

## Target product profiles

Target product profile performance floors, all against laboratory NAAT as reference except syphilis (TPPA and RPR), for genital specimens:

- Gonorrhoea: sensitivity 90% minimal, 98% optimal. Specificity 90% minimal, above 98% optimal.
- Chlamydia: sensitivity above 90% minimal, 100% optimal. Specificity 98% minimal, 100% optimal.
- Trichomonas: sensitivity 85% minimal for clinician-collected samples, 98% optimal including self-collected. Specificity 99% minimal, 100% optimal.
- Syphilis, treponemal component: sensitivity above 80% minimal, above 90% optimal. Specificity above 90% minimal, above 95% optimal. Non-treponemal component: sensitivity above 95% of high-titre (1:8) specimens minimal, above 99% optimal. Specificity above 80% minimal, above 95% optimal.
- HPV: sensitivity 85% minimal, 95% optimal, with no consensus reached on specificity.

Time to result, meaning sample collection through to result in hand, is 60 minutes minimal and 30 minutes or less optimal for every infection. Target price per test excluding any device or reader is under US$5 minimal and under US$1 optimal for gonorrhoea, chlamydia and trichomonas, and under US$3 and under US$1.50 for syphilis and HPV. Training required is under 90 minutes minimal, 30 minutes optimal.

No test identified in the landscape met all the targets for its infection.

## Strength and basis

Expert consensus, not measurement. The performance floors are what an international expert group agreed a useful test would need, informed by WHO-commissioned systematic reviews, published predictive models and the detection limits of platforms then in the landscape. The landscape figures beneath them are a mix of published meta-analysis, manufacturer product inserts and telephone interviews with developers, with pipeline entries largely self-reported and many cost fields left as to-be-determined.

## Key points

- The gap for chlamydia and gonorrhoea is severe at the low-cost end. Available point-of-care tests for both are antigen immunoassays with pooled sensitivity below 50% on vaginal swabs against NAAT, against a 90% minimal target. The authors note the syndromic approach to vaginal discharge is inadequate for women, which is what leaves the gap exposed.
- The one platform meeting the performance bar is not a point-of-care test in the operational sense. GeneXpert (Cepheid) performs well against laboratory assays but is tabletop and not portable, sits at level 2 (district hospital), needs mains power, takes about 90 minutes, and costs about US$17000 for a four-module instrument plus US$16.20 per CT/NG test. That is over three times the US$5 minimal price target and half again the maximal time to result.
- Pipeline platforms aiming at level 1 (health centre) were Atlas io (30 minutes, cartridges stable 2-25 C), Alere i, Molbio/bigTec Truelab (under 60 minutes, battery powered, reagents stable 3 months to 40 C) and Ustar. Prices were not disclosed for any of them.
- Treponemal-only rapid tests cannot distinguish active from past treated infection, because antibodies persist. Meta-analysed performance of four such tests on whole blood ranged 74.3-86.3% sensitivity and 95.9-99.6% specificity. In practice, seropositive pregnant women in resource-limited settings are treated regardless.
- The dual treponemal/non-treponemal DPP Screen & Confirm (Chembio) addresses this. Treponemal-line sensitivity was 94.6-96.7% and specificity 96.4-99.6% across whole blood, finger prick and plasma in the largest field evaluation (Yin, China, n=1323). Non-treponemal-line performance was weaker and less consistent, with specificity as low as 62.2% in one laboratory evaluation against RPR.
- Trichomonas microscopy of vaginal secretions, still the traditional method, is about 50% sensitive against culture or NAAT.

## Relevance to project

The design reference for the POC arm. It supplies the diagnostic characteristics the arm has to instantiate, and each one has a minimal and an optimal value, so the arm can be run at a floor and a ceiling rather than at a single assumed sensitivity: 90/98% for gonorrhoea, above 90/100% for chlamydia, 85/98% for trichomonas, above 80/90% treponemal for syphilis. Sensitivity is the parameter the impact estimate will be most exposed to, since a missed infection in the POC arm behaves like the SOC arm.

The time-to-result targets (60 or 30 minutes) are what justifies modelling POC as test-and-treat within a single visit, with no return-visit loss to follow-up. That assumption is the mechanism separating the POC and SOC arms, and this is the source that grounds it. GeneXpert at 90 minutes and district-hospital siting does not support it, which marks the difference between a test that exists now and the test the arm assumes.

The price targets bound any later cost analysis: US$5 and US$1 per test for the bacterial STIs, excluding instrument capital.

What it cannot supply is realised performance. These are specifications, and the paper's own conclusion is that nothing on the market met them in 2016. Field sensitivity from a deployed test in the modelled population is a separate need, and the antigen-test figures here (under 50% for chlamydia and gonorrhoea on vaginal swabs) are the relevant floor if the arm is meant to represent what was actually deployable at the time.

## Limitations

- Landscape data are from 2016 and earlier. Both the product set and the prices are now stale, and the CT/NG pipeline entries in particular have since resolved one way or the other.
- Target product profile values are consensus positions from an expert group, carrying no empirical grade, and the minimal/optimal split reflects negotiated judgement rather than a measured threshold for programme impact.
- Landscape entries drawing on manufacturer inserts and developer interviews were reviewed for accuracy with those same developers.
- Sensitivity and specificity for rectal and pharyngeal swabs were explicitly not determined for the gonorrhoea and chlamydia profiles.
- No cost-effectiveness or population-impact modelling. The report does not connect test characteristics to averted infections.
