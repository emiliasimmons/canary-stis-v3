PURPOSE 1, a phase 3 double-blind randomized trial at 25 South African and 3 Ugandan sites,
comparing subcutaneous lenacapavir every 26 weeks against daily oral F/TAF, with daily oral
F/TDF as an active control and a cross-sectional recency-assay estimate of background HIV
incidence as the primary comparator. Participants were sexually active women aged 16-25 not
on PrEP. Extracted text and tables in `raw/10.1056/nejmoa2407001`.

Among 5338 randomized HIV-negative participants over 4821 person-years, 55 incident HIV
infections: 0 of 2134 on lenacapavir (0 per 100 person-years, 95% CI 0.00-0.19), 39 of 2136
on F/TAF (2.02, 95% CI 1.44-2.76), 16 of 1068 on F/TDF (1.69, 95% CI 0.96-2.74). Background
incidence in the 8094 screened women with a central HIV test was 2.41 per 100 person-years
(95% CI 1.82-3.19). Lenacapavir versus background gave an incidence rate ratio of 0.00 (95%
CI 0.00-0.04). F/TAF did not differ from background (IRR 0.84, 95% CI 0.55-1.28), and
adherence explains it: in the 10% dried-blood-spot subsample most participants on either
oral arm had low tenofovir diphosphate (under 2 tablets per week), 34 of 37 F/TAF
seroconverters had low or undetectable drug, and medium-or-high adherence carried an odds
ratio of 0.11 (95% CI 0.01-0.49) for acquisition.

## Relevance to project

Two uses, and the PrEP result is the lesser one.

The first is STI burden in a high-HIV-incidence young female population, measured by
laboratory assay rather than syndrome. At baseline, among 5338 randomized women: chlamydia
25.2% (1345), gonorrhoea 8.7% (465), trichomoniasis 7.5% (401), syphilis 2.8% (149).
Prospectively, laboratory-diagnosed CT, NG or TV at asymptomatic screening every 26 weeks
ran at 48.7 per 100 person-years in the lenacapavir arm (930 events / 1908.8 person-years),
50.8 in the F/TAF arm (965 / 1899.4), and 48.4 in the F/TDF arm (452 / 933.4). These are
combined-endpoint rates for the three pathogens together, not per-pathogen, and they come
from scheduled asymptomatic screening, so they are the closest thing here to an
etiological-screening yield in a 16-25 female population and a plausible upper bound on
what the POC arm's test-and-treat panel could detect if it screened rather than waiting at
the symptomatic door. The Ugandan sites (15-16% of participants) are the nearer comparator
for Rakai; South Africa supplies the other 84%, so these are not Rakai targets.

The second is a bound on PrEP efficacy for any prevention-targeting pathway the model
represents. Lenacapavir's 100% reduction sets the ceiling for a long-acting agent under
trial-grade delivery, and the F/TAF arm sets the realistic floor for daily oral PrEP handed
to this age group without adherence support: no measurable population effect at all.
Modelling a targeting benefit that routes women to daily oral PrEP should not assume more
than the adherence distribution in Fig. 3A supports.

The trial says nothing about STI treatment's effect on HIV acquisition. All arms treated
STIs to local guidelines, so there is no untreated comparator and no cofactor estimate.

## Limitations

The background incidence comparator is cross-sectional, derived from a limiting-antigen
avidity recency assay on screening samples, with no longitudinal follow-up. The authors
note the recency algorithm tends to underestimate prospectively observed incidence, which
would if anything make the lenacapavir comparison conservative. The result is an interim
analysis stopped early for efficacy at 50% of participants past week 52, so person-time
beyond one year is thin (43 participants reached week 104). Sites were chosen for HIV
incidence of at least 3.5 per 100 person-years among AGYW in prior trials, which selects a
high-risk sampling frame; observed background incidence came in at 2.41. STI rates carry
the same selection. Gilead designed the trial, held the data, and ran the analyses.
