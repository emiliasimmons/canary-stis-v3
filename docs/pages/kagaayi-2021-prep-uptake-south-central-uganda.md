Programme evaluation of oral PrEP (TDF/3TC) rollout through government clinics in
HIV-hyperendemic fishing villages and trading centres across Rakai, Kyotera, Masaka, and
Lyantonde districts, April 2018 to March 2019. Enrolled 2,536 HIV-negative adults at
substantial risk, drawn from sex workers (53%), fisher folk (21%), truck drivers (13%),
HIV-discordant couples (8%), and others. Raw content in
[raw/10.1002/jia2.25588/content.md](../raw/10.1002/jia2.25588/content.md).

## Relevance to project

The model's first calibration site is the RCCS in Rakai. PrEP is a competing
HIV-prevention exposure: background PrEP coverage and its effective duration set the
counterfactual against which the POC diagnostic arm is measured. This paper gives the
local uptake fraction and, more importantly, the retention curve that determines how long
coverage persists. A median retention of 45 days means most enrollees cycle off PrEP
within two months, limiting the sustained coverage the model should assume. The
population-specific dropout hazards (sex workers, fisher folk, truck drivers, adolescents)
can inform heterogeneous PrEP attrition if the model stratifies by risk group.

Not usable as a population-level PrEP coverage estimate: the denominator is programme
enrollees, not the general Rakai population, and the programme had limited geographic reach
at the time.

## Key points

Of 2,750 eligible individuals, 92.2% accepted PrEP. Fisher folk were modestly less likely
to accept than HIV-discordant couples (aPR 0.91 men, 0.85 women).

Median retention was 45.4 days for both sexes. Retention was higher among women overall
(log-rank p < 0.001), but the highest-risk female subgroups dropped out fastest: female
sex workers (aRH 1.42 vs. women in discordant couples), female fisher folk (aRH 1.99).
Among men, fisher folk (aRH 1.37) and truck drivers (aRH 1.49) discontinued faster than
men in discordant couples.

Discontinuation rates decreased with age; the only individually significant contrast was
women 30-34 vs. adolescents 15-19 (aRH 0.78, 95% CI 0.63-0.96).

Eligibility screening used a national eight-item risk tool (multiple partners, condomless
sex, transactional sex, injection drug use, repeat STI diagnosis, recent PEP, or
HIV-positive partner not on ART). Clients who screened positive on any item were offered
PrEP.

## Limitations

Retention was defined as returning for a scheduled refill; clients who stopped attending
were assumed to have discontinued, but some may have obtained PrEP elsewhere or reduced
their risk. Reasons for discontinuation were not collected. The programme operated at two
central facilities and eight outreach sites in a limited area, so the enrolled population
is not representative of all PrEP-eligible adults in the RCCS footprint. The analysis
period (one year) does not capture longer-term retention patterns.

## Methodology

Secondary analysis of programme registers. PrEP uptake modeled with multivariable modified
Poisson regression (aPR). Retention estimated by Kaplan-Meier with a 4-week visit window;
discontinuation hazards from multivariable Cox regression (aRH), stratified by sex.
