Source: [billioux-2018](https://doi.org/10.1097/qad.0000000000001761)

Cross-sectional analysis of ART uptake, viral suppression, and geospatial patterns of
facility use among 1,554 HIV-positive participants in the RCCS, February 2015 to September
2016. Cataloged from abstract; the full text has not been extracted.

## Relevance to project

Supplies ART coverage and viral suppression rates for the Rakai calibration site at a
specific time point. ART coverage (68% of HIV-positive participants) and viral suppression
among those on ART (93%) are direct inputs to the model's background HIV prevention
landscape. The product (approximately 63% population-level viral suppression among
HIV-positive individuals) bounds the fraction of HIV-positive agents who are effectively
non-transmitting due to treatment.

## Key points

- 1,554 HIV-positive RCCS participants, 2015-2016.
- 68% were on ART.
- 93% of those on ART were virally suppressed.
- Median distance to nearest ART facility was 3.10 km; median distance actually traveled
  was 5.26 km, with 57% bypassing their nearest facility.
- No association between distance traveled and viral suppression status.

## Limitations

Single time window (2015-2016); ART coverage has likely increased since then and the model
may need a more recent estimate for its calibration period. The 68% ART coverage reflects
the pre-UTT era in Rakai.

## Methodology

Population-based cross-sectional study within the RCCS. Viral load measured by laboratory
assay; ART status by self-report confirmed with clinic records. Geospatial analysis of
facility use.
