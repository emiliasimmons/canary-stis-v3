Intensive pharmacokinetic study in 17 HIV-negative adults on 30 days of daily oral
TDF/FTC followed by a 30-day washout, characterising tenofovir-diphosphate (TFV-DP) in red
blood cells against peripheral blood mononuclear cells and extending the measurement to
dried blood spots. Raw content in
[raw/10.1089/aid.2012.0089/content.md](../raw/10.1089/aid.2012.0089/content.md).

## Relevance to project

The measurement basis for the PrEP adherence categories that later trials and cohorts
report. When a PrEP effectiveness estimate is expressed by dried blood spot concentration
band rather than by pill count, the mapping from concentration to doses per week comes from
this pharmacokinetics: roughly 19 fmol per million red cells, or 230 fmol per 3-mm punch,
per dose per week at steady state. Reading such an estimate into a PrEP efficacy prior
requires that mapping, and this paper is where it originates.

Nothing here is a model parameter. Populations, adherence distributions, and HIV outcomes
are all absent; the useful content is the unit conversion and the caution that a single
spot measures a smoothed average, not a current adherence state.

## Key points

TFV-DP half-life was 17.1 days (IQR 15.7-20.2) in red blood cells against 4.2 days (IQR
3.7-5.2) in peripheral blood mononuclear cells, so steady state in red cells takes roughly
two months and a level reflects average exposure over the preceding four months.

Extrapolated steady-state TFV-DP was 130 fmol per million red cells, 1.3-fold the
peripheral blood mononuclear cell level. Red-cell and dried blood spot concentrations
related as y = 0.085x with r-squared 0.83, implying about 12 million red cells per 3-mm
punch.

Applying iPrEx's finding that four or more doses per week lowered HIV acquisition risk by
more than 95%, the corresponding steady-state exposure is above 75 fmol per million red
cells or above 900 fmol per punch.

Parent TFV and FTC in the same spot, with half-lives near 15 and 10 hours, report recent
dosing, so one card carries both a recent and a cumulative adherence signal.

Spots were stable at -20 degrees C for at least 7 months and at 4 degrees C short term, but
fell 47% below the -80 degrees C reference at room temperature after roughly two weeks,
which constrains field collection.

## Limitations

Seventeen HIV-negative volunteers, median age 30, in Colorado, with directly supervised
dosing during the pharmacokinetic visits, so the variability is smaller than in a field
adherence population.

Dose-response curves for one to seven doses per week are simulations from a
one-compartment model fitted to these participants, assuming dose proportionality and a
30% coefficient of variation, not observed dosing arms.

The 17-day half-life cannot separate sustained low adherence followed by a compensatory
high-adherence period from steady intermediate adherence at the same measured
concentration.

Spots were collected by venipuncture; fingerstick comparability was untested here.
