Source: [smith-2008](https://doi.org/10.1097/olq.0b013e31815b07dd)

Utility elicitation study in Sexually Transmitted Diseases. Women read short scenarios
describing five health states associated with pelvic inflammatory disease (ambulatory PID
treatment, hospital PID treatment, ectopic pregnancy, chronic pelvic pain, infertility) and
valued them on a visual analogue scale (VAS) and by time tradeoff (TTO). No PDF is held on
this machine; this page is written from the abstract.

## Relevance to project

A parameter source for the sequelae side of a cost-effectiveness or burden calculation, if
the project extends beyond HIV infections averted to weighted outcomes. Untreated
chlamydial and gonococcal infection in women is the pathway to PID, so a POC panel that
identifies those infections at the symptomatic door averts some fraction of these states,
and their utility weights set how much that is worth relative to an HIV infection averted.

It supplies nothing for the transmission model: no incidence, no progression rates, no
probability that an infection becomes PID.

## Key points

Valuations came from 56 women with a PID history and 150 without. On the VAS, women with a
PID history rated three states significantly lower (P < 0.05): ectopic pregnancy 0.55
against 0.63, chronic pelvic pain 0.45 against 0.53, infertility 0.53 against 0.66. The
gaps held after adjusting for demographic and childbearing characteristics.

The same differences did not appear on the time-tradeoff measure, and the two acute
treatment states (ambulatory and hospital PID treatment) were valued alike by both groups
on both instruments.

The authors' reading is that a cost-effectiveness analysis taken from the patient
perspective will value PID prevention more highly than one from a societal perspective,
because experience of the disease lowers the valuation of its sequelae.

## Limitations

Written from the abstract alone. The sampling frame, setting, age distribution and the
exact TTO values are not available here, and the population is almost certainly not
sub-Saharan African, so transporting these weights to a Rakai analysis needs an argument
the page cannot supply.

Scenario-based elicitation from brief written descriptions, valued in a fixed order
(ambulatory, hospital, ectopic pregnancy, pelvic pain, infertility), which invites ordering
effects.

Group sizes are small and unbalanced (56 against 150), and the significant differences are
on the VAS only, the weaker of the two instruments for economic evaluation.
