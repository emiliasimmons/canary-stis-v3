Three-arm RCT (STOP trial) at three antenatal clinics in Kampala, Uganda, randomising
pregnant women with a positive point-of-care treponemal test 1:1:1 to notification slips
alone (standard of care), notification slips plus SMS, or notification slips plus telephone
calls. Primary outcome was the proportion of male partners who attended the ANC and were
treated for syphilis. No extracted content on disk; worked from the abstract and bib
metadata.

## Relevance to project

The trial quantifies how poorly passive and lightly augmented partner notification performs
for syphilis in an East African urban ANC setting. Overall male partner attendance was
approximately 25%, with no statistically significant difference among the three arms. This
is consistent with the range reported in [Alam et al.](alam-2010-partner-notification-developing-countries.md)
(median 54% notified but far fewer treated) and reinforces the project's decision not to
model a partner-notification pathway: even with active outreach (SMS, phone calls), the
partner treatment cascade for syphilis in this population is too low and too insensitive to
modality to justify a dedicated arm. If a PN pathway were ever added, 0.25 partners
treated per index case is a defensible upper bound for passive notification of male
partners through ANCs.

The trial also provides context for care-seeking structure: the entry point is maternal
syphilis screening at ANC, not symptomatic presentation, so the cascade here is
screen-positive women attempting to bring in asymptomatic male partners. The low attendance
reflects the well-documented barrier of male reluctance to attend female-coded health
facilities, not a failure of the notification message to reach them.

## Key points

- Partner attendance was approximately 25% across all three arms, with no significant
  difference between notification slips alone, slips plus SMS, and slips plus phone calls.
- The trial population was pregnant women testing treponemal-positive at ANC, so it
  measures notification of asymptomatic male partners in a maternal health setting, not
  notification of symptomatic patients' partners.
- Low partner attendance was associated with worse birth outcomes, establishing a clinical
  consequence of PN failure beyond reinfection risk alone.

## Limitations

Abstract only, so the exact attendance percentages per arm, confidence intervals, and
secondary outcomes are not available. The treponemal test is lifetime-positive and does not
distinguish active from past infection, meaning some women notifying partners may not have
had active syphilis requiring partner treatment. The trial is urban Kampala, not rural
Rakai, so the care-seeking environment (facility density, transport costs, male employment
patterns) differs from the calibration site. Syphilis-specific PN dynamics may not
generalise to the other three modelled STIs, particularly gonorrhoea and chlamydia where
index cases are more often symptomatic men rather than screen-detected women.
