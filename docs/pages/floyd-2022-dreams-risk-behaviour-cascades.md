Source: [floyd-2022](https://doi.org/10.1097/QAD.0000000000003157)

Impact evaluation of the PEPFAR DREAMS Partnership, a multi-component HIV prevention package for adolescent girls and young women (AGYW), across three sites: urban informal settlements in Nairobi (n=1081, ages 15-22), rural Gem in western Kenya (n=1171, ages 13-22), and rural uMkhanyakude in KwaZulu-Natal (n=2184, ages 13-22). Closed cohorts randomly sampled from demographic surveillance frames, enrolled 2017-2018 and followed to 2019. Because DREAMS was not randomized, impact is estimated observationally: propensity-score regression adjustment comparing the counterfactuals that all versus no AGYW were invited to DREAMS by 2018. All outcomes are self-reported.

## Relevance to project

This is a comparator and an upper bound on the prevention-targeting pathway, not a calibration target. The project's second pathway asks how much HIV incidence reduction comes from using a positive STI diagnosis to route someone into prevention. DREAMS is the largest real-world test of the analogous step for HIV testing, and it is where the cascade breaks: among the most engaged DREAMS invitees in Kenya (HIV-negative, three or more core interventions, 2019), PrEP awareness was around 90%, ever-use was around 10% in Nairobi and around 20% in Gem, and current or recent use was around 1% in Nairobi and around 10% in Gem. Among sexually active invitees, condom awareness was 90-100% but participation in condom promotion in the past 12 months was around 20% in Gem and around 45% in Nairobi. If the POC arm is credited with prevention linkage after a positive test, these are the numbers that say the credit should be small: a knowledge-to-uptake conversion in the single-digit-to-20% range, not near unity.

The behavioural effect sizes give the other half of the bound. Comparing all-invited to none-invited, condomless sex in the past 12 months (all ages) differed by -11.5 percentage points (95% CI -21.8 to -0.2) in Gem, -6.2 (-16.3 to 4.4) in Nairobi, and +1.5 (-6.4 to 9.1) in uMkhanyakude. At least two lifetime partners fell only in younger AGYW in Gem (2.5% invitees vs 9% non-invitees, aOR 0.3, 95% CI 0.1-0.6). Transactional sex in the past 12 months showed no impact anywhere, sitting at 2-3% in younger and 5-10% in older AGYW. So an intensive multi-year package moved partner-count and condom behaviour by at most about ten percentage points in one of three settings. A model attributing a large behavioural response to a single POC STI diagnosis is claiming more than DREAMS delivered.

Testing uptake is the one place the intervention worked hard. In 2018, knowledge of HIV status under all-invited versus none-invited was 86 vs 56% in Nairobi (difference 29.3, 95% CI 21.9-37.0), 80 vs 68% in Gem (11.8, 6.6-17.0), and 56 vs 49% in uMkhanyakude (7.0, 2.8-11.4). Usable as context for the plausible ceiling on a community testing-access intervention, and as a caution that the differential had largely closed by 2019.

The sites are Kenya and KwaZulu-Natal, not Rakai, and no STI outcomes are measured. Nothing here transfers directly as a parameter.

## Key points

- The setting gradient is large and matters. Nairobi and Gem delivered HIV testing through DREAMS "safe spaces"; uMkhanyakude did not coordinate testing across implementing partners, and PEPFAR discontinued its DREAMS investment there at the end of 2018. Effects track that difference more than they track the intervention content.
- Coverage of the intervention was partial: 74% of AGYW followed up in 2019 had been invited in Nairobi, 57% in Gem, 53% in uMkhanyakude, and lower among older AGYW (40% in uMkhanyakude).
- In uMkhanyakude, PrEP reached AGYW only through female-sex-worker services and reached none of the cohort members who self-reported selling sex.
- The 2018 knowledge-of-status gap shrank by 2019 and vanished in uMkhanyakude, so the impact is a timing effect on testing as much as a level effect.

## Limitations

- Invitation to DREAMS was targeted on poverty, schooling, orphanhood, and pregnancy, all measured at enrolment and adjusted for. Residual confounding on unmeasured social or sexual risk remains the authors' main stated concern, and it runs in the direction of the intervention effect if implementers preferentially invited higher-risk girls.
- Exposure is self-reported invitation to DREAMS, not verified receipt of any specific component, so the estimand is intention-to-treat over a package whose contents varied by site.
- All outcomes are self-reported sexual behaviour, collected by tablet self-completion for sensitive items. Social desirability bias plausibly moves with intervention exposure, which would inflate apparent behavioural impact.
- Retention was 78-87% overall but 15-20% lower among older AGYW who had ever had sex in Nairobi and Gem, differentially removing the highest-risk stratum.
- The cascade percentages read off Figure 2 are approximate in the source's own text ("around 10%", "around 20%"), so treat them as order-of-magnitude, not point estimates.
