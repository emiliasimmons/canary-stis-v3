Systematic review and random-effects meta-analysis of empirical per-act heterosexual HIV-1 transmission probabilities, searched to September 2008, covering 43 publications on 25 distinct study populations, of which 26 studies entered the main pooled analysis. Study designs were retrospective partner, prospective serodiscordant couple, and simple prospective or cross-sectional studies where exposure was inferred from partner-pool prevalence. Meta-regression on log-transformed estimates was used to explain heterogeneity. Extracted content in [raw/10.1016/s1473-3099(09)70021-0/](../raw/10.1016/s1473-3099(09)70021-0/).

## Key points

- High-income settings, no commercial sex exposure: female-to-male 0.04% per act (95% CI 0.01-0.14), male-to-female 0.08% (0.06-0.11), combined 0.08% (0.04-0.16). Heterogeneity across high-income estimates was not significant.
- Low-income settings, excluding commercial sex exposure: female-to-male 0.380% per act (0.131-1.099), male-to-female 0.300% (0.144-0.626). Including commercial sex, female-to-male in low-income settings rises to 0.867% (0.279-2.701). Heterogeneity across low-income estimates stayed highly significant throughout.
- Direction of the sex asymmetry flips by setting. In high-income settings male-to-female is roughly twice female-to-male; in low-income settings the two are near-equal (RR 1.02, 0.53-2.00), and female-to-male in low-income settings is 3.25 times (1.09-9.74) the high-income figure.
- The final meta-regression on the 30 partner-study estimates without commercial sex kept sex, setting, their interaction, and antenatal-clinic HIV prevalence, explaining 82% of the variance. Log infectivity rose by 0.046 per one-percentage-point increase in ANC HIV prevalence (RR 1.05 per point, 1.01-1.08).
- Genital ulcer disease in the susceptible partner raised per-act infectivity 5.29-fold (1.43-19.58) against no STI, adjusted for commercial sex exposure. Non-ulcerative STI without GUD gave 1.11 (0.30-4.14), indistinguishable from no STI. GUD status alone explained 57% of variance across those 17 estimates; adding commercial sex exposure took it to 81%. Commercial sex exposure itself carried RR 11.08 (3.47-35.35).
- Stage of infection explained 95-96% of variance across 14 estimates. Against the asymptomatic phase, early infection RR 9.17 (4.47-18.81) and late infection RR 7.27 (4.45-11.88), adjusted for setting. Pooled absolute values: early 0.658% per act (0.283-1.532), asymptomatic 0.072% (0.053-0.097), late 0.553% (0.200-1.525).
- Receptive anal intercourse in heterosexual couples pooled at 1.69% per act (0.32-8.91), from two studies. Vaginal-only male-to-female from five studies was 0.076% (0.052-0.111).
- Male circumcision, two female-to-male studies. Baeten: 1.3% per act (0.5-2.0) uncircumcised against 0.5% (0.3-0.7) circumcised, a 2.6-fold difference, widening to 4.5-fold in the presence of GUD (1.8% against 0.4%). Cameron: 18.5% (2.3-34.8) against 2.2% (0.0-6.4), and with GUD 42.8% (1.26-73.0) against 6.7% (0.0-19.2), with no transmissions in either group absent GUD.
- Viral load, from two studies. Gray's combined per-act estimates were 0.23% above 38,500 serum copies/ml, 0.13-0.14% at 1,700-38,499, and 0.01% below 1,700.
- Male-to-female and combined estimates in the absence of anal intercourse, GUD, commercial sex, and outside the early or late phase converge near 0.07% per act, which the authors take as the cofactor-free asymptomatic value.

## Relevance to project

The per-act transmission prior for a sub-Saharan African setting, and the more defensible one for Rakai than high-income-country pooling: 0.38% female-to-male and 0.30% male-to-female per act in low-income settings without commercial sex exposure, against 0.04% and 0.08% in high-income settings. The confidence intervals span roughly an order of magnitude, so these are calibration bounds rather than point values.

For the coinfection pathway this is the central quantitative source. Genital ulcer disease at RR 5.29 (1.43-19.58) is the cofactor prior for syphilis in the model, and the null result for non-ulcerative STI (RR 1.11, 0.30-4.14) is the honest starting point for gonorrhoea, chlamydia and trichomoniasis: this meta-analysis found no per-act infectivity effect for them. That is a constraint on how much HIV reduction the POC arm can claim through the direct-treatment channel, given that three of the four STIs the model tracks are non-ulcerative.

The stage multipliers (early 9.17, late 7.27 against asymptomatic) and the pooled absolute values by stage give the infectiousness trajectory over untreated infection. The circumcision-by-GUD interaction, where the uncircumcised-to-circumcised ratio widens from 2.6 to 4.5 in the presence of ulcers, matters because Rakai has high male circumcision coverage and a POC arm acting on syphilis would be acting on the same interaction term.

The ANC-prevalence coefficient is a warning rather than a parameter. Per-act infectivity estimated in higher-prevalence populations is systematically larger, which the authors read as unmeasured exposure outside the index partnership. A model that fits both incidence and per-act probability in a high-prevalence setting is at risk of double-counting that.

## Limitations

- All but one included study collected data before 2001, in the pre-ART era, so nothing here reflects treatment-era infectiousness.
- Low-income-country estimates stayed severely heterogeneous after every stratification the authors tried, which they attribute to some mix of poorer study quality, genuinely more variable risk factors, and under-reported high-risk behaviour. The pooled low-income figures are therefore summaries of a spread.
- Several key sub-analyses rest on two studies (anal intercourse, circumcision, viral load). The circumcision estimates differ by more than an order of magnitude between the two.
- Per-act probability requires knowing the number of unprotected acts and that the index partner was the source. Coital counts were self-reported, condom use was controlled inconsistently (three studies not at all), and the authors judged extramarital or parenteral contamination possible in ten studies.
- The GUD analysis mostly measures GUD in the susceptible partner (susceptibility), with a single study measuring it in the index case (infectivity). Those are different quantities and were pooled.
- No data allowed separating the effect of transmission mode within the stage analysis, and HIV-2 was excluded throughout.
