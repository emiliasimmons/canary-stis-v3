Source: [chang-2010](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0010923)

A cluster-randomized trial run by the Rakai Health Sciences Program across the 15 non-
overlapping catchment areas of its mobile ART clinic program, May 2006 to July 2008.
Clusters were randomized 2:1 to receive community-based peer health workers (10 clusters,
970 patients) or usual care (5 clusters, 366 patients). Peer health workers were people
living with HIV, already on ART with good adherence for six months and literate, nominated
by fellow patients; they ran clinic counseling sessions and biweekly home visits where
they recorded symptoms, self-reported adherence, and a pill count.

Primary outcomes were pill-count adherence and cumulative risk of virologic failure
(>400 copies/mL). Neither moved: cumulative failure 17.8% (124/698) in the intervention
arm against 21.6% (56/259) in control, RR 0.81 (95% CI 0.61-1.08). Virologic failure at
individual 24-week marks from ART start was unaffected through 72 weeks and lower from 96
weeks on (96 weeks RR 0.50, 0.31-0.81; 144 weeks RR 0.39, 0.16-0.95; 192 weeks RR 0.067,
0.0065-0.71), an effect confined in stratified analysis to patients who had already
started ART before the trial. Loss to follow-up, defined as no pharmacy pickup for over 90
days, was 2.2% (21/966) versus 4.1% (15/366), RR 0.56 (0.36-0.88).

## Relevance to project

Context and a set of background levels for the Rakai HIV care cascade in the early ART
years, not a calibration target for anything the model varies. canary-stis has no
peer-health-worker or task-shifting arm, so the intervention contrast itself is out of
scope; the usable content is the control-arm denominators.

Concretely, for adults on ART in Rakai over 2006-2008 with ART eligibility at CD4 <=250 or
WHO stage IV: viral load above 400 copies/mL at a given 24-week mark ran 10-15% in the
control arm and 5-10% in the intervention arm, cumulative ever-failure was about one in
five over a median 103 weeks of follow-up, mortality was 8.5-9.3% over the trial, and loss
to follow-up was 2-4%. Median CD4 at ART start was about 160 cells/uL (IQR 77-217) and
geometric mean plasma HIV-1 RNA at start was 36,000-44,000 copies/mL. These bound how much
of the Rakai HIV-positive population was virally suppressed in the years just before the
model's likely calibration window, which matters because unsuppressed people on treatment
still transmit and because the STI cofactor effect the project is chasing acts on that
transmitting fraction.

The service-delivery description is also a design reference for the care-seeking door the
POC arm operates through. Rakai's ART program was decentralized and mobile: staff traveled
from a central facility to government clinics in each catchment area once every two weeks,
and on the other 13 days of 14 a patient's options were travel to the central facility, a
phone hotline, or a non-program provider. A test-and-treat panel placed at the symptomatic
care-seeking door in this setting is available a fraction of the time, and the mean clinic
distance to the central facility was 23.1 km in the intervention arm and 35.5 km in
control. Nothing here is STI-specific.

## Limitations

Underpowered for the per-timepoint outcomes it reports as positive: the trial was powered
on cumulative failure, observed failure was lower than the assumed 28% at 24 weeks, and
28% of participants had no virologic outcome at all. The global test across time points
was not significant (p = 0.16), and the surviving effect rests on the pre-trial-initiator
stratum, where numbers at 168 and 192 weeks fall to 39 and 27 controls. Standard of care
changed in both arms during the trial (viral-load-guided care from late 2006, enhanced
adherence counseling and second-line provider talks in 2007), so the control arm is not a
fixed baseline across the period. Open label, unblinded, unrestricted randomization by
drawing of lots with only 15 clusters, and the arms differ noticeably in mean clinic
distance. Generalisability is limited by the mobile-clinic model nested in a long-standing
research cohort, though 47% of patients were walk-ins rather than cohort referrals. Loss
to follow-up defined by pharmacy pickup will misclassify anyone transferring to another
provider, and mortality came from verbal autopsy.
