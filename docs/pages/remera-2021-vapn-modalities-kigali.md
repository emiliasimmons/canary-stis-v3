> Note: summary derived from the abstract only, not the full source.

Pilot of voluntary assisted partner notification (VAPN) in 23 health facilities in Kigali, Rwanda, October 2018 to December 2019. Index cases were persons with a new HIV diagnosis before antiretroviral therapy initiation, or persons already on ART, who reported sexual partners of unknown HIV status. Three referral modalities were offered: contract referral (the index client agrees to notify partners within a set period, after which the provider follows up), provider referral (a health worker notifies partners directly), and dual referral (client and health worker together). A Bayesian hierarchical logistic regression, adjusted for factors chosen by Bayesian variable selection, estimated the association between modality and the odds of identifying an HIV-positive partner.

## Relevance to project

As with other partner-notification entries, this bounds a pathway the model omits. The finding that matters for any future PN arm is that the three modalities were comparable: there was no association between referral modality and the odds of identifying an HIV-positive partner. Modality choice therefore does not, on this evidence, set the cascade yield, which removes one degree of freedom a PN parameterisation might otherwise carry.

What did predict partner positivity was the index case. Partners of male index cases (adjusted odds ratio 1.84, 95% credible interval 1.50 to 2.28) and of newly diagnosed index cases (aOR 1.82, 95% CrI 1.45 to 2.30) were more likely to be HIV-positive. The overall yield, 7.1% positivity among tested partners, was higher than the Rwandan national average, which is the case-finding efficiency argument for index testing.

The Kigali setting (urban, relatively well-resourced, high ART coverage) differs from rural Rakai, so the numbers are context references rather than transferable point estimates.

## Key points

- 6,336 index cases were recruited, leading to the testing of 7,690 partners.
- HIV positivity among tested partners was 7.1% (546/7,690).
- No association was found between referral modality and the odds of identifying an HIV-positive partner; all three modalities were comparable.
- Partners of male index cases (aOR 1.84, 95% CrI 1.50 to 2.28) and of newly diagnosed index cases (aOR 1.82, 95% CrI 1.45 to 2.30) were more likely to be HIV-positive.
- Yield exceeded the national average, and the authors recommend scale-up toward the first UNAIDS 95 target.

## Limitations

Abstract only: the cascade denominators, loss to follow-up at each step, and the variable-selection detail were not read. Modality was not randomised, so any apparent modality difference (and the absence of one) may partly reflect who chose which modality. Kigali is urban with high facility density, making provider and dual referral more feasible than in dispersed rural settings.
