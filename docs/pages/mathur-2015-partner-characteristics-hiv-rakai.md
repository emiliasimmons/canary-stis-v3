Source: [mathur-2015](https://doi.org/10.1097/qai.0000000000000539)

Analysis of self-reported partner characteristics and HIV acquisition among young men
(n = 1969) and women (n = 2826) in the Rakai Community Cohort Study, 2005-2011. The study
controls for individual-level risk factors to isolate the contribution of partner
attributes (age, circumcision status, HIV status, number of partners) to seroconversion
risk in a population-based cohort aged 15-24.

## Relevance to project

The model's sexual network assigns partnerships with age-mixing and risk-stratification
rules that determine who is connected to whom. This study quantifies which partner
characteristics predict HIV acquisition among AGYW and young men in Rakai, the first
calibration site. It can inform:

- **Age-mixing structure**: the magnitude of age-disparity effects on HIV risk, bounding
  how much mixing-pattern fidelity matters for incidence.
- **Partner-attribute risk weights**: how circumcision status, partner HIV status, and
  concurrency of partners associate with seroconversion, after controlling for
  individual behavior.
- **Calibration comparator**: age- and sex-specific seroconversion rates among youth in
  the RCCS cohort over a period overlapping the model's calibration window.

The study is observational and self-reported, so partner HIV status and sexual behavior
measures carry reporting bias. It does not measure STI coinfection or care-seeking, so the
partner-risk effects it estimates are not adjusted for STI cofactors.

## Key points

- Examines both male and female youth in the same cohort with the same design, allowing
  sex-stratified comparisons of which partner characteristics matter.
- Controls for individual-level confounders (own number of partners, alcohol use,
  circumcision) to isolate partner-level effects.
- Draws from the same RCCS sampling frame the model calibrates against.

## Limitations

- Self-reported partner data, particularly partner HIV status, is subject to
  misclassification.
- Covers 2005-2011, a period before large-scale ART and voluntary medical male
  circumcision (VMMC) rollout shifted the HIV landscape in Rakai.
- No STI coinfection data, so the partner-risk estimates may partially reflect unmeasured
  STI transmission pathways.

## Methodology

Population-based cohort study using the RCCS survey rounds. Exposure is self-reported
partner characteristics; outcome is HIV seroconversion between consecutive survey visits.
Multivariate regression adjusting for individual-level risk factors.
