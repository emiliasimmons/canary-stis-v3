Paired-specimen comparison nested in a population-based cohort in rural Rakai District,
2002-2003, in which women provided a self-administered vaginal swab and were separately
offered a pelvic examination with a physician-collected cervical swab, both tested for
carcinogenic HPV.

## Relevance to project

A specimen-acceptance bound for the POC arm in the calibration setting. Uptake of the
diagnostic step is a coverage parameter distinct from care-seeking and from test
sensitivity, and this gives the two ends of the range in Rakai women: at least 86%
provided a self-collected vaginal swab against 51% accepting a pelvic examination. A POC
panel that requires a clinician-collected specimen loses roughly a third of women who
would have tested with a self-collected one, which is a plausible size for a
specimen-collection coverage penalty on the intervention arm.

Detection agreed between specimen types (92% raw agreement, kappa 0.75, similar in
HIV-positive and HIV-negative strata), which supports treating a self-collected vaginal
specimen as informationally equivalent to a cervical one for a molecular assay.

The pathogen is HPV, outside the four curable STIs the model tracks, so the agreement
statistics do not transfer as sensitivity parameters for gonorrhoea, chlamydia,
trichomoniasis, or syphilis. The acceptance figures are the transferable part.

## Key points

Carcinogenic HPV prevalence was 19% in self-collected and 19% in physician-collected
samples, measured by Hybrid Capture 2, with genotyping by PCR.

Agreement among paired observations was 92%, kappa 0.75. Kappa was 0.71 in HIV-positive and
0.75 in HIV-negative women, so concordance did not degrade with HIV infection.

Compliance with self-collection was at least 86%; 51% accepted the pelvic examination.

## Limitations

Written from the abstract alone; no PDF was available on this machine, so the age range,
sample size, and reasons for declining the pelvic examination are not recorded here.

Only about half of participants have paired specimens, and those who accepted the pelvic
examination may differ from those who did not, so the agreement estimate is conditional on
acceptance.

Collected in 2002-2003, before the RCCS communities went through combination HIV
intervention scale-up, so the acceptance figures may understate current willingness to be
examined.
