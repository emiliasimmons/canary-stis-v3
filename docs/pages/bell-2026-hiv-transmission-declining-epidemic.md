Source: [bell-2026](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.04.29.26350859)

Matched case-control study nested within RCCS rounds 20-21 (2021-2025), with contact tracing, venue-based FSW enrollment, and phylogenetic analysis. 187 incident HIV cases identified from 38,899 HIV tests among 22,255 people; 164 enrolled and matched to 164 HIV-negative controls. Preprint, not yet peer-reviewed.

## Key points

- Partnership with a female sex worker was the strongest predictor of incident HIV in men (OR 15.5, 95% CI 3.7-64.8). 43% of male cases reported at least one FSW partner vs 6.3% of controls.
- Men with FSW partners had larger sexual networks (median 6 vs 2 partners) and 91% also had non-sex-worker partners, creating bridging potential.
- Transmission modeling attributed 34.4% (95% CI 31.5-36.8%) of all male infections and 80% (73.2-85.4%) of infections among male clients to sex with FSWs. Across sensitivity analyses, 13-18% of all infections attributed to FSW partnerships.
- Despite concentration in FSW networks, transmission was locally fragmented: cases rarely shared partners or venues, and phylogenetic analysis found few large clusters. This is consistent with patterns across eastern and southern Africa.
- Oral PrEP use among HIV-negative partners of incident cases was low (8.9% in women, 2.1% in men). Viral suppression among partners of cases was lower than among partners of controls (74% vs 97% in female partners).
- Marital dissolution was a strong correlate (OR 3.1, 95% CI 1.7-5.6) and 76% of venue-enrolled FSWs were previously married.
- Most reported FSW venues were small, informal (bars/guesthouses), and not on existing key population program venue lists.

## Relevance to project

Characterizes the sexual network structure driving ongoing HIV transmission in the model's calibration site during the period of declining incidence. The finding that male client networks are large, bridge between FSW and general-population partners, and have low prevention coverage is relevant to the network identification pathway (pathway triad): STI diagnosis at a POC visit could identify these high-transmission-potential networks.

The fragmented transmission structure (no large phylogenetic clusters, venues not on program lists) suggests that POC diagnostics reaching these networks through clinical encounters may be more effective than geographic or key-population targeting alone.

## Methodology

Conditional logistic regression for case-control comparisons, matched on sex, age, community type, and religion. Bayesian additive cumulative hazard model for transmission attribution, with four imputation strategies for untraceable partners. Phylogenetic linkage via deep sequencing (veSEQ-HIV, Illumina) with region-specific TN93 distance thresholds.

## Limitations

- Southern Uganda only, though epidemiological trends are consistent with many late-stage African epidemics.
- FSW partners were frequently untraceable (only 14.4% enrolled); transmission attribution relies on imputation.
- Viral suppression at time of sampling meant many partnerships could not be evaluated for genetic linkage.
- Sex work client status not collected for male partners of female cases.
- Preprint, not peer-reviewed.
